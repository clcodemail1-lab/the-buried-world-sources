THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 18 - 125,000 Years Ago, Someone Knew the Poison Tree

SOURCES AND LIMITS

One publication carries this film, and it was read in full together with its
supplementary materials: a study in Science, published in the issue of 12
November 2026, of a stone-tool site on the shore of a former lake in southern
Saudi Arabia. A second study, published in January 2026, supplies the record
the film compares it with; for the two facts taken from it (its age and its
source plant), its abstract was read. The photographs of residues come from
the authors' own data set. The companion file dispatch-18-method.txt holds the
complete fact-check table.

THE PHOTOGRAPHS

Six micrographs and residue maps from the authors' data set (3), by Chris
Clarkson, licensed CC BY 4.0. They are shown framed on the channel's dark
ground, enlarged, with the status bar of the microscope software cut away;
the images themselves are unchanged.
  - MDF87 230-2: bast fibre and a wood fragment on a Levallois point
  - MDF87 264-2: where the residues sit on the tool (residue map)
  - MDF87 303: palm leaf tissue (scale bar 100 micrometres)
  - MDF87 504-1: plant fibres on a toothed tool
  - MDF87 230-2: residue along the working edge
  - MDF87 Lev Pt: resin near the base of a Levallois point

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW THIS FILM WAS MADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The narration is synthetic speech. Apart from the six credited photographs,
every still is generated; none of the generated images reproduces a real
find, a real site photograph, a figure from the paper or a real person. The
generated stone point shows the TYPE of tool described, not the published
point. No figure of the paper is reproduced. The diagrams are drawn in code.

The opening seconds are built to work without sound - a stone point first,
then a caption naming the place and the period - and on-screen text is set
large for viewers watching on a television.

The film carries the EU marking for AI-generated content.
