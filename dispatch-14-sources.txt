THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 14 - These Four Graves Were Closed. Somebody Came Back Anyway

SOURCES AND LIMITS

Every claim in this film comes from one of the four publications below. All
four were read in full, not from abstracts or press releases - three are open
access, the fourth was read through institutional access; the companion file dispatch-14-method.txt sets out what was dropped
and why. Where a paper hedges, this film hedges with it, in the paper's own
words.


THE FOUR PUBLICATIONS

1. Pomeroy, E. et al. (2026). Evidence for funerary behaviour and lethal trauma
  in a new Neanderthal individual from Shanidar Cave. Scientific Reports 16(1),
  article 30042. Published online 15 October 2026. CC BY 4.0.
  doi:10.1038/s41598-026-69193-z
  -- Source of Shanidar A (an older adult, about forty-five by the wear on the
  anterior teeth, deposited around 75,000 years ago), of the minimum number of
  five individuals in the cluster behind the fallen boulder, of the exclusion of
  the single-event reading, of the sequence of depositions made in most cases
  before the previous body had fully decomposed, of the healed facial injury and
  the perimortem scapula perforation consistent with a wooden spear, and of the
  authors' statement that a hunting accident and interpersonal violence cannot
  be told apart.

2. Carballo-Perez, J. and Molinero Polo, M. A. (2026). From individualized
  mummies to collective accumulations: an archaeothanatological analysis of
  funerary reuse in Theban Tomb 209 (Twenty-Fifth Dynasty-Ptolemaic Egypt).
  Frontiers in Environmental Archaeology 5, article 1888379. Published online
  18 August 2026. CC BY 4.0.
  doi:10.3389/fearc.2026.1888379
  -- Source of Theban Tomb 209, of the sixteen mummified
  individuals in primary position together with one mummified canid, of the
  disturbed thermally altered deposit holding at least four more whose
  provenance remains uncertain, of the use of the chamber from the twenty-fifth
  dynasty through the Persian period into Ptolemaic times, of the shift from
  individually coffined bodies to direct stacking, of the authors' reading that
  this is not a loss of funerary order but a change in depositional logic, and
  of their caution that the present heights of the bodies are not the heights at
  which they were placed.

3. Rodwell, W. (2026). Westminster Abbey's Roman Sarcophagus and its
  Afterlife. Journal of the British Archaeological Association, published
  online 20 October 2026, pp. 1-35.
  doi:10.1080/00681288.2026.2675788
  -- Source of the fourth-century limestone coffin cut for Valerius Amandinus,
  of its reuse around the turn of the eleventh century for a man laid in a
  wooden coffin and covered with lime, of the recut lid carrying a tenth- or
  eleventh-century cross, of the argument - stated by the author as an argument
  - that the occupant is Abbot Aelfwig (abbot 993 to about 1020), of the
  exhumation and reinterment in the new sacristy courtyard about 1258, of the
  lid's later use as a floor slab, and of the coffin being struck by workmen in
  1820, broken into five pieces and forgotten until its excavation in 1869.

4. Vukovic, J., Bernhardsson, C., Edlund, H., Delbarre, G., Smith, M.,
  Darvill, T. et al. (2026). Diachronic reuse of Neolithic burial monuments by
  Bronze Age newcomers in Southwestern Britain. Scientific Reports 16(1),
  article 26819. Published online 26 August 2026. CC BY 4.0.
  doi:10.1038/s41598-026-66094-z
  -- Source of the thirty genomes from twelve burial sites dated between about
  3800 and 1400 BCE, of the ancestry turnover between about 3000 and 2500 BCE,
  of the four first-degree relatives at Monkton-up-Wimborne (3364-3093 BCE) and
  the grave's careful backfilling with compacted chalk rubble, of the unrelated
  man of Beaker-associated ancestry placed centrally in that fill more than
  fifteen hundred years later (1519-1321 BCE) under a small flint cairn, of the
  three men in a cist at Sisters Long Barrow (3888-3527 BCE) and the young woman
  in a scoop cut into the top of the mound two millennia later (1534-1436 BCE),
  of the five further British Isles sites with individuals directly radiocarbon
  dated to both periods, and of the authors' own cautions about sampling bias
  and about how far their interpretation can be pressed.


WHAT THE FILM DOES NOT CLAIM

- It does not claim that anybody was murdered at Shanidar. Headlines about the
  world's first murder are not a reading of that paper, which states plainly
  that a hunting accident and interpersonal violence cannot be told apart.
- It does not revive the flower burial. The paper treats that interpretation as
  having failed further scrutiny, and the film mentions it only to drop it.
- It does not treat the Theban stack as a measurement of height. The paper says
  the bodies have settled, and the film says so with it.
- It does not say that Abbot Aelfwig is proved. The paper writes "it is argued",
  and the film keeps that wording.
- It does not say how widespread the reuse of older graves was. Five further
  sites is what the comparative data give, and the paper calls the extent
  unclear.
- It does not claim that the people who reopened the Dorset grave could still
  see it. That the feature may have survived as a damp hollow is a possibility
  in the paper, not a finding.
- It shows no human remains anywhere. Every find at the level of the body is
  drawn as an excavation plan or section instead.

HOW THIS FILM WAS MADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The narration is synthetic speech. Every still is generated; none of them
reproduces a real find, a real site photograph or a real person. The diagrams
are drawn in code from the published descriptions, which is why the labelling
in them can be trusted and the generated images carry none.

No human remains appear anywhere in this film. That is a deliberate rule, not
an accident of the material: in all four cases the finding is at the level of
the body, and in all four it is shown as an excavation plan or a section -
the form archaeology itself works in - rather than as a picture of the dead.

Every figure that appears on screen is listed in the section above or comes
from one of the four publications. Where a number could not be checked, it is
not shown.

The film carries the EU marking for AI-generated content.
