THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 14 - Who Made The Terracotta Army? Look At Their Fingerprints

SOURCES AND LIMITS

One publication carries this film. Its full text is open access but could not
be retrieved from here, so every figure from it comes from the authors' own
abstract; five further details come from news reports that quote the full
text, and are marked as such. The background on how the soldiers were made
comes from a second paper that was read in full. The companion file
dispatch-14-method.txt holds the complete fact-check table.


THE PUBLICATION

1. Li, X., Shen, J., Lan, D., Wang, J., Yu, Y., Cai, J., Xia, Y. and Zhou, P.
  (2026). Who produced Emperor Qin Shihuang's Terracotta Army, China? A
  Bayesian mixture model approach to fingerprint age and sex determination.
  Journal of Cultural Heritage 82, 274-281. Available October 2026 (Crossref
  record created 13 October 2026; issue dated November 2026). Open access,
  CC BY-NC-ND.
  doi:10.1016/j.culher.2026.09.014
  -- From the abstract: the 79 well-preserved fingerprints from Unit T23 of
  Pit 1; the mean ridge breadth measurements and the Bayesian mixture model;
  the criticism that earlier linear-regression approaches treat ridge breadth
  and ridge density as independent; the estimate of 45.0 % adult males,
  32.7 % adult females and 22.3 % juveniles under 15; the under-15 threshold
  taken from Qin legal texts on age-based legal responsibility; the
  "diverse workforce including women and children"; and the authors' own
  cautions - that the proportions are fingerprint frequencies, not counts of
  people, and that behavioural biases and a modern European reference dataset
  may overestimate women and juveniles.
  -- From reports quoting the full text (ScienceAlert; Ancient Origins and
  Asianet citing phys.org): the 36 figures; the location of the prints, mainly
  on heads and arms, also on armour, hands, collars and one foot; the 91 prints
  first recorded and 12 set aside as probable repeats [ScienceAlert only]; 73
  prints from shaping and assembly and 6 on separately made ornaments pressed
  onto the armour [ScienceAlert only]; corrections for clay shrinkage and
  population differences; Qin bamboo texts on apprenticeship (one year for an
  apprentice, two for a new artisan [ScienceAlert only]); Qin lacquerware
  inscriptions naming adult women, young girls and young boys; and the
  authors' suggestion that younger apprentices may have done simpler tasks
  such as attaching armour details [ScienceAlert only].


BACKGROUND, READ IN FULL

2. Quinn, P. S., Zhang, S., Xia, Y. and Li, X. (2017). Building the
  Terracotta Army: ceramic craft technology and organisation of production at
  Qin Shihuang's mausoleum complex. Antiquity 91(358), 966-979.
  doi:10.15184/aqy.2017.126
  -- Source of: around 7,000 life-size figures (warriors, generals and horses)
  in three pits; work beginning when the 13-year-old came to the throne and
  ending with his death less than forty years later; a workforce that may have
  run to 700,000 craftsmen and labourers, some of them slaves; figures 1.8-2 m
  tall with clay walls up to 0.1 m thick, weighing 150-200 kg; local loess
  clay tempered with sand from Mount Lishan; a standardised paste probably
  prepared centrally and handed to the workshops; about 1,050 tonnes of clay
  and temper; component parts from moulds assembled in many combinations;
  firing with no sustained temperature above about 850 C; stamps and
  inscriptions read as administrative or quality-control marks; kiln remains
  reported about 200 m from Pit 1 and never investigated in detail.

WHAT THE FILM DOES NOT CLAIM

- It does not claim that any single print was left by a child or a woman. The
  method estimates the mixture of the whole set and cannot assign one print.
- It does not claim that the army was mostly made by women and children. Adult
  men are the largest single group of prints in the estimate, and the authors
  say their reference data may push the share of women and juveniles up.
- It does not claim that the proportions are proportions of workers. They are
  proportions of prints.
- It does not claim the result holds for the whole army. All 79 prints come
  from one excavation unit in one pit.
- It shows no real object. Every photograph-like shot is generated.

HOW THIS FILM WAS MADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The narration is synthetic speech. Every still is generated; none of them
reproduces a real find, a real site photograph or a real person. Where a stamp
or a written slip is the subject, it is shown worn beyond reading - no
generated image carries invented legible writing. The diagrams are drawn in
code; every curve in them is schematic and says so, because the 79 published
measurements were not available to us.

The opening seconds are built to work without sound - the find first, then a
caption naming the place and the period - and on-screen text is set larger
than in earlier dispatches, for viewers watching on a television.

The film carries the EU marking for AI-generated content.
