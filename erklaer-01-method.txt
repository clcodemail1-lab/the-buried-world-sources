THE BURIED WORLD — EXPLAINER 01, METHOD
Roman Concrete Heals Itself. Nobody Meant It To.


WHY THIS FILM EXISTS

The channel's films run twelve to eighteen minutes and assume you will sit
still for them. This is a test of a second, lighter way in: the same material,
shorter, in dialogue, with drawn figures doing the explaining.

It is a test and is published as one. If it does not bring viewers who were
not already here, it will not become a series.


HOW THE CONDENSATION WAS MADE

The long film has 63 blocks and 12,703 characters of speech. This has
160 lines and 7479 characters — a little under half. Every line was written
against a block and carries that block's number, in the film's source list and
in the script file itself. Nothing was written from memory of the long film.

Four things were cut. None of them is a claim; all of them are detail that
does not survive the shorter form:

  - the composition in detail, and the term opus caementicium. The channel's
    rule is that a hook must work without a dictionary.
  - the ROMACONS cores by site. A list of place names needs a map, and this
    film has no map.
  - the Smeaton/Aspdin history, compressed from four blocks to two. Names and
    dates do not carry here without pictures of what they made.
  - nothing else.

Blocks of the long film not cited anywhere in this one, each with its reason:

  [10]  where the ash was quarried, and that it was shipped — detail
  [16]  what makes the ash reactive: unstable silica and alumina — detail
  [20]  the Pantheon dome by its span and date — detail; the shorter film
        keeps only that aggregate was chosen by height in a dome [19]
  [21]  transition ("so the recipe is not a secret")
  [22]  restates the lumps, already carried by [05]
  [38]  the ROMACONS sites by name — needs a map
  [39]  core dimensions — detail
  [46]  transition into the popular story

🚨 A ninth block was on this list when it was first written, and should not
have been. [61] says the written evidence is ambiguous exactly where it would
matter: Vitruvius uses one word for the lime that goes into plasterwork and
another for the lime that goes into structures, and whether the second means
what hot mixing needs it to mean is a question about Latin. That is not a
transition — it is the reason the intent question cannot be settled, and the
shorter film had dropped it. It was caught while listing these gaps, and four
lines were added to the film and re-recorded. The list above is what remains.

This is the failure mode a simplified version has: not a false claim, but a
missing limit. It is the reason this file exists.


WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY KEPT

Every place where the long film limits itself is also in this one:

  [36]  self-repair does not mean indestructible
  [45]  what increases is resistance to cracking — nobody has shown that a
        Roman pier gets stronger in the sense an engineer means
  [49]  by the property engineers design to, Roman mortar is not close to
        modern concrete and would not be allowed in a modern frame
  [54]  survivorship: the structures that fell down are not there to be
        measured, so to some degree we are admiring a museum of successes
  [07]  whether anyone intended a self-repairing material is an open argument

A simplified film that is more confident than the one it simplifies would be
worse than no film at all. That is the whole reason the block numbers are
published.


THE SECOND VOICE

B is not there to feed lines. Every objection B raises is one the long film
raises and answers itself — that the lumps are mistakes in the mix [23], that
water entering a crack should be the end of it [32], that the finding is only
a model [33], that the recipe was lost [47], that we cannot reproduce it [48],
that Roman concrete is simply better [49], that steel is simply better [52],
that the failures are missing from the sample [54].

And B is right once, at the end. A says the Romans built a material that heals
itself; B says they may have stumbled into one. Block [07] leaves exactly that
open, so A concedes. If the format ever has B lose every exchange, it has
stopped being honest.


WHAT WE GOT WRONG WHILE MAKING THIS

  - The voice cache was keyed by line number. Cutting one line would have
    shifted every recording after it by one, and the film would have spoken
    the wrong sentences from that point on without raising a single error. It
    is now keyed by the text itself.
  - Three places in the code referred to lines by number. All four numbers
    were stale on the first run and would have animated the schematic against
    the wrong sentences. Each number now carries a sample of its line and the
    build stops if they disagree.
  - A third schematic was built, for the mineral plates growing in the pores,
    and then deleted. The background there is the electron micrograph of those
    plates; the drawing stood beside it and showed the same thing worse.
  - The subtitle timing let one line end after the next began.

None of these changed a claim. They are listed because the channel publishes
its workings, and because every one of them would have been invisible in the
finished film.


LABELLING
