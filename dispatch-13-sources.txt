THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 13 - The Sickle Was Under Her Chin
Full source list

Four graves, four centuries, two countries. The lead item is a study published
in August 2026; the other three are older and are here because they show what
the first one is part of.

Every DOI below was checked against the Crossref record - title, journal, year,
type and first author, not merely clicked. Where something rests on a
university announcement rather than a peer-reviewed paper, this list says so.

ONE THING THIS FILM DOES NOT SHOW: human remains. Not a bone, not a skull, not
a tooth, not a burial with a body in it - in any shot. The graves are drawn as
excavation plans instead. Three reasons, and the third is the one that matters
most: the people in this film are real, named, published individuals; a
generated "skeleton" would be an invention about a particular human being; and
the closing section of this very film criticises a facial reconstruction for
having been built from a scan of somebody else. A film that raises that
objection cannot then show an invented face or skeleton of these women.

ITEM 1 - GRAVE 75 AT PIEN (the lead item)

  Polinski, D. et al. (2026). A unique, non-normative burial of a young adult
  female from a 17th-century cemetery in Pien, north-central Poland. A
  multidisciplinary approach.
  Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports, 2026.
  doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2026.105967

  WHAT WAS FOUND: a woman of roughly 17-19, buried around the middle of the
  seventeenth century, with a curved iron sickle laid across the neck, blade
  turned inward, and a triangular iron padlock closed around the big toe of the
  left foot. Excavated 2022 by the Institute of Archaeology, Nicolaus Copernicus
  University; the analysis appeared in August 2026.

  WHAT THE ANALYSIS SHOWS: metal threads near the skull, of pure silver and
  gilded wire, from a silk headdress - an expensive burial, not an outcast's.
  Genetic ancestry pointing to southern Scandinavia, while the strontium in the
  enamel does not support that she herself migrated. Carbon and nitrogen values
  placing her with the other adults of the cemetery. A haemangioma in the
  sternal body which the researchers explicitly do not consider the cause of
  death.

  WHAT IT LEAVES OPEN, and the film says all three: traces of copper, manganese
  and gold on the palate, from an object placed in the mouth or nose that has
  not been identified; the cause of death, with testing continuing; and why this
  woman in particular, where the team offers illness, hunger or misfortune as a
  suggestion and presents it as one.

  ONE CLAIM WE LEFT OUT: this is widely reported as the first documented Polish
  grave combining sickle AND padlock. That is probably right, but a superlative
  needs checking that the narrative does not need, so it is not in the film.

  A CORRECTION MADE BEFORE BROADCAST: an earlier script draft said the copper
  staining had ruled out coins "on the size of the stain". No accessible source
  supports that, so it was cut rather than softened. What is in the film is what
  the study reports: copper, manganese and gold, and no identification.


ITEM 2 - THE FIVE AT DRAWSKO

  Gregoricka, L. A., Betsinger, T. K., Scott, A. B. and Polcyn, M. (2014).
  Apotropaic Practices and the Undead: A Biogeochemical Assessment of Deviant
  Burials in Post-Medieval Poland.
  PLoS ONE 9(11), e113564.
  doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0113564

  Polcyn, M. and Gajda, E. (2015). Buried with sickles: early modern interments
  from Drawsko, Poland.
  Antiquity 89(348), 1373-1387.
  doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2015.129

  🚨 A CORRECTION MADE BEFORE BROADCAST, and it changed the meaning: an earlier
  script draft said five burials had been excavated at Drawsko, four of them
  with sickles across the throat. Both papers say FIVE individuals with a
  sickle, and Polcyn & Gajda give the population they come from - a cemetery of
  more than 250 burials, in use across the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries.
  The old wording read as though almost every excavated grave had one. It
  overstated how common the practice was by orders of magnitude. The film now
  gives the denominator, because five out of more than 250 is the actual finding
  and it is the more interesting one.

  ON THE SICKLES: they lay across the throat OR the abdomen - the second
  placement understood to open the gut rather than take the head. Which lay
  where, individual by individual, is not something the accessible reports set
  out, so the film and the graphic both give it as a collective statement.

  ON THE PEOPLE: Gregoricka records six deviant burials, five of them with a
  sickle - one adult male, three adult females (one 45-49, one 30-39) and one
  late adolescent female. Where an age is not published, our graphic says
  "adult" rather than inventing one.


ITEM 3 - THE CHILD AT LUGNANO IN TEVERINA

  Soren, D. (2015). The Children's Cemetery of Lugnano in Teverina, Umbria.
  In: The Archaeology of Childhood. State University of New York Press.
  doi.org/10.1515/9781438458069-017

  🚨 WHAT RESTS ON WHAT, because the two are not equally solid. The cemetery,
  its fifth-century date and the malaria epidemic behind it are published and
  long-established: haemozoin, a residue malaria leaves, was identified in bone
  from the site. The particular burial in this film - the child of about ten
  with a stone placed in the mouth, the teeth having left marks in it - was
  announced by the University of Arizona in October 2018 and is documented
  through that announcement and the excavation reporting, NOT through a
  dedicated peer-reviewed paper.

  We ran it anyway because the case is eight years old, consistently reported,
  and the film claims nothing beyond it. Note in particular what the film does
  NOT say: it does not claim malaria was demonstrated for this child. The
  reporting notes an abscessed tooth consistent with the disease; the
  haemozoin evidence is from the cemetery, not from this individual. The
  sentence in the film is "the bones from that cemetery carry haemozoin", and
  that is deliberate.


ITEM 4 - THE WOMAN AT LAZZARETTO NUOVO, VENICE (the closing case)

  Nuzzolese, E. and Borrini, M. (2010). Forensic Approach to an Archaeological
  Casework of "Vampire" Skeletal Remains in Venice: Odontological and
  Anthropological Prospectus.
  Journal of Forensic Sciences 55(6).
  doi.org/10.1111/j.1556-4029.2010.01525.x

  Minozzi, S. et al. (2012). Commentary on: Nuzzolese E, Borrini M. Forensic
  approach to an archaeological casework of "vampire" skeletal remains in
  Venice.
  Journal of Forensic Sciences 57(3).
  doi.org/10.1111/j.1556-4029.2012.02100.x

  WHY THIS CASE CLOSES THE FILM INSTEAD OF SUPPORTING IT: it is the most famous
  "vampire burial" in the world and the weakest of the four. The 2010 reading -
  a brick placed in the mouth to stop a shroud-eater - has been contested on
  three separate grounds. First the ground itself: the remains lay among stones,
  bricks and roof tiles, jaws fall open as a body decays, and things fall in; on
  a neighbouring Venetian cemetery a skeleton was found with a thighbone in its
  mouth, which nobody reads as ritual. Second the folklore, which is documented
  for parts of the German-speaking world rather than for Italy. Third the
  procedure, and here the film is if anything too cautious: the objection is not
  a remark to the press but a formal Commentary published in the same journal
  (the second DOI above).

  AND THE FACE IS DISPUTED TOO: in 2023 a new facial reconstruction of the same
  woman was produced by anatomical deformation - taking the scan of a different
  person's skull and adjusting it to hers. The author of the original
  reconstruction rejects it, and a paper about the dispute followed in 2024.
  This is why the film shows the method as two abstract grids and not as a face.


ON THE PICTURES

  Every photographic-looking shot in this film is generated - there is no
  photograph of any real find in it, and no real, identifiable object is
  depicted. What is shown is an example of the TYPE: an iron sickle, a
  triangular padlock, a limestone pebble. The prompts are in the production
  repository.

  The drawn graphics carry no AI label because they are not generated: they are
  drawn in code, which is also why the labelling in them is correct.
