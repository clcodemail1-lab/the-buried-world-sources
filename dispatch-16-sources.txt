THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 16 - This Ethiopian Palace Could Have Stood 16 Floors High

SOURCES AND LIMITS

One publication carries this film, and it was read in full: an open-access
structural study of the walls of the palace Grat Be'al Gibri at Yeha, in
northern Ethiopia. Every number in the film is in it. Three reports about the
paper were read for the way its central number travelled, and the film says
where they differ from the paper. The companion file dispatch-16-method.txt
holds the complete fact-check table.


THE PUBLICATION

1. Drieschner, M. and Schnelle, M. (2026). Numerical Investigations of
  Timber-Reinforced Wall Constructions with Uncertain Material Parameters for
  the Ancient Palace Grat Be'al Gibri in Yeha, Ethiopia. Heritage 9(7), 270.
  Published 9 July 2026 (Crossref: published-online and created 9 July 2026).
  Open access, CC BY 4.0.
  doi:10.3390/heritage9070270
  -- Source of: the 14C date of around 800 BC; the footprint of about 60 by
  60 m and the claim of the largest known palace-like building of its age in
  South Arabia and East Africa; the six-metre podium of stone chambers packed
  with loose rubble; wall thicknesses of 2.20 m (podium) and 1.90 m (ground
  floor), interior walls no thinner; the six monolithic sandstone pillars of
  about 10 m and up to 20 t; the single narrow entrance, the two long
  corridors, the staircase in the south-east corner, the post bases less than
  2 m apart; beams of 0.21-0.28 m, mostly 0.24 m, laid exclusively horizontally
  in alternating layers and joined with wooden nails; African olive and
  Cordia africana; the South Arabian practice of beams laid horizontally and
  vertically; rock and wall paintings of at least nine storeys; the
  reconstruction of five regular and three recessed floors; the finite element
  models of a wall corner and a doorway wall, 3.36 m high, elements of 0.12 m;
  tension in the clay as the decisive failure; 48 and 56 floors in the
  deterministic case; the material ranges and the Monte Carlo sampling; the
  worst-case scaling factors 19 and 23 and the best-case ranges 97-143 and
  119-136; the load factor 1.2; the conclusion of 16 storeys in the worst case;
  the small influence of the wood and the termite resistance of the two
  species; the reading of even performance as a refined design; the
  conclusion that an exceptional load caused the failure, consistent with the
  fire; the outlook listing ceiling loads, the overall geometry, horizontal
  forces, soil stiffness and the fire scenario as open; the later use of the
  same wall construction in Aksumite architecture and Ethiopian churches; and
  the research history (1906, 1971-1973, 2009).

WHAT THE FILM DOES NOT CLAIM

- It does not claim that the palace had sixteen floors. Sixteen is the number
  of floors the ground-floor walls could have carried in the authors'
  worst-case calculation; the excavators' reconstruction still shows eight.
- It does not claim a number for the doorway wall beyond "a little more".
- It does not claim that the authors commented on the press release. The link
  between 19 and 16 is arithmetic on figures that stand in the paper.
- It does not claim that fire has been modelled. The authors say it is the
  next study.
- It shows no real photograph of the site. Every photograph-like shot is
  generated and labelled.

HOW THIS FILM WAS MADE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The narration is synthetic speech. Every still is generated; none of them
reproduces a real find, a real site photograph, a figure from the paper or a
real person. The reconstructions of the standing palace are paintings made
from the paper's written description, not copies of its figures. No
generated image carries writing of any kind. The diagrams are drawn in code;
the ranges and runs in them are schematic and say so.

The opening seconds are built to work without sound - the find first, then a
caption naming the place and the period - and on-screen text is set large
for viewers watching on a television.

The film carries the EU marking for AI-generated content.
