WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every figure spoken in the film was checked against the
primary publication before recording, and those publications are named at the
end - with the reservations that apply to them.

If something here is wrong, tell me and I will correct it and say so.


THE IMAGES
==========

All 40 shots were made for this film. No press photographs of any of the four
finds appear anywhere in it, and no third-party images are used. Press images of
current excavations are under copyright and cannot be licensed for a monetised
channel; rather than borrow what cannot be cleared, this channel draws what the
published record describes.

Two kinds of images appear, and the film keeps them apart on purpose:

  RECONSTRUCTION - AI-generated, marked "AI RECONSTRUCTION" on screen for as
                   long as it is visible, alongside the EU marking for
                   AI-generated content. Photographic-looking shots show place,
                   find and laboratory; painted shots show people and actions,
                   which are always interpretation.

  DIAGRAM        - the overview, the area comparison, the two timelines, the
                   sex-ratio bars and the closing panel. These were drawn for
                   this film, not generated. Image models place lettering
                   unreliably, and two subjects here - Maya calendar signs and
                   Greek script - invite a model to invent writing outright.

One shot deserves its own note. [30] shows a destroyed patch of papyrus with no
legible ink. An earlier version produced convincing Greek lettering, and it was
discarded: the film says the papyrus carries Book II of the Iliad, so invented
characters would have asserted a wording nobody has read.

DIAGRAMS - WHAT THEY REST ON
============================

There is no world map in this film. Drawing one would have meant drawing
coastlines from memory, and this channel does not draw geography it cannot
source. The overview at 0:38 is typographic instead.

The area comparison at 1:08 is to scale: the small rectangle holds exactly
4,497/183,000 of the AREA of the large one, not of its edge length. The timeline
at 1:41 is linear. The Calakmul timeline at 2:56 draws the proposed
early date with SOFT EDGES on purpose - it is a conference finding, not a
peer-reviewed result, and the graphic says so in plain words.


THE FOUR REPORTS
================

1. AMAZONIA - Paerssinen, M., Kalliola, R., Ranzi, A. et al. (2026): "Over
   20,000 precolonial earthworks in the Southwest Amazonia", Nature.
   Airborne lidar over c. 4,497 square kilometres in Acre and Amazonas, Brazil.
   MEASURED: 432 earthworks recorded, 396 of them previously unknown.
   EXTRAPOLATED: 24,000-30,000 across a cultural landscape of c. 183,000 square
   kilometres, dated c. 600 BCE - 850 CE, densest 100-300 CE, with a modelled
   population of 1.25-3 million. The population figure rests on stated
   assumptions and the authors say so. The film keeps the two numbers apart;
   that distinction is the point of the closing section.

2. CALAKMUL - Salazar Lama, D., with Archaios / CEMCA, the Stresser-Pean
   Foundation and INAH (2026). A charcoal mural c. 1.9 m wide, lifted from
   Substructure II C in 2005 and in conservation since, re-read using digital
   imaging, photogrammetry and graphic reconstruction: the hero Juun Ajaw, and a
   sign from the 260-day ritual calendar. Proposed date 400-200 BCE.
   RESERVATION: presented as a lecture in Campeche, not as a peer-reviewed
   paper, and the team states that further sampling is needed before the date
   can be called firm. The film says this out loud rather than in a footnote.

3. MAMMOTHS - Moots, H. M., Diez del Molino, D., Dalen, L. et al. (2026):
   "Ancient DNA and the sex ratios of mammoth accumulations", Current Biology.
   doi:10.1016/j.cub.2026.07.023. 521 woolly mammoths across Eurasia and North
   America, 448 sequenced for the first time; c. 30,000-20,000 years ago.
   Natural deposits: 34% female (421 animals). Eleven human accumulation sites:
   70% female (100 animals). The researchers' own caution is quoted in the film:
   matriarchal herds had a collective defence and were not the easy target, but
   they were the PREDICTABLE one - and the data cannot say whether every animal
   was hunted or some were found dead.

4. OXYRHYNCHUS - Oxyrhynchus Archaeological Mission, Institute of Ancient Near
   East Studies (IPOA), University of Barcelona, directed by Maite Mascort and
   Esther Pons; excavation team under Nuria Castellano, November-December 2025;
   papyrus studied January-February 2026 by Margalida Munar (conservation),
   Leah Mascia (papyrology) and Ignasi-Xavier Adiego. A Roman-era body, c. 1,600
   years old, in Tomb 65, Sector 22, with a papyrus placed on the abdomen as
   part of the embalming: Book II of the Iliad, the Catalogue of Ships. The
   mission had found papyri in that position before, all of them magical or
   ritual text; this is the first literary work.
   NOTE ON DATING: some secondary coverage gives "first to second century CE".
   That figure comes from reporting on other papyrus packets at the site and
   contradicts the mission's own statement. The film uses only "Roman-era, about
   sixteen hundred years old".
   NOTE ON INTERPRETATION: one outlet described the fragment as probably reused
   as cartonnage - that is, as material. The excavation team describes
   deliberate placement. The film follows the team.

WHAT THE FILM DOES NOT CLAIM
============================

It does not claim the Amazon figure is wrong. It is a legitimate projection from
a real survey, and the team labels it as one. What the film argues, at
6:37, is narrower: a projection and a count read identically in a headline,
and it costs nothing to keep track of which is which.

It does not claim the Calakmul date is wrong either - only that it is not yet
settled, which is what the researchers themselves say.

"We don't know" is a finding, not a failure.
