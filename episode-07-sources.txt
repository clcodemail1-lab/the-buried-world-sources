WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every figure, name and date was checked against specialist
sources before the film was recorded, and the works those checks rest on are
named at the end.

This subject has a particular problem: one Egyptian victory wall is the single
richest source for an event that spanned a dozen kingdoms, and it was written
by the winning side. A film that leans on it owes the viewer an account of
what it does and does not carry. Where a figure is contested, this list says
so; where a figure was cut from the script because the sources would not carry
it, this list says that too.

Four things were cut or corrected during fact-checking, and they are set out
in the companion note, "How this film was made". The two that mattered most:
the direction of the last Ugarit correspondence was wrong in the first draft,
and the well-known story of a tablet still sitting in the kiln appears nowhere
in the film - not even as a debunking, because the debunking is no better
attested than the story.

ABOUT THE IMAGES
================

Every photographic-looking image in this film is an AI-generated
reconstruction, and every one of them carries the EU marking and the words
AI RECONSTRUCTION on screen. None of them reproduces a specific artefact,
monument or excavation, and none should be read as a record of one. Where the
film needed a real inscription to be legible - it never does - it would have
been drawn from the published literature instead, as the diagrams are.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

  - The Egyptian reliefs in this film are inventions in the manner of the
    genre, not copies of the wall at Medinet Habu. The wall itself is a
    protected photographic subject and is not reproduced here.
  - Painted scenes are marked as paintings because they show things nobody
    photographed and nobody can photograph: a workshop, a family on a road,
    a granary the moment after it was emptied.


WHAT WAS CUT
============

Four things were removed or corrected at the fact-checking stage. They are
listed because a film that asks the viewer to weigh evidence owes them its
own workings.

  - THE DIRECTION OF THE LAST UGARIT LETTERS. The first draft had Ammurapi
    writing for help and receiving a question in reply. It is the other way
    round: Alashiya warns that enemy ships have been sighted and asks where
    Ugarit's own forces are, and Ammurapi answers that his troops are in Hatti
    and his ships in Lukka. The closing passage was rewritten. Tablet numbers
    are deliberately absent from the film: the literature assigns them
    inconsistently.
  - THE TABLET IN THE KILN. The often-repeated story that the last Ugarit
    tablet was still baking when the city fell does not appear in this film,
    and neither does the debunking of it. What is attested is that the claim
    was "once thought" - which leaves the correction no better supported than
    the story.
  - THE DRIEST SIX PER CENT. The draft said six; the study says 6.25 per
    cent. The film now says six and a quarter. The same study says the dry
    years are "coincident with" the collapse, not that they caused it, and
    the script was held to that wording.
  - FIGURES THE SOURCES WOULD NOT CARRY. Cut entirely: the tonnage of the
    Hittite grain demand (conversions differ by a factor of two), the origin
    of the tin, the length of the Greek illiterate period (300 to 400 years
    depending on the source), and any percentage for population decline.
