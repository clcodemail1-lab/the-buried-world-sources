THE BURIED WORLD — EXPLAINER 02, METHOD
The Scrolls. Nobody Could Read Them. Then Somebody Did.


WHY THIS FILM EXISTS

The channel's films run twelve to eighteen minutes and assume you will sit
still for them. This is a second, lighter way in: the same material, shorter,
in dialogue, with drawn figures doing the explaining.


HOW THE CONDENSATION WAS MADE

The long film has 84 blocks and 13,882 characters of speech. This has
178 lines and 7,765 characters — 56 per cent of it. Every line was written
against a block and carries that block's number, in the film's source list and
in the script file itself. Nothing was written from memory of the long film.

Four things were cut. None of them is a claim; all of them are detail that
does not survive the shorter form:

  - the inventory in figures: eighteen hundred scrolls and fragments, perhaps
    eight hundred books, five hundred and eighty-five unrolled, around two
    hundred published. Four numbers in one breath do not carry when spoken.
  - the second and third scrolls by name — Oxford, seventy columns. The title
    page stays, because it is the thing the reporting merged with the complete
    reading, and that merge is corrected out loud in this film.
  - the two synchrotrons by name. Two institute names in a row carry nothing
    without a picture of the machines.
  - the levels reached in the nineteen-nineties below the Bourbon floor. Cut
    for length at the last pass, and it is the one omission that costs
    something: it is the clearest evidence of how much of the villa is still
    there.

Blocks of the long film not cited anywhere in this one, each with its reason:

  [06]  a forward glance at the June announcement; this film reaches it in order
  [07]  transition into the house
  [29]  restated inside the exchange about what the library is worth [26][27][30]
  [55]  the second and third scrolls by count — the title page is kept, the tally is not
  [56]  the title itself is spoken in the film, as the claim B then corrects [59][60]
  [57]  that the work ran to at least eight books — detail
  [58]  transition ("now the part that got compressed in the reporting")
  [67]  transition, in the first person
  [71]  the nineteen-nineties levels — cut for length, see above
  [78]  transition into the closing section

One block was missing from this film entirely when the script was first
written, and it is the one that mattered most. [54] is where the long film
stops to say what "complete" means: the scroll was unwrapped along its whole
length, but the reading is not clean, and where the surface is lost the text
has holes. Without it, this film would have let "read from end to end" stand
unchallenged — and would have sounded more certain than its own source. It was
caught while looking for objections to give to B, not by any of the checks.
Four lines were added and recorded.

This is the failure mode a simplified version has: not a false claim, but a
missing limit. It is the reason this file exists.


WHERE THIS FILM HOLDS BACK, AND WHY

The long film qualifies in four places. All four are in here, because a
shorter version that sounds more certain than its source would be worse than
no shorter version at all.

  - the owner of the villa. The best-supported guess is a Roman senator, the
    father-in-law of Julius Caesar. The film says, in the same breath, that
    there is no deed and that it stays a guess [14].
  - what "read from end to end" means. The scroll was unwrapped along its
    whole length; the reading is not clean, and where the surface is lost the
    text has holes. The team says so, and so does this film — B asks, and A
    takes it back [54].
  - the attribution of the newly read text. A Stoic handbook in an almost
    entirely Epicurean collection is a reason to be more careful, not less;
    that is the papyrologist's own phrasing [63][64]. The film also carries
    the caution that cuts the other way: Chrysippus was already in this
    library, so a Stoic on those shelves is not unheard of [65].
  - the second, Latin library. It is the single most repeated claim about this
    site, and nobody has seen it. The film calls it a hypothesis [72][73].

One more thing belongs here, because it is the objection a viewer will have
and the film does not dodge it: a model trained on text we already have,
reading text nobody has seen, could in principle invent a word. The answer
given is the one the record supports — the prize conditions were fixed before
anybody read anything: four passages of a hundred and forty characters, at
eighty-five per cent legibility [45][49]. That is a check, not a proof, and
the film does not call it more than it is.


THE SECOND VOICE

B is not there to feed lines. Every objection B raises is one the long film
raises and answers itself — that the lumps of charcoal were worth throwing
away [16], that peeling one scroll in four years is not a method [22][23],
that a library of one man's lecture notes is a disappointment [26][30], that a
model reading unseen text could be inventing it [45], that "read end to end"
cannot mean every word [54], that a Stoic book in an Epicurean library is a
small thing to call a discovery [66], that archaeologists who would rather not
dig are being convenient [77], and that nobody bothered with these scrolls for
two hundred and fifty years [79].

And B is right once. A gives the announcement the way it was reported
everywhere — the first scroll read from end to end, Philodemus, On the Gods,
book eight. B takes it apart: those are two different scrolls. The title page
came off one, the complete reading off another, and they were announced
together. A concedes [59][60]. If the format ever has B lose every exchange,
it has stopped being honest.


WHAT WE GOT WRONG WHILE MAKING THIS

All of these were caught before publication, most of them by checks that
exist because an earlier film went wrong in the same way:

  - The script was written at 220 lines and would have run to 10:38, against
    8:54 for the first explainer. A difference in retention could then have
    been the subject or the two extra minutes, and the comparison would have
    answered neither. Cut to length twice — not by removing objections, but by
    having A describe less and answer more.
  - Both cuts broke the text where they joined. Eight places: an answer to a
    question removed while the question stayed, two pronouns left without a
    referent, a line ending in a comma, a sentence that read as present tense
    where it described a position since overturned. Found by reading the
    speech aloud end to end; the build now lists every join a cut created, so
    the next one is read in twenty places instead of a hundred and seventy.
  - The scene read its audio lengths from the first film's folder. It found
    nothing there, fell back line by line to an estimate, and built a timing
    grid twenty seconds short — every background change and both schematics
    would have sat against the wrong sentences. Nothing reported an error. It
    was caught only by the check that holds the assembled audio against the
    computed grid.
  - The flag that says "this film is voiced" asked whether any recordings
    existed, not whether these lines had them. The other film's recordings
    answered yes.
  - Two numbers in the script cited the wrong block, and one stated one and a
    half metres of papyrus where the long film says roughly one point four.
    Found by a check that now holds every figure in a line against the block
    it cites.
  - This file itself, on its first run, carried the first film's title and
    block count with this film's line count.

None of these changed a claim that reached the screen. They are listed because
the channel publishes its workings, and because every one of them would have
been invisible in the finished film.


LABELLING

The EU AI Act mark is on screen from 0:03 to 0:15 in the lower half, and a
small permanent mark sits bottom left for the whole film, so it survives a
clip taken out of the middle. Proven on the finished file, not on the script:
across the 15-second edge, 56.4 % of pixels change in the mark's area against
0.1 % in a control area of the same size. The permanent mark was checked at
8 points spread over the film, the last at 553 seconds.
