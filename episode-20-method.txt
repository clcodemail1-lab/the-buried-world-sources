THE BURIED WORLD
Episode 20 - The Staircase Under the King's Inscription: Arsameia's Tunnel
How this film was made, and what the fact check changed


WHY THIS PAPER

In November 2026 the Journal of Roman Archaeology published a new reading
of one of the strangest features of the ancient kingdom of Commagene: a
staircase cut by hand 158.1 metres down into the rock of the royal
sanctuary at Arsameia on the Nymphaios, its entrance framed by the Great
Cult Inscription of King Antiochos I. Most scholars treat such tunnels as
water tunnels; some see cult places. The new study (Lennart Wouter
Kruijer, Leiden University) does not pick a side. It asks what the tunnel
did to the people who used it. The film follows that shift - and is clear
that it is an interpretation, not an excavation result.


WHAT WAS READ

The paper (Kruijer 2026, Journal of Roman Archaeology, online 19 November
2026, pp. 1-20, doi:10.1017/S1047759426101196) is open access under CC BY
4.0 and was read in full as a PDF on 26 November 2026, including all
footnotes. Its Figures 2, 3 and 7 (photographs by the author and by Stefan
Riedel) are shown in the film under that licence. Figures 4-6 of the paper
are reproduced there from Brijder 2014 and are NOT used in the film; the
plan of the sacred road in the film is our own simplified drawing.
The 1963 excavation report (Doerner and Goell, Istanbuler Forschungen 23)
and the other works the paper cites were not read in the original; where
the film uses them, it uses them as the paper reports them, and says so.
Context outside the paper (the excavators, Nemrut Dag, the end of the
kingdom, Lucian's birthplace) was checked against a 1965 review of the
excavation report in Belleten, Harvard's Semitic Museum, a 2010 paper by
Belmonte and Gonzalez Garcia in the Journal for the History of Astronomy,
the Turkish Ministry of Culture, and encyclopaedia entries used only as
signposts; details in KONTEXT-BELEGE.md.


WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED

1. THE RELIEF ABOVE THE TUNNEL IS NOT CERTAINLY ANTIOCHOS.
   The draft said "Antiochos in Persian dress clasped the hand of Herakles".
   The paper identifies the king as Antiochos I (after Brijder 2014); other
   descriptions of the site identify the king on the Arsameia handshake
   relief as his father Mithridates I. The film now says "a king in Persian
   dress" and adds that the study names him as Antiochos while others have
   seen his father.

2. NEMRUT DAG IS NOT CERTAINLY ANTIOCHOS' GRAVE.
   The draft said Antiochos built the mound on Nemrut Dag "for himself".
   His burial chamber has not been found, and the author of the new study
   has himself asked whether Nemrut was a cenotaph (Kruijer and Versluys
   2025, cited in the paper, note 5). The film now says nobody has yet been
   able to show whether he lies inside.

3. THE WORKMEN'S FEAR: DEPTH AND DISTANCE, NOT TIME.
   The draft said the digging "went down into darkness for weeks". The
   paper gives no duration. It says the workers' apprehension grew once
   they were beyond 50 metres in depth and 70 metres from the entrance.
   The film now uses those two numbers.

4. "BASINS" BECAME "HOLLOWS".
   The paper (after Doerner and Goell 1963) describes two circular shafts,
   2.50 m across and only 40-50 cm deep. "Basins" suggested water
   installations, which the paper does not claim. The film says "round
   hollows ... less than half a metre deep".

5. THE AUTHOR'S VISIT.
   The draft said "nearly seventy years later". The excavations ran
   1953-1956; the author entered the tunnel in spring 2019. The film says
   "more than sixty years later".

6. NO INVENTED THEOLOGY.
   The draft named a Greek-Persian god pair (Zeus-Oromasdes) that the paper
   does not mention. It was replaced by the pair the paper does name:
   Artagnes-Herakles.

7. THE WATER LINE OF THE INSCRIPTION.
   The inscription says the king had springs built close by instead of a
   deep and distant supply (A 50-54). The draft asked whether this line
   "praises the tunnel or quietly replaces it". The paper does not connect
   the line to the tunnel. The film now only says that the text does not
   tell us whether the line has anything to do with the tunnel.

8. LUCIAN'S BIRTHPLACE.
   Lucian was born in Samosata around 125 AD, when it was no longer a royal
   capital. The film says "Samosata, the kingdom's old capital".

10. THE EUPHRATES CROSSING WAS DROPPED.
   The study describes the imagined route from Doliche as "crossing the
   Euphrates". Doliche and Arsameia both lie on the right (western) bank,
   and the study does not say which route it has in mind. The film keeps
   the seven days, the valleys and the Taurus foothills and leaves out the
   crossing.

9. "A FRENCH SCHOLAR" BECAME "ONE SCHOLAR".
   The processional reading is Albert Gabriel's (1966, as cited in the
   paper). His nationality was not checked; the film does not state it.

WHAT THE FILM SHOWS

Fourteen shots are real photographs: Figures 2, 3 and 7 of the study (CC BY
4.0; photographs by L. W. Kruijer and Stefan Riedel) and eight photographs
from Wikimedia Commons under CC BY 2.0 (Klearchos Kapoutsis, 2010; Herbert
Frank, 2007) - the handshake relief above the tunnel, the tunnel entrance and
inscription, the tunnel from inside, and Nemrut Dag. All are credited in the
source list. Every other photograph-like shot is generated and labelled on
screen as AI reconstruction; the generated reliefs show reliefs of the kind,
not the reliefs of Arsameia. The maps, the plan of the sacred road, the
section of the tunnel and all quotations are drawn in code.
