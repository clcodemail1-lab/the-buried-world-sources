THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 04 - Nobody Has Ever Found These People
How this film was made

Last updated 10 August 2026


THE SHORT VERSION
=================

Three papers came out in the last two weeks of July and the first week of
August. In all three, the people the paper is about are not available: there
is no skeleton that matches the footprints, there is no fossil at all for the
two genetic lineages, and the three Assyrian kings survive as names attached
to objects that were deliberately defaced.

That is the film. It is not a claim that anything is being hidden. It is an
argument that proving somebody was present and working out who they were are
two different jobs, and that we are much better at the first.


HOW THE ITEMS WERE PICKED
=========================

About fifty stories were read for this week's round-up, across news portals,
journals and government excavation announcements. Most were dropped, and the
reason is worth stating: the great majority rest on a single press release
with no publication behind it. The colossal statue from Sardis was announced
by a minister on social media. The four hundred and fifty mice from the
sanctuary at Gortyn - easily the most interesting find of the week - are
documented only on the excavation team's Facebook page, with the season still
running.

Those may all turn out to be excellent. They are not yet checkable. The three
items in this film were chosen because each has a peer-reviewed publication
with a DOI, which meant the numbers could be pinned to something.

Two stories were left out for a different reason. A mass grave near Athens and
a massacre hypothesis from a British villa are both strong material, and two
violence stories in one round-up would push this channel into a register it
does not want. A fourth candidate - six hundred and twenty-six masons' marks
from a gate at Ptolemais, peer-reviewed and thematically perfect - was held
back because the finished narration came in at eight minutes and forty-four
seconds and a fourth item would have burst the format.


WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED
===========================

Eight things, and all of them in the film's favour. The full list is in the
source document. Three are worth repeating here.

The paper's own abstract says the footprints indicate bodies larger than the
largest skeletal estimates for Paranthropus boisei. The detailed reporting
does not carry that. What is supported is narrower: all seven measurable
individuals exceed the published average, and four of the seven exceed the
largest skeletal estimate used in the comparison. The film says the narrow
version. Taking the abstract sentence at face value would have been the easy
route, and it would have passed a lazy check, because it is a direct quotation
from the paper.

The press release and several outlets state that the tracks belong to
Paranthropus boisei. The paper leaves the attribution open and explicitly
raises Homo erectus as the alternative. Here a press release contradicts the
study it is describing, and the study wins.

"The ghost lineage split 800,000 years ago" appears everywhere. It is a point
estimate with a published interval running from 610,000 to 1.27 million years.
The film says the interval out loud. A confidence interval read aloud costs
about four seconds and is the difference between a measurement and a slogan.


WHAT COULD NOT BE READ
======================

This matters enough to be its own section.

The Science paper is paywalled. Its preprint is open, and every figure quoted
in the film comes from the preprint - including one that appears in no press
coverage anywhere: in the team's own simulations, the deeper of the two
classifications is correct about seventy per cent of the time. That number is
in the film because leaving it out would have made the finding sound firmer
than its authors do.

The PNAS paper is paywalled and no preprint was found. The film uses the
abstract, the Max Planck release and reporting that quotes the paper. The
regression equations behind the stature estimates were not read.

The Journal of Cuneiform Studies paper is labelled open access, and it would
not open - every route returned an error. The film is therefore based on the
abstract and the Yale release, and it says in the narration that no
Assyriologist outside the paper has commented in the month since publication.
That is not a swipe at the authors. One month is normal. It is a statement
about how much weight the item can carry.


ON THE PICTURES
===============

Every image was made for this film and is marked AI RECONSTRUCTION on screen.
The diagrams are drawn in code and carry no marking, because nothing about
them is generated.

One production note belongs here because it changed the film. The first
attempts at painted scenes of early hominins kept producing figures in hooded
cloaks - a costume roughly a million and a third years out of place. After
several failures the scenes were rebuilt without any figures at all: a trail
of prints across wet silt, a shelf of skull casts, a plinth with only the feet
left on it. That was a workaround, and it turned out to be the right picture.
A film about people who cannot be identified has no business inventing their
faces.

There is also a mistake worth admitting. Three rounds of those retries were
judged against images that had never actually downloaded - a shell quirk had
been writing them to the wrong filenames without reporting an error. The
conclusion drawn from that, that the model was refusing instructions, was
wrong. It was caught by comparing checksums rather than by looking harder at
the pictures.


CORRECTIONS
===========

If something in this film is wrong, it will be corrected out loud rather than
quietly. Comments pointing at a specific figure and a specific source are the
most useful kind.
