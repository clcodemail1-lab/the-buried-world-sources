WHY THIS LIST EXISTS

This channel makes claims about the past using images that were generated by
a machine. That only works if you can check the claims. Everything the film
states is listed below with its source, and everything the film could not
verify was cut - the companion file episode-08-method.txt sets out the ten
corrections made before recording.


THE TWO PUBLICATIONS THIS EPISODE RESTS ON

Allaby, R.G., Ware, R., Cribdon, R., Hansford, T.A., Kinnaird, T.,
  Hamilton, D., Kistler, L., Murgatroyd, P., Bates, R., Fitch, S. &
  Gaffney, V. (2026). Early colonization before inundation consistent with
  northern glacial refugia in Southern Doggerland revealed by sedimentary
  ancient DNA. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.
  Published 10 March 2026. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2508402123
  -- 252 samples from 41 marine cores along the Southern River. Source of
     the 16,000-year date for oak, elm and hazel; of the Tilia record before
     the Allerod; of the Pterocarya identification and its deamination
     argument; of the taphonomic model (89-98% local in fine silts, up to 70%
     influxed in coarse sands); and of the wording "final submergence
     possibly as late as the 6th millennium BP".

Walker, J., Gaffney, V., Fitch, S., Muru, M., Fraser, A., Bates, M. &
  Bates, R. (2020). A great wave: the Storegga tsunami and the end of
  Doggerland? Antiquity 94(378), 1409-1425.
  DOI: 10.15184/aqy.2020.49
  -- Core ELF001A from the Outer Dowsing Deep, about 42 km from the present
     coastline: the first offshore identification of the Storegga deposit in
     the southern North Sea. Source of the conclusion that the event was
     "neither universally catastrophic, nor was it a final flooding event".


FURTHER SOURCES

Dekker, J., Peeters, H., Amkreutz, L. et al. (2021). Human and cervid
  osseous materials used for barbed point manufacture in Mesolithic
  Doggerland. Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports 35.
  -- The ZooMS study of ten barbed points, two of which proved to be human
     bone. The film gives the sample size in the same sentence as the result.

Amkreutz, L. et al., on the engraved bone from the Brown Bank area
  (Rijksmuseum van Oudheden, Leiden). Radiocarbon c. 13,480-13,285 years
  before present.
  -- The oldest decorated object recovered from the North Sea. Sources
     describe the species as aurochs or bison; the film says "a large wild
     cattle species" and does not choose.

Bonsall, C. - radiocarbon determination on the Colinda point, 1988.
  -- The measurement that moved the harpoon from the Mesolithic to the Upper
     Palaeolithic, c. 11,950-11,300 cal BC. Norfolk Historic Environment
     Record for the find circumstances, including the statement that no peat
     was retained and that the position is only roughly known.

Gaffney, V., Fitch, S. & Smith, D. - Europe's Lost Frontiers and the earlier
  mapping of Doggerland from commercial seismic survey data.
  -- Source for how the landscape was mapped at all, and for the naming of
     the Southern River.

Tsunami deposit elevations (10-12 m western Norway, 3-6 m north-east
  Scotland, above 20 m Shetland) and the displaced volume of roughly
  2,400-3,200 km3 are taken from the Storegga literature; the film quotes
  the range and treats "above 20 m" as a lower bound.


WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT IN THE FILM

- No figure for the area of Doggerland. Published estimates run from
  46,620 km2 to over 200,000 km2 - a factor of four.
- No rate of sea-level rise in metres per century.
- No water depth or distance for the harpoon find site.
- No length in kilometres for the Southern River.
- No date for the final submergence. The publication gives a possibility
  spanning a thousand years, and the film says so.


ABOUT THE IMAGES

Every image in this film was generated by an AI image model from a written
prompt, or drawn programmatically. None is a photograph of a real object,
site or person, and no third-party rights material is used. Each image is
labelled on screen as an AI reconstruction, and the EU marking for
AI-generated content is shown in the opening seconds.

The maps and charts are drawn from coordinates and published figures, not
generated - a picture model cannot be trusted with a number.

ABOUT WHAT THE MAPS DO NOT SHOW

No outline of Doggerland is drawn anywhere in this film. The maps show
points at their true coordinates on a grid, and nothing else. Drawing a
coastline would assert an area that the sources do not agree on.

The Southern River appears on one map as a shaded band, labelled in the
image as schematic. It marks roughly where in the frame the river system
lies. It is not a surveyed course.

HOW TO CHECK THIS FILM

Both central publications are open access. The PNAS paper is the one to read
if you only read one: its taphonomic model - which samples can be trusted and
which cannot - is the part of the argument the film spends most time on, and
it is the part popular coverage left out.

If you find an error in this list, the channel wants to know. Corrections are
made in the pinned comment and, where they matter, in a follow-up.
