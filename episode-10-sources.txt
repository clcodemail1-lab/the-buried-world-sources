WHY THIS LIST EXISTS

This channel makes claims about the past using images that were generated by
a machine. That only works if you can check the claims. Everything the film
states is listed below with its source, and everything the film could not
verify was cut - the companion file episode-10-method.txt sets out the
corrections made before recording, including a figure that was simply wrong.


THE PUBLICATIONS AND ANNOUNCEMENTS THIS EPISODE RESTS ON

Angelotti, G., Parsons, S., Nicolardi, F., Nader, Y., Johnson, S., Josey, D.,
  Henderson, P. et al. (2026). Complete virtual unwrapping and reading of a
  rolled Herculaneum papyrus. arXiv:2606.29085, submitted 27 June 2026.
  -- Source of the complete reading of PHerc. 1667; of the figures used in
     the film (roughly 1.4 metres of papyrus, about 22 columns); of the
     identification of the title on PHerc. 139 as Philodemus, On Gods, book
     eight; of the statement that the readings are fragmentary where the
     papyrus surface is lost; and of the independent confirmation of the 2023
     readings on PHerc. Paris 4.

Vesuvius Challenge (25 June 2026). An entire Herculaneum scroll has been read
  for the first time. Announcement, Biblioteca Nazionale di Napoli.
  https://scrollprize.org/firstscroll
  -- Source of the date and place of the announcement; of the description of
     PHerc. 1667 as a work of ethics with a Stoic focus, dated to the second
     century BC; of the naming of Aristocreon, nephew and pupil of
     Chrysippus, in the last surviving column; and of the seventy-plus
     columns recovered from PHerc. 172.

Nicolardi, F., quoted in the reporting of 25-29 June 2026.
  -- Source of the caution used in the film: that the text comes from an
     almost entirely Epicurean collection is a reason to be more careful
     about the attribution, not less.

Seales, W.B. et al. (2016). From damage to discovery via virtual unwrapping:
  Reading the scroll from En-Gedi. Science Advances 2(9), e1601247.
  DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.1601247
  -- Source of the En-Gedi scroll being read in software in 2015 and
     published in 2016; of its content being the opening of Leviticus; and
     of the method the film describes as virtual unwrapping.

Vesuvius Challenge (October 2023 and February 2024). First letters prize and
  grand prize announcements.
  https://scrollprize.org/firstletters and https://scrollprize.org/grandprize
  -- Source of Luke Farritor reading the word PORPHYRAS; of the grand prize
     going to Youssef Nader, Luke Farritor and Julian Schilliger for more
     than 2,000 characters; and of the prize condition (four passages of at
     least 140 characters at 85 per cent legibility).

Standard reference works on the collection and the villa
  -- Source of the figures for the collection: more than 1,800 scrolls and
     fragments on the official list, perhaps 800 original works; 585 fully
     unrolled by the middle of the twentieth century, around 200 of those
     ever published; Philodemus with 44 identified works; parts of Epicurus,
     On Nature; and parts of two works by Chrysippus already identified in
     this library. Also of the 1752 discovery, of Camillo Paderni first
     recognising the writing and of his method of cutting the rolls
     lengthways, and of Antonio Piaggio's machine of 1756 taking four years
     over the first scroll.

Janko, R. Published arguments on the library of the Villa dei Papiri.
  -- Source of the argument, labelled in the film as a hypothesis, that a
     Latin library should exist in the unexcavated part of the house.

NOT USED, AND WHY: the number of scrolls still unopened. Sources give about
270, "some 400" and "more than 600" - a factor of two apart. The film says
"several hundred" throughout and gives no figure. The same applies to the
temperature of the pyroclastic surge, widely reported as 750 C and given in
the specialist literature as a range from roughly 240 to 500 C.

WHAT THE IMAGES ARE NOT

The man shown in shots 14, 16 and 23 is a painting made for this film,
following the phenotype in Wang et al. 2023. The figure in shots 7, 8, 17 and
80 is a generated image of a museum reconstruction - it stands for the
familiar reconstruction the film argues with, and it is not a photograph of
the one in Bolzano.

No image in this film shows human remains. Shot 15 shows a conservation cell
through frosted glass; shot 73 shows a radiograph; the wound is drawn as a
schematic in shots 74, 75 and 87. That is a deliberate limit, not an
oversight.

Every image carries a permanent AI label, and the official EU marking is
shown in full between 0:03 and 0:15.
