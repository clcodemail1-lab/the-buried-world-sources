WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every figure was checked against the sources named at the
end before a word was recorded.

There is a particular reason this time. This film is about a find being read
off its neighbours, so it states its own neighbourhood: which shots are
reconstructions, which are diagrams drawn from published figures, which
numbers were measured by somebody else, and which of the three papers could
actually be read in full.

All three papers are in the same issue - Antiquity, volume 100, number 412,
August 2026 - and all three are open access. That is unusual and worth saying
out loud: nothing in this film rests on a press release.

No photograph in this film is of a real object or a real place. Every image is
either a reconstruction made for the film and marked AI RECONSTRUCTION on
screen, or a diagram drawn for it. Nothing is a press picture, and no
third-party image was used as a reference.

Two deliberate omissions. No human remains appear anywhere in this film: the
Tamir necropolis is shown as stone rings in grass and as counted dots, never
as bodies or open graves. And nothing here portrays a specific catalogued
find - the helmets, the grave goods and the bird bones are reconstructions of
a type.

SOURCES
=======

All three papers are in Antiquity, volume 100, number 412, August 2026.


ITEM 1 - THE HELMETS OFF BENICARLO

  Frallicciardi, M., Alvarez-Romero, C., Domenech-Carbo, M. T., Graells i
  Fabregat, R. and Santoro, A. M. (2026). Radiocarbon dating and
  characterisation of textiles preserved in late medieval helmets from
  Benicarlo (Castellon, Spain). Antiquity 100(412), 1068-1087.
  doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10341

  ACCESS: open access, CC BY. At least forty-three iron helmets recovered in
  1990 from the underwater site of Piedras de la Barbada at about six metres
  depth. Textile linings preserved inside marine concretion; five radiocarbon
  samples run at Beta Analytic (Miami) and CEZA (Mannheim). Four calibrated
  ranges fall between 1306 and 1444; the fifth, B.II.2, gives 1474-1638 and
  does not fit.

  ON THE EARLIER ATTRIBUTION: the paper states it plainly - "based on their
  association with early material found at the site" - amphorae, Roman anchor
  stocks and bronze helmets including Montefortino types of Punic War date.
  That is an inference from context, not a published date that has now been
  overturned. The film says so.

  ON THE LINING: the authors raise the obvious objection themselves. Part of
  the sampled textile could have been replaced during the period of use, in
  which case the date belongs to the replacement. They argue against it - no
  repair stitching, four agreeing dates, a typology that fits - but they write
  it down. So does the film.


ITEM 2 - THE TAMIR NECROPOLIS

  Alcouffe, A., Duchesne, S., Labba, C., Noost, B., Gerard, P., Trixl, S.,
  Zvenigorosky, V., Erdene, B., Keyser, C., Ludes, B., Riberon, A., Turbat,
  T., Boyer, A. and Crubezy, E. (2026). Genetic relatedness, social status and
  cemetery organisation: the Xiongnu Tamir necropolis, Mongolia.
  Antiquity 100(412), 1032-1049. Published online 26 May 2026.
  doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10360

  ACCESS: open access, CC BY 4.0. Forty-four graves, forty-seven individuals,
  roughly 100 BC to AD 100. Nineteen people belong to two lineages spanning
  five to six generations - ten in lineage A, nine in lineage B. Twenty-eight
  show no kinship to anyone else in the cemetery. The organisation of the
  ground tracks status rather than descent.

  ON THE METHOD: linear discriminant analysis, machine-learning classifiers
  and cultural phylogenetics applied to burial customs alongside ancient DNA.
  The lead author states the limit himself: "We are not suggesting that tombs
  or funerary customs are inherited in the same way as genes. What emerges is
  not a biological genealogy, but a 'map' of cultural relationships."


ITEM 3 - THE PIGEONS OF HALA SULTAN TEKKE

  Carter, A. L., Reese, D. S., van Bommel, R., van der Meer, M. T. J. and
  Cakirlar, C. (2026). Uncovering the lives of rock doves (Columba livia) in
  Late Bronze Age Hala Sultan Tekke, Cyprus. Antiquity 100(412), 979-995.
  Published online 21 May 2026.
  doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10351

  ACCESS: open access, CC BY-NC-SA. One hundred and eighty-three columbiform
  specimens, one hundred and fifty-nine identified as Columba livia, a minimum
  of fifty-five individuals. Thirty-seven sampled for carbon and nitrogen
  isotopes. Mean nitrogen-15 of 8.82 per mil against 9.92 for humans at the
  site; standard ellipse area of 1.26, the range seen in managed livestock.
  Ninety-one per cent of the material is Late Cypriot II A-C, the fourteenth
  and thirteenth centuries BC.

  ON THE CLAIM: the authors write that the birds "may have been
  semi-domesticated". Their own framing of the significance is that this is
  not "the origin of pigeon domestication" but "the earliest direct
  biomolecular evidence for a commensal human-pigeon relationship that we
  currently have". The previous earliest direct evidence is morphologically
  domestic pigeon bone from Hellenistic Nea Helos in Greece, about 323-265 BC.


WHERE THIS FILM DISAGREES WITH THE REPORTING
============================================

1. "Archaeologists were embarrassingly wrong about these 'Roman' helmets."
   There was no published Roman date to be wrong about. The attribution was a
   working inference from the surrounding finds, made because iron in that
   condition cannot be dated directly, and the helmets carried features that
   could be read as late Roman or as medieval. The film says assumed, and
   gives the reason.

2. "43 helmets." The paper says at least forty-three, and that this is likely
   an underestimate - the count is what can be seen at the surface of the
   concreted block. The film says "at least".

3. "Fourteenth to fifteenth century", stated flat. Four of five samples agree;
   the fifth is a century and a half later. A chart with only the four would
   be the tidy version. Both the film and diagram [11] carry all five.

4. "Wealth and power mattered more than family." The study does not set riches
   against kin. It shows that the arrangement of the ground does not follow
   kinship. Nineteen people in two lineages are a family - they are simply not
   buried as one.

5. "Pigeon domestication is a thousand years older than we thought." The
   authors say the opposite in as many words: this is not the origin. It is
   the earliest direct chemical evidence of the relationship. The film uses
   their wording.

6. "The pigeons' diet was almost identical to the humans'." Close, not
   identical - 8.82 against 9.92 per mil, about a third of a trophic step. The
   argument that carries weight is the tightness of the spread, and that is
   what the film uses.


WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
===================

  That anyone was careless. Proximity is the first thing an excavation gives
  you and very nearly the best - stratigraphy is proximity, context is
  proximity. The failure in all three cases is not that someone read the
  neighbours, but that the neighbours stayed the only witness.

  That the new dates are free of the same problem. The helmets were dated from
  their padding, which is also only something lying next to the iron. Three
  millimetres instead of three metres. The film says this in the closing
  section rather than leaving it to the comments.

  That ancient DNA settles belonging. It settles relatedness. Adoption,
  marriage, followership and debt fill cemeteries and leave no trace in a
  genome.

  That the isotopes distinguish feeding from tolerating. They do not, and no
  better instrument is coming. A pigeon that was fed and a pigeon that lived
  close enough to steal leave the same signature. That limit is stated in the
  film and it is the point of the closing section.

  That the Tamir plan drawn in diagrams [19], [21] and [22] is the excavation
  plan. It is not. How the forty-four graves actually lie is not stated in the
  accessible text, so all three diagrams are marked schematic on screen.
