THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 10 - The Animals Were Keeping The Records
Full source list

Four papers. In three of them a question about people is answered by an animal
that lived alongside them: why one Stone Age village held while its neighbours
emptied, how far an early Chinese state reached, who in a medieval Baltic port
was eating what. The fourth is the condition attached to all of it - that the
reading only works once you know what the animal was.

Three are in the Journal of Archaeological Science, one in its companion
Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports.

A NOTE ON DATES, because a previous dispatch got this wrong and the correction
is worth keeping in sight. Elsevier assigns an issue months in advance, and an
article appears online long before the issue it is bound into. Crossref records
only `published-print` for all four of these papers - October 2026 for the
three in JAS, November 2026 for the one in Reports - and there is no online
date in the record at all. The Crossref entries themselves were created on
26 August, 10 September, 12 September and 13 September 2026. That is the
deposit date of the DOI, and for this publisher it sits at or very near the day
the article went online. This film therefore names no publication month at all,
in the commentary or here: the issue dates are labels, and the deposit dates
are the closest thing to an event these records contain.

Every DOI below was checked against the Crossref record - title, journal,
volume, pages and author list, not merely clicked. That check is not decorum:
in an earlier dispatch a DOI that resolved perfectly turned out to belong to a
different paper.

WHAT THIS LIST HAS TO SAY ABOUT ITSELF: the full texts of all four papers were
not machine-readable from here. ScienceDirect refuses automated requests. Every
figure quoted in this film therefore comes from the authors' own abstract, plus
background that is marked as background where it appears. Nothing in the film
goes beyond what those abstracts state. Where a number would have needed the
full text - how many animals were in each assemblage, which lineages carry
which labels, what the three categories of animal burial are called - the film
says so instead of estimating.

ONE THING THIS FILM DOES NOT SHOW: slaughter. The first paper turns on culling
and the fourth on burial, and there is no carcass, no blood and no killing in
any shot. What is shown is measurement: trays, callipers, sections, benches.
The reason is not squeamishness. The findings here are counts and comparisons,
and a picture of a dying animal would carry a claim none of these papers make.

ITEM 1 - THE VILLAGE THAT HELD, AND THE PIGS

  Katzir, N., Khalaily, H., Vardi, J. and Sapir-Hen, L. (2026).
  Subsistence strategy and the divergent fates of PPNB megasites: Pig
  intensification as a key to settlement persistence into the seventh
  millennium BC.
  Journal of Archaeological Science 194, 106683. October 2026 issue;
  Crossref record created 26 August 2026. Open access, CC BY 4.0.
  doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2026.106683

  WHAT WAS DONE: a new faunal assemblage from Motza, a large settlement in the
  Judean hills that stayed densely occupied through the Final Pre-Pottery
  Neolithic B, was analysed and its economic structure compared with eleven
  contemporary settlements of the same class across the southern Levant.

  WHAT IT SHOWS: settlements that persisted and settlements that were abandoned
  occupy distinct, non-overlapping regions of the multivariate space. The
  separation is not driven solely by the dominant taxa - sheep and goats - but
  by how far pig husbandry was integrated into the subsistence economy.
  Biometric and demographic data from different phases at Motza indicate
  managed production rather than incidental presence: body size reduction and
  intensive juvenile culling.

  WHAT IS PROPOSED, AND MARKED AS A PROPOSAL IN THE FILM: that pig
  intensification worked as a settlement-proximate, household-level
  provisioning strategy, reducing dependence on landscape-scale grazing and
  buffering the logistical costs of aggregation.

  BACKGROUND, NOT FROM THIS PAPER: Motza is a site of roughly 30 hectares in
  the hills west of Jerusalem, with the Final PPNB occupation dated in the
  excavation reports to about 7100-6700 cal BC. The film's "nine thousand years
  ago" refers to that range.

  WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED: that pigs saved anybody. The paper demonstrates a
  pattern and offers a reason for it.


ITEM 2 - TWENTY-ONE DOGS AND THE TRAFFIC INTO AN EARLY STATE

  Xiaokaiti, X., Li, Z., Zhao, X., Zhao, H. and Wang, X. (2026).
  Ancient mitochondrial genomes of Erlitou dogs reveal diverse population
  dynamics during early state formation in China.
  Journal of Archaeological Science 194, 106692. October 2026 issue;
  Crossref record created 13 September 2026.
  doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2026.106692

  WHAT WAS DONE: 21 ancient dog samples from Erlitou (c. 1750-1520 cal BC) in
  the Central Plains were analysed for maternal genetic structure - the
  mitochondrial line, which passes only from mother to offspring.

  WHAT IT SHOWS: high maternal diversity, which phylogenetic and network
  analyses suggest derives from multiple geographic sources; one individual
  carries a western Eurasian lineage. Combined with previously published
  isotopic evidence for millet-based diets, the authors read this as dogs of
  diverse maternal lineages being incorporated into the local agricultural
  system. They also note that one lineage identified at Erlitou traces its
  presence in the Central Plains to the Early Bronze Age, so established
  chronologies of canine lineage replacement require reassessment.

  WHY NO LINEAGE NAMES APPEAR IN THE FILM: the haplogroup labels are precisely
  the kind of term that has to be looked up before it means anything. The
  commentary says "maternal lines" and "the western half of Eurasia", which is
  what the labels amount to here.

  WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED: anything resting on that single western Eurasian animal.
  It is one dog, and the film says so.


ITEM 3 - WHAT THE CATS OF A MEDIEVAL PORT WERE EATING

  Krajcarz, M., Choy, K., Yun, H., Makowiecki, D., Grezak, A., Iwaszczuk, U.,
  Van Neer, W. and Krajcarz, M. (2026).
  Tracking individual dietary choices in complex past food webs: Integrated
  bulk collagen and compound-specific stable isotope analysis of urban cats.
  Journal of Archaeological Science 194, 106687. October 2026 issue;
  Crossref record created 12 September 2026.
  doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2026.106687

  WHAT WAS DONE: cat bone collagen from medieval Gdansk and six regional
  comparative sites was measured two ways - bulk collagen carbon, nitrogen and
  sulphur, and compound-specific carbon and nitrogen on individual amino acids.
  Cats are used as dietary proxies because they forage opportunistically and so
  sample the resources around them.

  WHAT IT SHOWS: despite apparent isotopic homogeneity in the conventional
  two-isotope space, the expanded framework reveals substantial
  individual-level dietary variability - marine baselines, grain imported from
  inland regions, freshwater and terrestrial sources - and individual trophic
  positions that are uncorrelated with the bulk values.

  THE POINT THE FILM TAKES FROM IT: a uniform result can be an artefact of the
  instrument.

  WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED: which household any cat belonged to. That is not in the
  data, and the film says so.


ITEM 4 - A DELIBERATELY BURIED CANID IS NOT NECESSARILY A DOG

  Siegenthaler, A., Larson, G. and Charlton, S. (2026).
  A deliberately buried canid is not necessarily a dog.
  Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports 75, 106049. November 2026 issue;
  Crossref record created 10 September 2026. Open access, CC BY-NC 4.0.
  doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2026.106049

  WHAT WAS DONE: the correlation between domestic status and burial was
  analysed within the general practice of animal inhumation. Canid interment,
  alone or as part of a human burial, is documented worldwide across the last
  15,000 years, and such burials have been used extensively to support claims
  about the emergence of dog domestication.

  WHAT IT SHOWS: three general categories of animal interment - and that animal
  burial alone cannot be taken as evidence for domestic status.

  WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED HERE, AND THE FILM KEEPS TO IT: the abstract does not
  name the three categories, and it does not say which wild species were
  buried. The commentary therefore states only what a grave does and does not
  establish.

ON THE PICTURES

  Every photographic-looking shot in this film is generated - there is no
  photograph of any real find in it, and no real, identifiable object is
  depicted. What is shown is an example of the TYPE: a cleaned jawbone, a tray
  of bones, a grave pit after excavation. The prompts are in the production
  repository.

  The seventeen drawn graphics carry no AI label because they are not
  generated: they are drawn in code, which is also why the labelling in them is
  correct.

  Living animals appear only in painted reconstructions, at a distance and
  turned away. That is a decision about the argument, not about taste: these
  animals are instruments in the papers, and a portrait would say something
  the papers do not.

  No third-party images or footage are used anywhere in this film, so no
  third-party rights apply.

  Corrections are welcome and will be made out loud.
