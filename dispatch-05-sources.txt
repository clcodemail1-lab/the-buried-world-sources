WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every figure was checked against the sources named at the
end before a word was recorded.

There is a particular reason this time. This film argues that a find only
becomes a finding when somebody goes and looks - and two of its three papers
are behind paywalls. Being vague about which text was actually read would make
the film an example of its own subject. The access position for each item is
stated plainly below, along with three points where the reporting went further
than the studies do.

No photograph in this film is of a real object. Every image is either a
reconstruction made for the film and marked AI RECONSTRUCTION on screen, or a
diagram drawn for it. Nothing is a press picture, and no third-party image was
used as a reference. Tutankhamun's dagger is mentioned in the narration and is
deliberately NOT depicted: it is a real object in a real museum, and a
generated picture of it would be a forgery.

SOURCES
=======

ITEM 1 - THE MARKS AT PTOLEMAIS

  Kordas, A. U. (2026). Life on a Hellenistic Construction Site: Stonemasons'
  Marks on the Tauchira Gate at Ptolemais in Cyrenaica. European Journal of
  Archaeology, First View, published online 30 July 2026.
  doi.org/10.1017/eaa.2026.10038

  ACCESS: open access (CC BY-NC), and the only paper of the three this film
  could read directly. The counts, the two systems and the palaeographic
  dating are the author's own. What was not available to this film is the
  detailed distribution map, which is why the diagram of mark positions is
  labelled schematic on screen.

  Zelazowski, J. (ed.) (2012). Ptolemais in Cyrenaica. Studies in Memory of
  Tomasz Mikocki. Warsaw.
  For the history of research: the site has been described by European
  travellers since the eighteenth century, and the Polish mission has worked
  there since 2001.

  Faculty of Archaeology, University of Warsaw: Polish Archaeological Mission
  to Ptolemais.
  For the interruption caused by the war in Libya and the return in 2023.


ITEM 2 - THE SKY-IRON RINGS

  Gounelle, M. and Mantzourani, E. (2026). Iron in Greece in the second
  millennium B.C.E. Journal of Archaeological Science 192 (August 2026),
  106622.
  doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2026.106622

  ACCESS: paywalled. This film is based on the abstract and on reporting that
  quotes the study directly, principally Science News. The nickel value for
  each individual ring is in the full text and was not read - which is exactly
  why no percentage is spoken in the film.

  Comelli, D. et al. (2016). The meteoritic origin of Tutankhamun's iron
  dagger blade. Meteoritics and Planetary Science 51(7): 1301-1309.
  For the statement that meteoritic iron in the eastern Mediterranean was
  already established, and for the best-known case.

  Rehren, T. et al. (2013). 5,000 years old Egyptian iron beads made from
  hammered meteoritic iron. Journal of Archaeological Science 40: 4785-4792.
  For the Gerzeh beads.


ITEM 3 - THE SHELL BEAD

  Price, B. A., Schinsing, S. M. and Kidwell, J. B. (2026). AMS Radiocarbon
  Dating of Giant Rock Scallop Beads from the Interior of California's
  Southern Coast Range. California Archaeology.
  doi.org/10.1080/1947461X.2026.2659482

  ACCESS: paywalled. This film is based on the summary published by phys.org
  and on further reporting, all of which agree on the figures quoted here:
  8,508 years for the Turkey Flat bead with a range of 8,846 to 8,228,
  3,674 years for the Lichtenstein bead, and 68 kilometres from the coast.

  The excavations took place between 2012 and 2020 ahead of the California
  Flats Solar Project. Direct dating destroys part of the object, and it went
  ahead with the agreement of representatives of the Salinan, Chumash and
  Yokuts.


WHERE THIS FILM DISAGREES WITH THE REPORTING
============================================

1. "Isotope analysis traced the bead to the southern coast." Several summaries
   say this. There was no isotope analysis. The origin follows from the range
   of the species - Crassadoma gigantea lives below the tideline off southern
   California and not on the stretch of coast nearest the site. What was
   measured is the age. The film says so in the segment itself.

2. "Ptolemy VIII, who ruled Cyrenaica between about 145 and 116 BC." The dates
   are the wrong way round. Ptolemy VIII held Cyrenaica from 163 to 145 BC;
   from 145 to 116 he was king of Egypt. The film gives no regnal years at all
   and says only "the second century BCE", because the dating is
   palaeographic - a specialist reading letterforms, not an inscription with a
   date on it.

3. "Meteoritic iron contains up to 50 per cent nickel", and "the thirteen
   rings range from 1 to 50 per cent." Neither figure can be traced to the
   abstract. Iron meteorites typically carry something in the range of six to
   eighteen per cent, with nickel-rich ataxites above that; single extreme
   specimens are not a useful general figure. No percentage is spoken in the
   film, and the diagram counts objects rather than percentages.

4. "About a third of the objects came from meteorites." That is incompatible
   with the study's own counts: thirteen of ninety-one is fourteen per cent,
   and the authors call ten of the thirteen probably meteoritic.

5. "Seven work gangs built the gate." The author writes that the seven letter
   labels MAY represent work gangs, and that the monograms MAY be workshop
   owners or private donors. The film keeps both as interpretations, because
   a mason's mark is accounting rather than a signature.

6. "Egypt has more meteorites." Deserts do not produce meteorites; they
   preserve them and make them findable. The film says "keeps them, and shows
   them".

7. "The bead proves a trade network." The authors say the opposite - one bead
   cannot establish a route, and it could have passed through many hands or
   been carried the whole way by one person. The film says that too.


WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
===================

  That anyone was negligent. None of the three delays is a failure. A tally of
  work only becomes evidence once somebody treats work as a historical
  question; the portable instrument did not exist when the rings were
  excavated; and the Californian permission is the reason there is a date at
  all, not an obstacle to one.

  That the museum stores are full of secrets. They are full of correctly
  labelled, properly stored objects that nobody has yet had a reason to ask
  anything about. That is a different claim, and a duller one.
