THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 03 - Nobody Went Looking For Any Of This
How this film was made

Last updated 9 August 2026


THE SHORT VERSION
=================

Three archaeological finds reported in the first days of August 2026. Every
number in the film was checked against a source before recording. Two numbers
did not survive that check and were cut rather than softened. No press
photographs are used; every image was made for this film and is marked. There
is no gospel here - where a source states a limit, the film states it too.


THE ORDER OF WORK
=================

1. Material check. Around fifteen archaeology stories from 5-9 August were read
   before three were chosen. They were NOT chosen for being the most
   spectacular - a mass grave at Phaleron and a possible fourth-century
   massacre in Britain were both stronger stories and both left out, because
   two violence items in one round-up would move this channel into a register
   it does not want.

   They were chosen because they share a claim: in none of the three cases did
   archaeology decide where to dig.

2. Script. Written as thirty-one numbered shots, in English, with the German
   production notes kept separate.

3. Fact check, before recording. This is a gate, not a review. Nothing gets
   voiced until every figure has a source.

4. Images. Nineteen AI images and twelve drawn diagrams.

5. Voice, music, sound, edit, and a six-part automated check on the finished
   file before release.


WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED
===========================

Two distance figures were cut. Both were plausible and both were attached to
the wrong thing - the most common way a number goes wrong.

  "Ninety kilometres downstream" for Dunaujvaros. Ninety kilometres is the
  distance to the Paks power station. The published figures for Dunaujvaros
  itself disagree with one another depending on what is being measured
  (roughly 60 km straight line, 70 km as usually quoted, 82 km by road), so
  rather than pick one, the film gives no distance at all.

  "Forty miles down the same coast" for Sutton Hoo. It is roughly twenty.

One claim was downgraded. An early draft called the Sizewell C excavation "one
of the largest archaeological operations in Britain". That is a reasonable
thing to think and nothing supported it, so the film now quotes the people
running the project describing it as the largest and most complex dig of a
generation - attributed to them, not asserted.

One connection was deliberately not made. Intercisa was garrisoned for
generations by a Syrian archer cohort. That is documented and it is a good
detail. The altar recovered from the riverbed is dedicated to Jupiter and has
not yet been dated or attributed by the museum. Joining those two facts would
have produced a sentence that sounds like knowledge and is invention, so the
film says instead that the attribution is still open.

Two things were kept out of the film entirely: the energy and shipping
consequences of the Danube low water, which are a different story and would
have taken over the section; and any attribution of the drought to climate
change, which is not what these reports are about.


WHERE THE FILM PUSHES BACK ON ITSELF
====================================

The obvious version of this episode is an outrage piece: look how little of our
past is chosen, look what we are missing. The film includes a section (at 5:48)
that argues against its own momentum. English planning policy only put the cost
of archaeological recording onto the developer in 1990. Before that, a
developer could destroy a site and record nothing. The rule this film describes
is the reason Sizewell was excavated at all, and the reason somebody was
standing beside a railway cutting in Moravia in May.

The finding is a distortion in the sample, not a failure of the discipline. A
version of this film without that section would be more shareable and less
true.


THE IMAGES
==========

Nineteen shots are AI-generated. They are marked "AI RECONSTRUCTION" on screen
for as long as they are visible, alongside the EU marking for AI-generated
content, which also appears full-width in the first fifteen seconds.

Twelve shots are diagrams, drawn in code for this film - because image models
place labels unreliably, and a diagram with an invented number on it is worse
than no diagram.

Two rules govern the AI images here:

  NO PEOPLE in any photographic-looking shot, including modern archaeologists.
  An AI-generated person standing in an excavation would be a claim about a
  real working team. Excavation areas are shown empty. People appear only in
  painted shots, and only in the past.

  NO INVENTED LETTERING. The Roman altar is shown with its carving worn past
  legibility. The two inscribed fragments went through two attempts: the prompt
  forbade legible characters and the model produced plausible-looking letters
  anyway. Rather than argue with the model, the shot was changed - the slabs
  are now shown still crusted with dried river mud, and the inscribed face is
  not in the picture at all. Nobody knows what those fragments say, so nothing
  in this film pretends to.

The map of central Moravia uses real coordinates and shows nothing else: no
rivers, no borders, no distances. Freehand geography is not allowed on this
channel.


SOUND
=====

Narration is a synthetic voice, used consistently across the channel. Music was
composed for this episode - new music every time. Sound effects come from
licensed libraries; the list is in the source document.

Every effect is normalised to a target level BEFORE the mix ratio is applied.
That sounds like a technicality and is not: in an earlier episode four of nine
effects sat forty to sixty-five decibels below the speech and were therefore
inaudible, with no error reported anywhere. The finished file is now measured
for this, and this episode's seven effects all sit in the carrying range.


WHAT THIS FILM DOES NOT CLAIM
=============================

It does not claim the three finds are connected. They are not.

It does not claim that development-led archaeology is a scandal.

It does not claim to know what the Intercisa altar belonged to, what the
inscribed fragments say, or why the Kojetin metalwork was broken before it went
into the water.

The closing statement - that a find map is strong evidence of where we have
looked and weak evidence of where people were - is about sampling. It is not a
claim that the archaeological record is worthless. It is a claim about how to
read it.

"We don't know" is a finding, not a failure.

Corrections are welcome and will be made in public.
