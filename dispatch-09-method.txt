THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 09 - Nothing Here Happened All At Once
How this film was made


WHAT THE FILM IS

Four papers, all in the Journal of Archaeological Science, carried by its
September and October 2026 issues. A burnt house on Cyprus, three caves near
Granada, 294 settlements in Belize, and 146 fragments of Roman glass from a
villa in south-western France. They are together because each of them takes
apart the same habit: reading an archaeological pattern as one simultaneous
event.

The full source list, with every DOI, is in the companion file
dispatch-09-sources.txt.


THE HONEST LIMIT OF THIS EPISODE, AND IT COMES FIRST

The full texts of all four papers were not machine-readable from here.
ScienceDirect answers automated requests with HTTP 403 - including from a real
browser - and the two open repository copies sit behind bot checks. Every
figure in this film therefore comes from the authors' own abstract, plus
background material that is labelled as background wherever it appears.

That distinction matters, so here it is spelled out. What IS verified: that
these four papers exist, are peer-reviewed, and were written by the people
named, in the journals named. Each DOI was checked machine-side against the
Crossref record for type, journal, volume, pages and author list - not merely
clicked. Since this episode the DATE fields are read as well, and read for what
they are: `published-print` is an issue label, `published-online` is an event,
and where the second is missing the record's own creation date is the closest
thing to one. What is NOT verified: anything the full texts say beyond their
abstracts. The film claims nothing beyond them.

Where a number would have needed the full text, the film says so instead of
estimating. It does not give a date for the burnt building at Palloures, it
does not name the Roman villa, and it does not say how many times a piece of
glass was remelted - the paper reports compositional groups, not cycles, and
the commentary says that out loud.


A CORRECTION MADE AFTER THE FIRST CUT, AND IT IS THE WORST ONE

The first version of this film said the four papers were "published in
September" and "published in October". They were not. September and October
are ISSUE dates: Elsevier assigns a journal issue months ahead, and an article
appears online long before the issue it is bound into.

The Crossref records for all four carry only `published-print`. There is no
online date in them at all. What the records do carry is their own creation
date - 30 July, 3 August, 25 August and 27 August 2026. That is the deposit
date of the DOI, which for this publisher sits at or very near the day the
article went online. It is the closest thing to a publication day these records
contain, and it is offered as that and not as more.

How it happened: the DOIs were checked machine-side against Crossref for type,
journal, volume, pages and author list, and the date field was read straight
off and believed. A field labelled `published` was treated as an event when it
was a label. That is precisely the mistake this film is about, committed while
making it. It was caught before broadcast, by the obvious question nobody on
this side had asked: how can a film recorded in August cite papers from
October?

The commentary now names the issues as issues: "a paper in the same journal's
October issue", not "a paper out in October". The affected lines were re-recorded
and the film re-cut.


THREE CORRECTIONS MADE BEFORE RECORDING

1. A landscape description that came from nowhere. The opening line described
   the Cyprus site as sitting "on a hillside above the sea". That the site lies
   on the coast north of Paphos is documented; that it sits on a hillside is
   not, in any source consulted. It was not badly researched - it was not
   researched at all, because it sounded like a good first picture. It now says
   "on the west coast of Cyprus".

   The principle: a setting is a factual claim too. Numbers get checked;
   descriptions of places slip through.

2. A quotation made too narrow. The script had the authors' phrase
   "unequivocally problematised" applying to that one site. The abstract says
   it for Chalcolithic Cyprus as a whole. The correction makes the claim
   stronger, which is unusual - but quoting somebody more cautiously than they
   wrote is still putting words in their mouth, only in the other direction.

3. "Difficult" turned into "impossible". One radiocarbon date from Cariguela
   is markedly older than the others, and the authors write that it "remains
   difficult to interpret". An earlier draft had them unable to interpret it.
   Between those two lies exactly the distinction this whole film is about, and
   the line immediately afterwards - "that is in the paper; it is not a hedge I
   am adding" - only works if the quotation is right.


WHY THERE ARE NO HUMAN REMAINS IN THIS FILM

The second item concerns cannibalism in the Neolithic. There is nevertheless
no bone, no skull, no tooth and no burial anywhere in the fifty shots - not
suggested, not blurred, not in shadow.

Not squeamishness. The finding of that paper is a chronology: three cave sites
long treated as one phenomenon turn out to be separated by more than a thousand
years and to have been done differently each time. A picture of butchered bone
would pull the attention exactly where the text is not looking, and would turn
a dating result into a horror story. The five taphonomic signatures are shown
as a drawn list instead, and the section is otherwise carried by places and
instruments: a cave mouth, a sediment section, a dating laboratory, a dish of
vermilion pigment.

The music follows the same rule. The score for that section is deliberately no
darker than the rest of the film.


THE PICTURES

Thirty-five shots are generated with nano_banana_pro at 2k and 16:9. Fifteen
are not generated at all: they are drawn in code, which is also why the
labelling in them is correct - image models set text unreliably, so every
caption in this film sits in a drawn graphic.

Three generated shots were redone, and two of those failures were instructive
enough to record:

  A scale bar that nobody asked for. The sherd-layer shot came back with a
  measuring scale reading "10cm" - readable text in a film that carries none.
  Removing the words "scale bar" from the prompt did nothing: it belongs to the
  genre "excavation photograph from above", and the model delivers the genre.
  What removed it was a different framing - closer on the sherds, with the
  ground surface and the trench edge almost out of shot, and with them the
  place where such a scale lies.

  Cinnabar that read as blood. The Majolicas shot was meant to show cinnabar
  ore: red mineral in pale rock. Sitting directly after the cannibalism
  passage, wet-looking red on stone reads as one thing regardless of what the
  prompt says. Asking for it dry and matte produced the same macro again. What
  worked was changing the object: not the ore but the pigment made from it, as
  dry powder in a glass dish on a laboratory bench.

  Both are the same lesson. When a picture keeps coming back wrong, the next
  sentence does not help; a different composition does, and if that is not
  enough, a different object.


THE GRAPHICS, AND WHAT THEY REFUSE TO DRAW

The Belize map shows exactly 294 dots, because 294 is published - and their
positions are schematic, because the coordinates are not. Both graphics that
use it say "schematic" on their face. Drawing 294 invented dots on a real
coastline would have made a claim the paper does not support.

The radiocarbon graphic shows the SHAPE of the distributions - clustered,
recurrent, one far older - and says on its face that these are not published
probability values. The Cariguela bar has no endpoints, drawn dashed and
running open, because the abstract gives none.

The glass mechanism carries no numbers at all. That antimony and manganese
oxidise the iron, and that their capacity is spent on remelting, is documented;
ratios are not.


THE SOUND

Voice: ElevenLabs, one fixed voice used on every episode of this channel, at
0.92 speed. Music: six pieces composed for this episode alone. Ambience: field
recordings from a licensed library, never AI-generated - synthetic ambience
sounds dead.

One measurement worth passing on. This channel estimates episode length in
CHARACTERS PER SECOND rather than words per minute, after a previous episode
missed its target by 55 seconds. Predicted length here before the first
recording: 8:59.9. Measured on the first cut: 8:53.0. The two most recent
episodes came out at 15.49 and 15.47 characters per second while their words
per minute differed by seven per cent - because words per minute also measures
how long the words of the subject happen to be.


LABELLING

Every generated shot carries the EU AI-content mark and "AI RECONSTRUCTION"
permanently in the lower left, and its shot number in the lower right. The
official EU mark is shown large in the lower half of the frame from 0:03 to
0:15. The drawn graphics carry no such mark, because they are not generated.

No third-party images or footage are used anywhere in this film.

Corrections are welcome and will be made out loud.
