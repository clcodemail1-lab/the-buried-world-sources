WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every figure was checked against the sources named at the
end before a word was recorded.

There is a second reason this time. All three items are journal papers rather
than site reports, and access to them is uneven: two sit behind paywalls, and
the third is labelled open access but would not open. A film that argues about
the difference between evidence and interpretation has no business being vague
about which text it actually read. The access position for each item is stated
plainly below.

No photograph in this film is of a real object. Every image is either a
reconstruction made for the film and marked AI RECONSTRUCTION on screen, or a
diagram drawn for it. Nothing is a press picture, and no third-party image was
used as a reference.

ITEM 1 - THE FOOTPRINTS

  Hatala, K. G., Roach, N. T., Harris, J. W. K., Kiura, P., Ndiema, E., Sila,
  P., Kipkebut, A., Waweru, V., Kisale, S., Longaye, M., Sale, K. and
  Behrensmeyer, A. K. (2026). Fossil footprints from Koobi Fora, Kenya.
  Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 123(31): e2530996123.
  Published online 27 July 2026.
  doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2530996123

  ACCESS: paywalled. This film is based on the abstract, the Max Planck
  Society press release of 27 July 2026, and reporting that quotes the paper
  directly. The regression equations, the confidence intervals for individual
  stature estimates, and the size of the skeletal comparison sample are in the
  full text and were not read.

  Domínguez-Rodrigo, M. et al. (2013). First partial skeleton of a
  1.34-million-year-old Paranthropus boisei from Bed II, Olduvai Gorge,
  Tanzania. PLOS ONE 8(12): e80347. Open access.
  For the statement that no incontrovertible partial skeleton of the species
  was known before that paper.

  Hawks, J. (2026). The big feet of Paranthropus boisei. johnhawks.net.
  The only substantial comment from someone not involved in the study that
  could be found. He supports the finding and reverses the framing.


ITEM 2 - THE GHOST LINEAGES

  Zhang, Y., Biddanda, A., Johnson, S. A., O'Dushlaine, C. and Moorjani, P.
  (2026). Recovering signatures of archaic hominin introgression using
  ancestral recombination graphs. Science. Published online 30 July 2026.
  doi.org/10.1126/science.aef8874

  ACCESS: paywalled. The full text of the preprint version is open, and every
  number quoted in the film - the coalescence estimates, both confidence
  intervals, the sample composition and the 70 per cent classification
  accuracy - comes from it, not from the press release. Differences between
  the preprint and the peer-reviewed version are possible.
  bioRxiv, doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.03.709416

  Ragsdale, A. P. et al. (2023). A weakly structured stem for human origins in
  Africa. Nature 617: 755-763.
  The competing model, which accounts for the African data without a ghost
  population.

  Durvasula, A. and Sankararaman, S. (2020). Recovering signals of ghost
  archaic introgression in African populations. Science Advances 6: eaax5097.
  The earlier work this study builds on.


ITEM 3 - THE THREE KINGS

  Frahm, E. and Edmonds, A. J. (2026). Journal of Cuneiform Studies.
  doi.org/10.1086/741239
  Yale University news release, 9 July 2026.

  ACCESS: the publisher's page is marked open access, but the full text could
  not be retrieved through any route on 10 August 2026 (HTTP 403). This film
  is based on the abstract and the Yale release. That limitation is why the
  film says out loud that no Assyriologist outside the paper has commented.

  Valk, J. (2019). The Origins of the Assyrian King List. Journal of Ancient
  Near Eastern History 6(1).
  For the manuscript position: five manuscripts of the King List survive,
  three of them substantially, and one of those omits a king the others carry.
  In other words, the list was already known to have been edited.


WHERE THIS FILM DISAGREES WITH THE HEADLINES
============================================

1. "Footprints bigger than any known skeleton." The paper's own abstract says
   the tracks indicate body sizes that exceed the largest skeletal estimates.
   The detailed reporting is narrower and the film follows the narrower
   version: all seven measurable individuals come out above the published
   average, and four of the seven above the largest skeletal estimate in the
   comparison. Those are different claims.

2. "The footprints were made by Paranthropus boisei." Several outlets, and the
   press release, say this. The paper does not. It says the internal shapes
   align with prints previously attributed to that species, and that the sizes
   raise the possibility of Homo erectus instead. The open attribution is the
   whole point of the segment, so the film keeps it open.

3. "Eight males walked together." The paper says the tracks indicate a group
   that included multiple adult males, inferred from print size, with no
   method for determining sex set out in the abstract or the release. The film
   leaves the group composition out entirely.

4. "Two species crossed the same surface within hours." That finding belongs
   to a different paper by the same lead author, published in Science in 2024,
   at a different site. It is not in this study and is not in this film.

5. "Scientists discover two hidden human populations." Nothing was discovered
   in the sense a fossil is discovered. Two statistical signals were separated
   out of living genomes. There is no bone, no tooth, no site and no sequenced
   DNA, and the authors write that recovering hominin DNA beyond a million
   years is unlikely outside permafrost.

6. "The ghost lineage split 800,000 years ago." That is the point estimate.
   The published 95 per cent interval runs from 610,000 to 1.27 million years,
   and the film says the interval rather than the point.

7. "Three lost kings discovered." Nothing was excavated. Three objects that
   have been in collections and in print for a long time - one of them found
   in 1894 - were read differently. The film says that in the segment itself.

8. "The Assyrian King List was thought to be objective." It was not. That it
   was shaped for legitimacy has been discussed for decades, and the
   manuscripts visibly disagree with one another. What is new here is three
   names.


NUMBERS CUT DURING THE FACT CHECK
=================================

  Body mass for the footprint individuals. Four different sets of figures are
  in circulation, from four outlets, and they do not agree. The stature range
  is quoted instead, and the mass was dropped.

  Any percentage for the second, deeper lineage. Three numbers circulate and
  each is measured against a different base: about 0.3 per cent of the
  detected Denisovan ancestry, about 0.002 per cent of the whole genome, and
  3 to 5 per cent within the Denisovan genome itself - that last one taken
  from earlier literature, not from this study. Quoting any of them without
  its base would be wrong, and quoting them together would be nonsense. The
  finding is described without a figure.

  A date for the super-archaic gene flow. "More than 200,000 years ago"
  appears in the press release. It has no counterpart in the full text, where
  the authors say the timing still needs pinning down. Dropped.

  The names and regnal years of the three kings. The abstract and the press
  material differ on the dates, and the rebel's reign is inferred from a
  single dated document rather than attested. Three similar-sounding names
  with uncertain years carry nothing in an audio narrative, so the film names
  the objects instead of the men.
