HOW THIS FILM WAS MADE
The Only Way To Read Them Was To Destroy Them: The Herculaneum Scrolls
The Buried World, episode 10

------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THIS FILE IS

Every claim in the film was checked against published work before the
narration was recorded. This file lists what the check changed. It is
published because the corrections are the point: a film about people who
got a reading wrong should show its own workings.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
CORRECTIONS MADE BEFORE RECORDING

1. THE NUMBER OF SCROLLS — corrected.
   The draft said "seventeen hundred and fourteen scrolls and fragments".
   The official list carries 1,814; general accounts say "more than
   1,800". The film says "more than eighteen hundred".

2. FRAGMENTS ARE NOT BOOKS — added.
   The draft repeated the raw figure without qualification. A shattered
   roll receives several inventory numbers, so the collection of ~1,800
   items represents perhaps 800 original volumes. The film now says so.

3. LENGTH AND COLUMNS OF PHerc. 1667 — corrected to the primary source.
   Press reports gave "nearly 1.5 metres" and "20 columns". The Vesuvius
   Challenge gives roughly 1.4 metres and about 22 columns. The film uses
   the project's own figures.

4. "COMPLETE" DOES NOT MEAN "UNBROKEN" — added as its own passage.
   The scroll was virtually unwrapped along its full length and read end
   to end. The readings are still fragmentary: where the papyrus surface
   is lost, the text has holes in it. The team states this plainly, and
   so does the film.

5. THE ATTRIBUTION OF THE TEXT — made more precise.
   The draft said the names in the text "point towards Chrysippus". What
   the last surviving column actually names is Aristocreon, nephew and
   pupil of Chrysippus. The work is Stoic in character and dated to the
   second century BC. The authorship is open, and the film says it is
   open rather than closing it.

6. A COUNTERWEIGHT TO THE HEADLINE — added.
   "A Stoic text in an Epicurean library" is less startling than it
   sounds: parts of two works by Chrysippus were identified in this
   library long ago. The film includes this against its own story.

7. CAMILLO PADERNI — corrected from a one-sided portrait.
   The draft used him only as the man who destroyed scrolls by cutting
   them in half. He is also the man who, in 1752, first recognised that
   the black lumps carried writing. Both are in the film.

8. THE GARDEN — comparison dropped.
   The draft said the villa's garden was "longer than a football pitch".
   A football pitch is 90-120 m, so the comparison does not hold. The
   film gives the measured figures instead: a colonnaded garden of about
   100 m and an open-air pool of 66 m.

9. THE DATE OF THE ERUPTION — left unresolved on purpose.
   The film says "in the year seventy-nine" and gives no day or month.
   Whether the eruption fell in August or October of that year is still
   disputed.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
FIGURES DELIBERATELY LEFT OUT

- HOW MANY SCROLLS REMAIN UNOPENED. Sources give about 270, "some 400",
  and "more than 600" — a factor of two apart. The film says "several
  hundred" throughout and gives no number.

- THE TEMPERATURE OF THE PYROCLASTIC SURGE. Widely reported as 750 C;
  the specialist literature gives ranges from roughly 240 to 500 C
  depending on site and method. Left out.

- A TOTAL FOR DECIPHERED WORKS. Only the two figures the sources carry
  consistently are used: 585 scrolls fully unrolled by the middle of the
  twentieth century, around 200 of those ever published.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
ON THE LATIN LIBRARY

The film states clearly that no second library has been found. The
argument that one should exist — made for years by Richard Janko — is an
argument from how a house of this class, owned by Latin-speaking Roman
aristocrats, would have been furnished. It is a hypothesis. The film
labels it as one, because this is the single most repeated claim about
the site.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
IMAGES

Every image in this film was generated or drawn for it. Nothing here is a
photograph of the villa, of a scroll, or of any real object, and no image
shows a real person. All images carry a permanent AI label and the
official EU marking appears in full at the start.

Corrections are welcome and will be made out loud.
