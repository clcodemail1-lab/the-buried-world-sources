WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS
====================

A channel that asks you to trust it should show its working. This page says how
the film was put together, what was checked, what had to be corrected or cut
before recording, and where it stops short of an answer.

None of it is required of me. I would rather you could check.


WHAT A DISPATCH IS
==================

This is the first of a second kind of film on this channel. The long ones take a
single find apart. A dispatch takes several recent reports and puts them next to
each other.

The risk in that format is obvious: four press releases, read aloud, in a
confident voice. So this one is built the other way round. The four reports here
share something real - in none of them did anything come out of the ground in
the weeks they were in the news. The earthworks had been lying under the forest
for eighteen centuries. The bones had been in museum drawers for decades. The
mural was lifted off its wall in 2005 and has been in conservation ever since.
What changed is what could be read.

That is the film's claim, and the last two minutes test it rather than celebrate
it.


THE ONE THING WORTH TAKING AWAY
===============================

The Amazonia survey is extraordinary work. It is also the clearest example this
year of a number changing shape as it travels.

  MEASURED:      432 earthworks, recorded by lidar over c. 4,497 square
                 kilometres. 396 of them had never been recorded by anyone.

  EXTRAPOLATED:  24,000-30,000, projected across a cultural landscape of about
                 183,000 square kilometres - roughly forty times the area that
                 was actually flown.

Both belong in the story. The team is explicit about which is which. But a
projection and a count read identically in a headline, and the population figure
- 1.25 to 3 million people - has the same shape: it rests on stated assumptions
about how many lived at each site.

The film says none of this to diminish the study. It says it because keeping
track of which number was measured and which was calculated costs nothing, and
because a channel that reports findings should model the habit.


WHAT WAS CHECKED, AND WHAT CHANGED BEFORE RECORDING
===================================================

Every figure was checked against the primary publication. Four things changed:

1. THE MUMMY'S DATE. Some coverage gives "first to second century CE". That
   figure appears in reporting on other papyrus packets from the site and
   contradicts the excavation mission's own statement. Cut. The film says only
   "Roman-era, about sixteen hundred years old".

2. THE MUMMY'S PAPYRUS - and this is the correction I came closest to getting
   wrong. One outlet, in April, described the fragment as probably reused as
   cartonnage: as material, not as an offering. That would have made a lovely
   story - a find reinterpreted between April and July, from wastepaper to
   ritual. It is not what happened. The excavation team described deliberate
   placement from the start, and the April wording is simply loose. Building a
   reversal out of it would have been inventing a plot. The film follows the
   team.

3. THE CALAKMUL DATE. The proposed 400-200 BCE was kept, but only with its
   status attached: this was presented as a lecture, not as a peer-reviewed
   paper, and the team says further sampling is needed. An earlier, narrower
   range from a previous structural study (400-390 BCE) was dropped - two
   competing precisions in one sentence would suggest a confidence that does not
   exist. The timeline graphic draws that date with soft edges for the same
   reason.

4. THE MAMMOTH FINDING. The headline version is "Ice Age hunters preferred to
   kill females". The researchers are more careful, and their caution is in the
   film as its own passage: matriarchal herds had a collective defence and were
   not the easy target - they were the PREDICTABLE one. And the data cannot say
   whether every animal was hunted or some were found dead and taken apart.


WHY THERE ARE NO PHOTOGRAPHS
============================

Not one press photograph appears in this film.

That is a legal position as much as an editorial one. Images of current
excavations are under copyright, and under German law a photograph of a
public-domain object is itself protected for decades - being ancient does not
make an image free. A monetised channel cannot use them without clearance. So
the film draws what the published record describes, and says on screen that it
is drawing.

The look of an image tells you what kind of claim it makes: photographic-looking
shots show PLACE, FIND and LABORATORY; painted shots show PEOPLE AND ACTIONS -
everything a human being is doing in this film is interpretation. The overview,
the area comparison, the two timelines and the bar panel are DRAWN, not
generated.

All reconstructions are AI-generated and marked twice: the EU marking for
AI-generated content together with the words AI RECONSTRUCTION, in the lower
left, for as long as the shot is on screen. The drawn diagrams do not carry that
mark, because they are not generated.


THREE IMAGES THAT WERE THROWN AWAY
==================================

Image models invent things quietly, and two subjects here invited it.

  THE PAPYRUS. The first version of shot [30] produced convincing Greek
  lettering. It was discarded. The film says the papyrus carries Book II of the
  Iliad; an image with invented characters would have asserted a wording nobody
  has read. The replacement shows only destruction. The shot lost its
  illustrative force and the film is more honest for it.

  THE MURAL. Shot [13] is deliberately weathered past legibility, so that no
  accidental smudge can be mistaken for a Maya calendar sign. The actual sign is
  discussed, not depicted.

  THE LONE BULL. The first version of shot [21] put two human figures on the
  horizon. The sentence it accompanies is about mammoths dying NATURAL deaths,
  alone. The image contradicted the words, which is a smaller error than a wrong
  date and just as avoidable.

There is also no world map in this film. Drawing one would have meant drawing
coastlines from memory, and this channel does not draw geography it cannot
source.


WHERE THIS FILM STOPS
=====================

It does not say the Amazon estimate is wrong. It is a legitimate projection from
real work.

It does not say the Calakmul date is wrong. It says it is not yet settled, which
is what the researchers say.

It does not say whether those mammoths were hunted or scavenged, because the DNA
cannot say.

"We don't know" is a finding, not a failure.
