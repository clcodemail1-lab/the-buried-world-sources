THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 05 - Nothing Here Came Out Of The Ground
How this film was made

13 August 2026


THE SELECTION
=============

Three items were chosen from about forty archaeology stories published between
4 and 12 August 2026. The rule for this channel's Friday strand is simple and
it removed most of them: no peer-reviewed publication, no place in the film.

That rule cost something this week, and it is worth naming. The best material
of the fortnight was a set of inscription fragments from Stratonikeia in
western Turkey - a decree issued on 1 August 480, exposing fifteen years of tax
fraud, with death as the penalty. It is vivid, it is well documented, and the
University of Warsaw says plainly that the full publication is still "a few
months" away. So it is not in this film. It would have fitted the argument
perfectly, which is exactly the kind of reason that should not be allowed to
count.

Also left out: a votive deposit of 450 mice at Gortyn, a colossal statue at
Sardis, new tombs at Hattusa, a dolium off Elba. All press releases, none with
a paper.

One item was excluded for a different reason. A strontium study of the Phaleron
mass grave near Athens (Stamer, Rothwell et al., Journal of Archaeological
Science: Reports 74) is properly published, fits this film's argument exactly -
teeth excavated in 2016, measured in 2026 - and overturns the reading that the
79 shackled men were foreign invaders. It was taken out because a mass grave
with iron shackles pulls a film into a register this channel does not work in.
That is an editorial choice, not a judgement on the study, and the study is
worth reading.


WHAT WAS ACTUALLY READ
======================

Of the three papers in the film, exactly one could be read in full: the
Ptolemais article, which is open access. The other two are behind paywalls, and
the film is built on their abstracts plus reporting that quotes them directly.

That limitation shows up in the film as an absence. No nickel percentage is
spoken for the Aegean rings, because the per-object values are in the full text
and the figures circulating in the reporting - "up to 50 per cent", "from 1 to
50 per cent" - cannot be traced to the abstract. A diagram counting objects is
honest with what is available. A bar chart of percentages would not have been.

The same applies to the drawing of the marks at Ptolemais. The published
distribution analysis exists; the map of it was not available. The film's
diagram therefore says "positions schematic" on screen rather than implying a
plan it does not have.


THE FACT CHECK
==============

Every figure was checked against the sources before recording. Three things
changed as a result, and they are all cases where the reporting went further
than the study.

The first is the one that appears in the film itself. Several summaries of the
California study describe an isotope analysis tracing the shell bead to the
southern coast. There was no isotope analysis. The age was measured by
radiocarbon; the origin follows from the range of the species. That is a
weaker kind of evidence, and it is the kind the study has.

The second is a date. Archaeology Magazine has Ptolemy VIII ruling Cyrenaica
"between about 145 and 116 BC". The two halves of his career are the wrong way
round: Cyrenaica was 163 to 145, Egypt 145 to 116. The film gives no regnal
years and says "the second century BCE", because the dating is palaeographic
anyway - somebody reading letterforms, not an inscription with a date on it.

The third is the nickel range described above.

Three further claims were softened rather than cut. "Seven work gangs" is the
author's own suggestion and she writes "may"; the film keeps the hedge, because
the hedge is the interesting part. "A third of the objects came from
meteorites" is incompatible with the study's own counts. And "Egypt has more
meteorites" describes the wrong mechanism - deserts do not produce meteorites,
they preserve them and make them findable.


WHAT THE FILM ARGUES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
==========================================

The argument is that a find does not arrive carrying its own question, and that
the three delays here have three different causes: nobody had treated a
builder's tally as a historical question; the instrument did not exist; and the
measurement destroys part of the object, so the descendants of the people who
made it had to agree first.

The film does not argue that anyone was slow or negligent, and it says so out
loud in the closing section. Nor does it argue that museum stores are full of
secrets. They are full of correctly catalogued objects that nobody has yet had
a reason to ask anything about. That is a duller claim and a truer one.


PRODUCTION
==========

Written first, then fact-checked, then recorded - in that order, so that a
figure which does not survive checking cannot survive by already being in the
voice track. Length was measured, not estimated: the same script read twice by
the same synthetic voice came out about three per cent apart, which is more
than it sounds like over nine minutes.

All images were generated for this film and are marked on screen. Seven of the
twenty-eight had to be redone, and the reasons are ordinary: a photographer
reflected in a museum case, a painted border where none was wanted, mudbrick
walls in a limestone city, a hooded cloak on a Californian hillside. One image
was refused three times and was solved by changing the subject rather than the
wording - when a prohibition does not take twice, the problem is usually what
was asked for.

Tutankhamun's dagger is named in the narration and deliberately not shown. It
is a real object in a real museum. A generated picture of it would be a
forgery, not an illustration.


CORRECTIONS

If something here is wrong, it will be corrected out loud rather than quietly.
