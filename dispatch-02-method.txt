WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS
====================

This channel makes claims about the past using images that are not photographs
of the past. That is a fair thing to be suspicious of. This page says exactly
how the film was put together, what was changed during the fact check, and
what the film is not able to tell you.

A news round-up needs this page more than a documentary does. Every item in it
arrives with a headline already attached, and the headline is written to be
shared rather than to be accurate.


THE ONE THAT WOULD HAVE BEEN AN EASY LIE
========================================

The silk story has an obvious framing: silkworm cocoons found in Uzbekistan,
two thousand years before the Silk Road, therefore silk did not come from
China. It is a good headline. It is the opposite of what the study says.

The cocoons are Bombyx mori - the domesticated Chinese silk moth, an animal
that cannot fly, cannot feed itself and does not exist outside human care. They
were found together with charcoal from mulberry, the only tree the caterpillar
eats. Moth plus mulberry IS the Chinese tradition. What the find puts in
Uzbekistan is not an independent invention; it is a working piece of that
tradition, three thousand kilometres west of it and two millennia early.

The film says this in its own section, out loud, because a viewer who has read
the headline elsewhere deserves to know why this film disagrees with it.

A second, quieter correction in the same story: these are described as the
oldest preserved cocoons with direct radiocarbon dates anywhere, including
China. That sounds like a statement about history and is a statement about
soil. Damp ground destroys cocoons; dry ground keeps them. The lead author says
so, and so does the film.


THREE MORE CORRECTIONS
======================

1. THE FISH WERE NOT OBSERVED BEING CARRIED. That prehistoric people stocked
   Lake Tesse with trout is the excavators' conclusion, and a well-argued one:
   the lake sits above waterfalls that fish cannot climb, so it had none of its
   own. But it is an inference, and the film presents it as one, with the
   reasoning attached rather than as a finding on its own.

2. NO DEATH TOLLS. The smallpox study measures two individuals in northern
   Chile. It carries no population figures and makes no claim about how many
   people died in the American epidemics. Neither does this film, although the
   temptation to reach for a large number there is considerable.

3. NO "MUMMIES". The reporting uses the word; the study speaks of individuals.
   "Mummy" summons an image that explains nothing about a genome, so the film
   leaves it out.


WHAT THE IMAGES ARE
===================

Every one of the 35 shots was made for this film.

28 are AI-generated (Higgsfield, nano_banana_pro), marked "AI RECONSTRUCTION"
on screen for as long as they are visible, alongside the EU marking for
AI-generated content in the first fifteen seconds. Photographic-looking shots
show place and object; painted shots show people and actions, because those are
interpretation and should look like it.

7 are drawn, not generated: the three-places panel, the silk timeline, the
system diagram, the Lake Tesse timeline, the elevation profile, the virus family
tree and the closing panel. Image models place lettering unreliably, and a
diagram whose labels are decorative is worse than no diagram at all.

There is no world map, and that is deliberate. Without real geodata the
coastlines would have to be drawn freehand, and invented geography is not
allowed here. The three sites are given typographically instead, with their
coordinates.

Two things were ruled out of the imagery before a single prompt was written:

  - No Silk Road iconography in the Uzbekistan section. No camel caravans, no
    markets, no bolts of coloured cloth. The whole point of that section is
    that the find is two thousand years EARLIER than any of that, and caravan
    pictures would flatten exactly the distinction the film is making.

  - No depiction of illness in the smallpox section. No pustules, no suffering
    faces, no bodies. That section is about a laboratory result and about a
    community whose outbreak nobody wrote down. It is shown as a settlement
    with people in it, and then the same settlement without them.

No press photographs are used anywhere - not of the cocoons, not of the traps,
not of the site in Chile. Nothing in this film rests on anyone else's rights.


HOW THE FILM WAS ASSEMBLED
==========================

The script is written first and is the only source for everything downstream:
each spoken block becomes one narration file, and the length of each shot is the
MEASURED length of its narration plus a pause. Nothing is cut to a planned
duration.

Narration: ElevenLabs, synthetic voice, the same voice in every episode.
Music: composed for this film, five pieces following the five sections. New
music is composed for every episode.
Sound: field recordings and effects from licensed libraries, each normalised to
a target level before mixing - a fixed mix factor across sources that differ by
40 dB decides at random which effects you can hear at all.
Camera moves over stills are computed sub-pixel, because integer-rounded moves
visibly stutter at slow speeds.

The whole chain is scripted and re-runnable, and every step is checked
mechanically before release: marking windows, image order, sound levels.


WHAT THIS FILM CANNOT TELL YOU
==============================

Why silkworm cultivation did not persist in Bronze Age Central Asia. The
authors call it unresolved and so does the film.

Whether the cocoons at Sapalli Tepe mean silk was PRODUCED there or only
traded there. The study does not separate those, so the film does not claim
either.

How many people the American smallpox epidemics killed. Not this study's
question.

If something here is wrong, tell me and I will correct it and say so.
