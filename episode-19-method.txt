THE BURIED WORLD
Episode 19 - Were the Inca Children Drugged Before Sacrifice? Their Hair Disagrees
How this film was made, and what the fact check changed


WHY THIS PAPER

In November 2026 a team from Poland, Peru, Argentina and the United States
published the widest toxicological study yet of the children sacrificed by
the Inca on the summits of the Andes: eight individuals from five mountains,
tested for ten substances in hair and nails. Since 2013 the common short
version has been that the children were sedated with coca and alcohol. The
new results are more varied than that, and the paper's own conclusion is
that there was no clear pattern. The film follows that open question - why
every mountain gives a different answer - and the limits of what hair can
show.


WHAT WAS READ

The paper (Socha et al. 2026, Antiquity, online ahead of print) is open
access under CC BY 4.0 and was read in full as a PDF on 16 November 2026,
including Tables 1 and 2, which were checked against the page images
because text extraction breaks them. The online supplementary material
(detection limits, chromatograms) was not read; nothing in the film depends
on it. The two earlier studies of the Llullaillaco children by Wilson and
colleagues (PNAS 2007 and 2013) were read in full through PubMed Central.
Two news reports (HeritageDaily and Archaeology News, both 3 November 2026)
were read for the way the result travelled; where they go beyond the paper,
the film follows the paper. Background on the ritual comes from the Spanish
chronicles as cited in these papers; the film marks it as chronicle, not as
finding. Background on the restitution debate comes from a 2022 news report
(El Heraldo / Telam).


WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED

1. "COCAINE IN ALL EIGHT" BECAME "COCA IN EVERY HAIR SAMPLE - ALL SIX".
   News reports said all eight children had taken coca. The paper found
   cocaine and its breakdown product in all six hair samples; the two nail
   samples (Ampato #1 and Quehuar) showed none.

2. "ALCOHOL IN FOUR" IS COUNTED FROM TABLE 1. The abstract says cocaethylene
   was found "in two (Chuscha and Llullaillaco)" - two sites. Table 1 shows
   it in four individuals: Chuscha and the three Llullaillaco children.

3. THE HARMINE FINDING IS PRESENTED AS THE WEAKEST IN THE STUDY. It is one
   trace (0.6 pg/mg) in one nail sample, from the girl known as Juanita; the
   authors describe the nail samples as contaminated, probably by
   environmental pollution, and ask for her hair to be tested. Harmine also
   occurs in plants other than Banisteriopsis. News reports turned it into
   an ayahuasca drink; the paper does not show a drink.

4. THE PAPER'S OWN INCONSISTENCY IS STATED. The discussion says all
   individuals buried on Ampato received the same substances; Table 1 shows
   harmine in Ampato #1 (nail) and none in Ampato #4 (hair). The statement
   appears to rest on the authors' earlier study of two other Ampato
   children (2022), which the film also reports.

5. "SEDATED" IS ATTRIBUTED. "Heavily sedated" is the 2013 team's
   conclusion about the oldest Llullaillaco girl, not a measurement. The
   2026 reading ("wellbeing and mood") is likewise the authors'
   interpretation. The film says neither reading is something the hair can
   prove.

6. AGES ARE GIVEN AS ESTIMATES. Studies give different ages for the
   Llullaillaco children (2007: 15, 7, 6; 2013: 13 and 4-5; 2026: about 14,
   7 and 6). The film uses the 2026 figures with "about" and says the
   estimates differ.

7. "COCA ON EVERY SUMMIT" BECAME "COCA IN EVERY CHILD WHOSE HAIR COULD BE
   TESTED". From Quehuar only a nail sample was analysed, and it showed no
   coca; the same holds for the nail of Ampato #1. An earlier draft also had
   every child carried up "with coca"; that line was cut.

8. NO CAUSE OF DEATH, NO MUMMY PHOTOGRAPHS. The film describes no killing
   and shows no body. It shows summits, shrines, objects of the type buried
   with the children, a laboratory and a plant.

WHAT THE FILM SHOWS

No photograph of any of the children, their bodies or their faces is used,
and no image shows a killing. Every photograph-like shot is generated and
labelled on screen; it shows a summit, a shrine, an object or a laboratory
of the TYPE described, not a published image. Maps, tables and the
month-by-month chart for Chuscha are drawn in code from the paper's text
and Tables 1 and 2.
