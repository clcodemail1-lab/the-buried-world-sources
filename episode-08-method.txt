HOW THIS FILM WAS MADE
The Buried World — Episode 8
"They Found A Forest Under The North Sea: What Doggerland Actually Was"

------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHY THIS EPISODE EXISTS

In March 2026 a team led from the Universities of Warwick and Bradford
published the largest sedimentary ancient DNA survey ever carried out on
Doggerland — the landscape now under the southern North Sea. It reported
temperate woodland more than 16,000 years ago, thousands of years before
those species are recorded on the British mainland.

That result only means something against an older claim: that Doggerland
was a land bridge, and that a tsunami ended it. This film checks both.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT WE CORRECTED, AND WHY

Every number, name and interpretation in the script was checked against
the primary publication before the narration was recorded. Ten claims did
not survive that check. They are listed here because a channel that says
"the reporting was too tidy" has to show its own work.

1. "A tsunami destroyed Doggerland."
   REMOVED AS A CONCLUSION. Walker et al. (2020) recovered the Storegga
   tsunami deposit offshore in the southern North Sea and concluded the
   event was "neither universally catastrophic, nor was it a final
   flooding event". The film builds this expectation deliberately and then
   resolves it against the publication, rather than repeating it.

2. "Parts of Doggerland were still above water 7,000 years ago."
   RESTATED. This figure appears widely in coverage of the 2026 study. The
   paper's own wording is "final submergence possibly as late as the 6th
   millennium BP" — a possibility spanning a thousand years, not a date.
   The film says so in those terms.

3. The area of Doggerland.
   CUT ENTIRELY. Published figures range from 46,620 km² (the seismically
   mapped area) through 100,000 km² to over 200,000 km² (reconstructions
   of maximum extent). A number that moves by a factor of four is not a
   number. The film gives no area figure.

4. The rate of sea-level rise.
   CUT. Estimates range from 1–2 m per century to "as much as six feet".
   Described qualitatively instead.

5. The Colinda harpoon as "Mesolithic".
   CORRECTED IN THE FILM. The Mesolithic attribution came from typology
   (Maglemose-type barbs). A radiocarbon measurement in 1988 returned
   c. 11,740 BP, calibrating to roughly 11,950–11,300 cal BC — Upper
   Palaeolithic. This correction is told as part of the story rather than
   quietly applied.

6. The find context of that harpoon.
   STATED, NOT GLOSSED. The Historic Environment Record notes that no peat
   was retained from the block the point came out of, and that the find
   location is only roughly known.

7. Distance and water depth at the find site.
   CUT. The record gives roughly 50 miles north-east of Cromer and calls
   the position "poorly located"; other sources say 25 miles. The often-
   quoted depth of 32 m belongs to a different find (a flint hammerstone
   40 km off Cromer), not to this one.

8. The length of the Southern River.
   CUT. No published figure in kilometres was found, so none is given.

9. The Storegga slide volume, and "a landslide the size of Scotland".
   RANGE ONLY; COMPARISON CUT. The displaced volume is given in the
   literature as roughly 2,400–3,200 km³. The film quotes the range. The
   size comparison circulating in popular coverage was dropped.

10. The species of the engraved bone.
    LEFT OPEN. Sources describe it variously as aurochs or bison. The film
    says "a large wild cattle species" and does not choose.

Two further points of care, not corrections:

- Pterocarya. The film does not present this as a settled sensation. It
  gives the authors' actual argument — a deamination signal close to 30%,
  indistinguishable from the oak and hazel in the same samples, in
  sediments their taphonomic model classes as secure — and then states
  plainly what that establishes and what it does not.

- Barbed points made of human bone. The 2021 ZooMS study examined ten
  points and identified two as human. The film gives the sample size in
  the same breath as the result.

HOW IT WAS PRODUCED

Narration: synthetic voice (ElevenLabs). Script written by hand from the
publications listed in the sources file.

Images: generated with an AI image model from written prompts, then
checked individually. They are reconstructions and are labelled as such
on screen throughout, in addition to the EU AI-generated content mark
shown in the opening seconds. No photograph of any real object, site or
person is used.

Maps and diagrams: drawn programmatically from coordinates, not
generated.

Music and sound: composed and mixed for this episode.

No third-party rights material is used in this film. Every image is
original to this production; the prompts that produced them are kept in
the production repository.
