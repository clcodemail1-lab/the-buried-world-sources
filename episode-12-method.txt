THE BURIED WORLD
Episode 12 - Nobody Ever Found One In Rome

How this film was made
Last updated 24 August 2026


WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Every episode of this channel goes through a fact-check before a single word
is recorded. That check regularly changes the film. This file lists what it
changed here, because a channel that argues with other people's captions has
no business hiding its own corrections.

Ten corrections were made to the script of this episode. One of them removed
a number that had been invented outright, and two removed contradictions the
film would otherwise have carried into the edit.


THE THREE THAT MATTERED

1. A DISTANCE THAT NOBODY HAD MEASURED.

The first draft opened with the Norton Disney site "half a mile from the
Roman road". No source says that. The figure came from nowhere - it simply
sounded like the kind of thing such a sentence contains. The published report
on the fortified villa at Potter Hill puts the site some 200 yards east of
the Fosse Way, and that is what the film now says.

This is the correction worth being most uncomfortable about. The other nine
were errors of reading. This one was an invention, in the first thirty
seconds, in a film whose entire argument is that unrecorded detail is how
this subject was ruined.

2. THE FILM CONTRADICTED ITSELF ABOUT WRITING.

An early block said that no dodecahedron carries "a letter, a numeral, or any
mark that means something". Eight minutes later the same film describes the
Geneva object, which has the twelve signs of the zodiac engraved on its twelve
faces. Both statements are sourced; together they are a contradiction.

The distinction the sources actually make is between writing and pictures. No
recorded dodecahedron carries a letter, a numeral, a scale or a graduation -
nothing that can be read as a value. Some carry engraved decoration, and one
carries pictures. The film now says that, and the Geneva block names the
difference out loud: "Pictures, not numbers."

3. THE SLOVAK FRAGMENTS WERE DESCRIBED WRONG.

The first draft had three fragments, all of them carrying engraved concentric
rings. Dragonidesova describes something more particular: three fragments from
probably two objects - two from Hurbanovo, likely from the same piece, which
do carry rings, and one from Chotin, which preserves part of an opening about
1.4 cm across with a single engraved line near its edge. The block was
rewritten to match, and the drawn diagram [73] shows the difference.


THE OTHER SEVEN

4. "None from Spain, none from Greece" was cut back. The primary source
   (Dragonidesova 2025, 30) names Italy, Africa and the former eastern Roman
   provinces. Spain and Greece appear only in secondary accounts. The film now
   says what the primary source says.

5. "Italy has been dug harder than anywhere else on earth for three hundred
   years" was replaced. It is unprovable as stated. The film now gives the
   checkable fact instead: excavation at Herculaneum began in 1738.

6. The alloy figures for the Norton Disney object DIFFER BETWEEN PUBLISHED
   ACCOUNTS. Three sources - the excavating group, Anglia Ruskin University's
   summary of the handheld XRF analysis, and a widely circulated report of it -
   give 75 per cent copper, 7 per cent tin, 18 per cent lead. Current
   Archaeology gives 63 per cent copper and 27 per cent lead. The film uses the
   majority figure and says only "roughly seven per cent tin and eighteen per
   cent lead". We have not been able to reconcile the two, and the disagreement
   is recorded here rather than hidden behind a single confident number.

7. The order of the object's public display was overstated. The first draft
   said "Lincoln, then Newark, then Nottingham". After Nottingham (to 18
   January 2026) it returned to Newark. The film now names the three places
   without claiming a sequence.

8. The count of known objects streams between 100, 116, 130 and 134 depending
   on which account you read. The film gives one number with its source: about
   134, after Guggenberger and Leach 2025.

9. The material of the Geneva dodecahedron is given as lead in one account and
   as silver in another. The film does not name the material at all - only the
   zodiac signs, which both accounts agree on.

10. A block about the growth of the corpus was added rather than corrected,
    because the fact-check turned up something the first draft had missed:
    Saint-Venant listed 41 examples in 1907, Thompson was working with about
    fifty in 1970, and the running total is now about 134. The corpus grew;
    the quality of the evidence did not.


ONE SOURCE WE COULD NOT READ

F. H. Thompson's 1970 note "Dodecahedrons again" (The Antiquaries Journal
50(1), 93-96) is the refutation on which act 4 of this film rests. Its full
text sits behind a paywall and WAS NOT READ for this film.

What we have instead: two independent accounts of Thompson's argument, which
agree on its substance - that the objects are too variable and too poorly
standardised for a measuring instrument, that there are no graduations and no
numerals, and that no tripod or mount has ever been found with one - and
Guggenberger and Leach's own restatement in 2025 that his conclusion still
holds. The bibliographic details, including the fact that the 1739 Aston find
is now lost, were confirmed against the journal's own listing.

That is good enough to report the argument. It is not the same as having read
it, and the difference is stated here rather than glossed over.


WHAT THE FILM DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO

It does not solve the dodecahedron. There are around fifty readings, one of
them published nine months before this film, and the film's position is that
none of them is settled - including the most recent, whose own authors write
that their case, "whilst not yet proven, seems plausible". That sentence is
quoted in the film because it is the honest one.

It does not treat the people who proposed the failed readings as fools. The
rangefinder idea was a serious proposal, refuted by argument in 1970. The
knitting demonstration genuinely works. The film says why each fails; it does
not sneer.

And it does not claim the distribution proves anything about function. The
map is the strongest evidence in the subject, and what it establishes is
where these objects are and are not - not what they were for.
