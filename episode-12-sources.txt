WHY THIS LIST EXISTS

This channel makes claims about the past using images that were generated by
a machine. That only works if you can check the claims. Everything the film
states is listed below with its source, and everything the film could not
verify was cut - the companion file episode-12-method.txt sets out the ten
corrections made before recording, including one number that had been
invented outright.


THE PUBLICATIONS THIS EPISODE RESTS ON

Guggenberger, M. and Leach, S. (2025). The Gallo-Roman dodecahedron and the
  receptacle of all becoming. The Antiquaries Journal 105, 31-54. Published
  online 17 November 2025.
  https://doi.org/10.1017/S000358152510036X
  -- Source of the running total of about 134 objects, of the distribution
  shares (roughly 70 per cent from the Gallic and Germanic provinces, about
  20 per cent from Britannia, around 90 per cent from the ground of the
  Gallic Empire), and of the breakdown of find contexts. Also the source of
  the Platonic-Pythagorean reading the film presents in act 6 - including
  the authors' own reservation, which the film quotes: that a
  Pythagorean-druidic aspect, "whilst not yet proven, seems plausible".

Dragonidesova, P. (2025). Mysterium Insolutum. Fragments of Roman
  dodecahedrons from Hurbanovo and Chotin. Studijne zvesti Archeologickeho
  ustavu SAV - Supplementum 3, 29-36.
  https://doi.org/10.31577/szausav.2025.suppl.3.2
  -- Read in full. Source of the three fragments from beyond the frontier and
  their reading as raw metal; of the object's description (twelve pentagonal
  faces, thirty equal edges, twenty knobbed corners, openings from 0.6 to 4.0
  cm, diameters 4-11 cm, weights 35-580 g with one outlier at 1,000 g); of
  the absence of any inscription; of the lost-wax casting and the deliberately
  unfinished interior; of the 1739 minute and its wording; and - the finding
  the whole film is built on - that most of the corpus reached collections
  through the antiquities market and carries no documented findspot.

Thompson, F. H. (1970). Dodecahedrons again. The Antiquaries Journal 50(1),
  93-96.
  -- The refutation of the rangefinder reading proposed by Kurzweil (1957)
  and developed by Mauel (1961), and the record that the 1739 Aston find is
  now lost. NOTE: the full text sits behind a paywall and was NOT read for
  this film. Thompson's argument is reported here from two independent
  accounts of it, and Guggenberger and Leach restate his conclusion in 2025.
  This is set out in the method file.

Artmann, B. (1996). A Roman icosahedron discovered. American Mathematical
  Monthly 103(2), 132-133.
  -- The twenty-sided companion object from near Arloff, and the detail the
  film uses: that it sat in a museum store in Bonn, shelved with the
  dodecahedra, because nobody had counted its faces.

Vujovic, M. B. (2021). Fragment of Roman dodecahedron from Deonica (Serbia).
  Instrumentum 54, 37-40.
  -- The south-easternmost recorded find.

Pirling, R. and Siepen, M. (2000). Das roemisch-fraenkische Graeberfeld von
  Krefeld-Gellep 1983-1988. Stuttgart.
  -- Grave 3040: a richly furnished fourth-century burial, with the
  dodecahedron beside a bone object shaped like a handle. The bone was too
  badly decayed to lift whole. This single find is the entire basis for the
  sceptre reading.

Tipper, S. (2024). Beautifully crafted Roman dodecahedron discovered in
  Lincoln - but what were they for? The Conversation, 9 May 2024.
  -- The Norton Disney object: about 8 cm, 245 g, undamaged and without any
  trace of wear; the 33rd from Britain and the first from the Midlands; the
  alloy figures from the handheld XRF analysis. The reported alloy figures
  differ between accounts - see the method file.

Brundle, L. (2024). LIN-BC9890: A Roman dodecahedron. Portable Antiquities
  Scheme database record, cited in Dragonidesova 2025.
  -- The excavated context: a Roman sand-quarrying pit reused for refuse,
  with roof tile, pottery and animal teeth in the fill.

Older literature cited through the two 2025 papers, not consulted directly:
  Saint-Venant, J. de (1907), the first monograph, listing 41 examples;
  Nouwen, R. (1993); Greiner, B. A. (1996), for the unbalanced-die argument;
  Hill (1994), for the wax traces; Gruell, T. (2016), for the ranges and the
  survey of find contexts; Guggenberger, M. (2013), for the counts of
  researchers and readings; Kolnik, T. (1979), for the Brigetio piece;
  Danova, M. (2021), for the destruction of the fort at Iza in AD 179.

Plato, Timaeus 55c, and Iamblichus, On the Pythagorean Life, are cited as the
  ancient texts on which Guggenberger and Leach build their reading.

WHAT THE IMAGES ARE NOT

None of these images is a photograph of a real dodecahedron. Every one of them
was generated or drawn for this film, and they stand for the objects the film
describes rather than depicting particular ones. Where a specific find is named
in the commentary - Norton Disney, Krefeld-Gellep, Geneva, the Slovak fragments
- the image beside it is a stand-in, not that object.

NINETEEN of the 84 shots are drawn rather than generated, and the reason is
worth stating plainly. Image models do not reliably produce a regular
dodecahedron: they will hand you ten faces, or twelve identical openings, or a
polished ornament. Wherever this film explains the geometry, the measurements,
the distribution or a number, the picture is drawn in Python from the true
coordinates of the solid - twelve pentagonal faces, thirty edges of equal
length, twenty corners. What you see in those shots is a dodecahedron, not a
picture of one. In the generated shots the object appears only where an
imprecision costs nothing: in a hand, part-buried, in half-light, behind glass.

Two of the drawn shots are diagrams of a documented range rather than
measurements of a particular object: [42], the spread of opening diameters,
and [47], why an unbalanced object comes to rest on its smallest opening. The
captions in those images say so.

The distribution map shows a SELECTION of documented findspots. It is not a
complete find map, and it cannot be: most of the corpus has no findspot at
all. It shows no routes and no trade.

Every generated image carries a permanent AI label, and the official EU
marking is shown in full between 0:03 and 0:15. The drawn diagrams carry no AI
label, because they are not AI images.
