Episode 6 - We Can Read Every Sign And Understand Nothing

How this film was made

Last updated 11 August 2026


WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS
====================

Every episode of this channel gets one of these. It says how the film was
built, what was changed during fact-checking, and where the evidence runs out.
It exists because a channel that asks you to trust a figure owes you the
working behind it.


THE ORDER OF WORK
=================

  1. Topic. One find, one open question, and something that can be stated as a
     number.
  2. Script, written in full before anything is recorded.
  3. FACT-CHECK GATE. Every figure, name, date and interpretation is checked
     against specialist sources. Nothing is recorded until this is done. What
     changed at this stage is listed below, in full.
  4. Images. Every shot is made for the film - none is a photograph of a real
     object. See the note on the signs, below, which matters more in this
     episode than in any before it.
  5. Diagrams. Maps, timelines and tables are drawn in code, not generated,
     because image models set labels unreliably.
  6. Narration, music, sound, edit.
  7. A machine check of the finished file before release: labelling windows,
     image order, loudness, and the level of every sound effect relative to
     speech.


THE SIGNS IN THE IMAGES ARE NOT LINEAR A
========================================

This is the one thing worth reading if you read nothing else here.

An image model cannot write Linear A. It can only produce marks that resemble
it. Every tablet, fragment and libation table in this film is a reconstruction,
and the marks on them are plausible linear strokes - not readable text, not a
transcription of any real inscription, and of no use for study.

Three consequences follow, and all three were applied:

  - No generated image in the film shows an inscription large enough or sharp
    enough to be read as a facsimile.
  - Everything the film actually needs you to read is DRAWN in code, not
    generated: the number system, the account with ku-ro, the libation formula,
    and Ventris's grid. Those carry the Latin transcription used in the
    scholarly literature - which is, after all, exactly what the film is about.
    What survives of Linear A, for practical purposes, IS the transcription.
  - Every image carries a permanent AI RECONSTRUCTION label in the bottom left,
    and an EU AI-generated content mark appears in the first fifteen seconds.

While the images were being checked, four came back wrong in ways worth naming,
because they show what these tools do when left alone: two tablets came back in
CUNEIFORM despite an explicit exclusion; a Minoan storeroom came back as a
Mesopotamian brick vault; a tablet came back as a terracotta roof tile; and a
1900 excavation came back as a clean vector cartoon. All four were remade. The
exclusions that fixed them are now part of the channel's standing setup.


WHAT THE FACT-CHECK CHANGED
===========================

Eight items were cut or qualified before recording. In detail:

  1. THE SIZE OF THE CORPUS. Sources give 7,362, 7,396 and "about 7,400" signs,
     depending on how damaged signs are counted. Rather than pick one, the film
     says "a little over seven thousand". The thumbnail says 7,000 - a figure
     that is true on every count. A round number that is certainly true beats a
     precise one that is arguable.

  2. TABLET COUNTS AGAINST OBJECT COUNTS. These are mixed up constantly, often
     in the same paragraph: Hagia Triada is given as both 147 tablets and 1,269
     inscribed objects. The film uses tablet counts only, and says so on screen.

  3. THE FRACTION SIGNS. There are seventeen of them, and that is solid. Their
     individual values are not: several are still disputed. The film gives the
     count and no values.

  4. THE MEANING OF THE LIBATION FORMULA. (J)A-SA-SA-RA-ME has been read as a
     divine name, as the vessel itself, and as "this dedication". None of these
     is established. The film says only that the formula recurs - which is what
     the argument about word order actually needs.

  5. NAMED AUTHORS OF REJECTED DECIPHERMENTS. Attempts in Indo-Iranian,
     Semitic, Anatolian, Etruscan and Kartvelian directions are all real, and
     they are listed in the source list. The narration names nobody. The film's
     point is about a statistical pattern - with around ninety frequently used
     signs and short words, some plausible matches will appear in almost any
     language - and that point does not need a target.

  6. AI DECIPHERMENT CLAIMS OF 2026. Several are circulating. None has appeared
     in a peer-reviewed journal, and none has been shown to keep working on
     inscriptions it was not built from. Repeating an unverifiable claim in
     order to knock it down is still repeating it, so the film does something
     more useful instead: it states the test. A decipherment has to keep
     producing sense on material it did not use. That is not a high bar
     invented by specialists to keep outsiders out - it is the bar Ventris
     cleared in public in 1953, with a tablet he had never seen.

  7. "67% OF WORDS". The 2019 MIT/Google paper reports 67.3% of correctly
     translated COGNATES on Linear B. The draft said "words", which overstates
     it. Corrected before recording.

  8. LINEAR A AT DRAMA, BULGARIA. Reported, but the attribution is not
     consistent across sources. Cut from the list of find spots. Thera, Kea,
     Melos, Kythera, mainland Greece, Miletus and Tel Haror all stayed.

Two further items were qualified rather than cut. Ventris's question to Evans
in 1936 is widely repeated but has no contemporary source, so the film marks it
as a story rather than quoting it. And "Minoan" is stated once, explicitly, as
a label invented by an English archaeologist in 1900 - we do not know what
these people called themselves, for the reason the whole film is about.


THE TITLE
=========

The episode plan called this one "A Script We Still Cannot Read". That title
was dropped because the film disproves it. We can read Linear A. The sound
values come from Linear B, transcription is routine, the number system is
completely understood, and ku-ro and ki-ro were recovered from the arithmetic.
What is missing is the language underneath, not the script.

This is the third time on this channel that a planned title has failed the
same way, and the rule has hardened: if the film has to take the title back,
the title is wrong, however well it would perform.


WHERE THE EVIDENCE ENDS
=======================

The film ends on an image - a name cut into a stone in a museum case, which we
can pronounce and cannot attribute. That is a real situation and not a
flourish: the libation tables carry a slot that changes from stone to stone,
and the best available argument says that slot is the dedicator's name.
"Best available argument" is doing real work in that sentence. It is a proposal
by one linguist, resting on where the variation sits, and it may not hold.

Corrections are welcome and will be made out loud.
