WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS
====================

This channel makes claims about the past using images that are not photographs
of the past. That is a fair thing to be suspicious of. This page says exactly
how the film was put together, what was changed during the fact check, and what
the film is not able to tell you.


THE ONE THAT CHANGED THE TITLE
==============================

This episode was planned under a different title: "The City Carved 18 Floors
Down", with "18 FLOORS UNDERGROUND" on the thumbnail. Both were dropped during
the fact check.

The figure of eighteen levels appears in almost every popular account of
Derinkuyu. Following it back does not lead to a survey. It leads to travel
writing, and from there to everyone who repeated it. The Italian survey
campaigns of the 1990s distinguish five overlapping levels; around eight are
open to visitors today; published depths range from about 45 metres to 85.

A number that the sources do not agree on gets cut. That rule exists because it
is easy to keep the one figure that makes the better thumbnail. Here it would
have cost the title, so the title changed instead.

The film says the popular figures out loud, in the first minute, and then says
they are not measurements. That turned out to be a better opening than the
number ever was.


SIX MORE CORRECTIONS
====================

1. THE CHICKENS. The 1963 discovery is usually told with a detail: the man's
   chickens kept disappearing through a crack in his cellar wall, which is why
   he broke it open. It is charming and it is everywhere. It traces to
   journalism, not to any excavation report. The film keeps the sledgehammer
   and the wall and drops the chickens.

2. XENOPHON. The Anabasis, written around 370 BCE, describes people living in
   houses cut down into the ground with their animals. It is cited constantly as
   the earliest written notice of the Cappadocian underground cities. The
   passage belongs to the march through Armenia, several hundred kilometres
   east. The film includes it - and says what is wrong with using it.

3. THE ANCIENT NAME. A name for Derinkuyu in antiquity circulates widely. It
   could not be traced to any scholarly source, only to travel writing and its
   copies. What is documented is Malakopea, the name used by the town's
   Greek-speaking inhabitants into the twentieth century. That is the name the
   film uses.

4. THE TUNNEL TO KAYMAKLI. Often given as nine kilometres and sometimes as
   fact. No survey supports it. It is not in the film, and it is not on the map
   either - a map is a claim like any other sentence.

5. FIFTY-TWO VENTILATION SHAFTS. Travel literature only. The film describes
   what the shafts do and does not count them.

6. SEVENTEEN THOUSAND LITRES OF WINE A YEAR. A real published figure, but an
   extrapolation from the number of pressing installations rather than a
   measurement. The film gives the counts, which are measurements, and leaves
   the litres out.


WHAT THE IMAGES ARE
===================

Every one of the 56 shots was made for this film.

48 are AI-generated (Higgsfield, nano_banana_pro), marked "AI RECONSTRUCTION"
on screen for as long as they are visible, alongside the EU marking for
AI-generated content in the first fifteen seconds. They fall into two kinds and
the film never mixes them: photographic-looking shots show place and find;
painted shots show people, actions and anything reconstructed - because those
are interpretation, and they should look like it.

8 are drawn, not generated: the map, the two architecture panels, the two
stratigraphy panels, the timeline of one complex, the timeline of datings, the
survey bar and the economy panel. Image models place lettering unreliably, and
a diagram whose labels are decorative is worse than no diagram. One attempt at
a painted Anatolian chamber came back with Egyptian hieroglyphs on the wall -
invented writing that would have looked like information.

No photographs of Derinkuyu, of any other Cappadocian site, or of any museum
object appear anywhere in the film. In Germany, a photograph of a
public-domain object is itself protected (Section 72 UrhG, confirmed by the
Federal Court of Justice). Rather than borrow what cannot be cleanly licensed,
this channel draws what the published record describes. Nothing in this film
rests on anyone else's rights.


HOW THE FILM WAS ASSEMBLED
==========================

The script is written first and is the only source for everything downstream:
each spoken block becomes one narration file, and the length of each shot is
the MEASURED length of its narration plus a pause. Nothing is cut to a planned
duration.

Narration: ElevenLabs, synthetic voice, the same voice in every episode.
Music: composed for this film, six pieces following the six acts. New music is
composed for every episode.
Sound: field recordings and effects from licensed libraries, each normalised to
a target level before mixing - a fixed mix factor across sources that differ by
40 dB decides at random which effects you can hear at all.
Camera moves over stills are computed sub-pixel, because integer-rounded moves
visibly stutter at slow speeds.

The whole chain is scripted and re-runnable, and every step is checked
mechanically before release: marking windows, image order, sound levels.


WHAT THIS FILM CANNOT TELL YOU
==============================

When these complexes were cut. That is the subject of the film and it is not
resolved at the end, because it is not resolved in the literature. The period
of heavy use is reasonably well argued - the Arab incursions into Byzantine
Anatolia, roughly the seventh to ninth centuries. That is a period of use, not
a construction date, and the difference matters.

Who cut them is much less mysterious than it sounds: the people who lived
directly above them. That part the film does state, because the evidence for it
is good.

If something here is wrong, tell me and I will correct it and say so.
