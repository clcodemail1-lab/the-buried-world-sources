WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every claim that could be checked was checked before the
film was recorded, and the works those checks rest on are named at the end.

This episode is about the limits of dating evidence, so it would be poor form
to be vague about its own. Where a figure is contested, this list says so.

If something here is wrong, tell me and I will correct it and say so.


THE IMAGES
==========

All 56 shots were made for this film. No photographs of Derinkuyu, of any other
Cappadocian site, or of any museum object appear anywhere in it, and no
third-party images are used. Photographs of public-domain objects are still
protected in Germany (Section 72 UrhG); rather than borrow what cannot be
licensed, this channel draws what the published record describes.

Two kinds of images appear, and the film keeps them apart on purpose:

  RECONSTRUCTION - AI-generated, marked "AI RECONSTRUCTION" on screen for as
                   long as it is visible, alongside the EU marking for
                   AI-generated content. Photographic-looking shots show place
                   and find; painted shots show people, actions and
                   reconstructions, which are always interpretation.

  DIAGRAM        - the map, the two architecture panels, the two stratigraphy
                   panels, the life-of-a-complex timeline, the datings
                   timeline, the survey bar and the economy panel. These were

DIAGRAMS - WHAT THEY REST ON
============================

The map at 1:41 uses real coordinates, linearly projected, with the
latitude axis stretched by 1/cos(38.4 deg); no coastlines are drawn, and no
distance is given. In particular it does NOT show a tunnel between Derinkuyu
and Kaymakli: that connection is widely repeated and has never been surveyed.

The two stratigraphy panels at 4:41 and 5:19 share one frame and one
scale on purpose, so that the second can be read against the first.

The datings timeline at 9:31 shows evidence strength as filled squares:
excavated at the site / inferred from the region / none. Xenophon is greyed
out - the passage is real, the location is not Cappadocia.


THE NUMBERS YOU WILL SEE ELSEWHERE
==================================

Almost every popular account gives Derinkuyu as eighteen levels, eighty-five
metres deep, with room for twenty thousand people. The film quotes those
figures as what is said, not as what is known. For the record:

  Levels    - no survey has published eighteen. The Italian survey work
              distinguishes five overlapping levels; around eight are open to
              visitors. "Eighteen" traces to travel writing.
  Depth     - published figures range from about 45 m to 60 m to 85 m.
  Capacity  - twenty thousand is an estimate of capacity, not a count, and not
              a claim that anyone lived there permanently.
  Name      - the "ancient name" often given circulates from travel writing.
              What is documented is Malakopea, the name its Greek-speaking
              inhabitants used into the twentieth century.

None of this makes the site less remarkable. It makes the popular figures
unusable, which is a different thing.


WHAT WAS CUT AND WHY
====================

Six things were removed from the script at the fact-check stage:

  1. "Eighteen levels" as a statement of fact - see above. It also cost the
     film its planned title.
  2. The 1963 discovery story in its usual form - the man whose chickens
     disappeared through a crack in his cellar wall. It is charming, it is
     everywhere, and it traces to journalism rather than to any excavation
     report. The film keeps the sledgehammer and the wall, which are not in
     dispute, and drops the chickens.
  3. Xenophon as evidence for Cappadocia. The passage (Anabasis IV) describes
     underground houses on the march through Armenia, several hundred
     kilometres east. The film says so instead of quietly dropping it.
  4. The tunnel to Kaymakli.
  5. The count of 52 ventilation shafts - travel literature only.
  6. A wine-production figure in litres per year, because it is an
     extrapolation rather than a measurement.

SOURCES AND FURTHER READING
===========================

Guenay, Goerkem (2024): "Byzantine Rock-Cut Architecture in Cappadocia and
  Beyond: The State of Scholarship", Valonia: A Journal of Anatolian Pasts 1,
  35-63. DOI 10.5505/valonia.2024.43531. - The backbone of this film. More
  than forty underground complexes known; "their dating and historical context
  remain rather obscure"; usually associated with the Arab incursions of the
  seventh to ninth centuries; used as temporary shelters by the people of the
  settlements above. Also the methodological core: carved sites subtract
  material where open sites add it.

Ousterhout, Robert (2017): Visualizing Community: Art, Material Culture, and
  Settlement in Byzantine Cappadocia, Dumbarton Oaks. - Rock-cut spaces do not
  provide actual stratigraphy but post-occupation accumulations; Cappadocia as
  a living, populated landscape rather than a monastic one.

Ousterhout, Robert (2005): A Byzantine Settlement in Cappadocia, Dumbarton
  Oaks. - The Canli Kilise survey: of 23 carved complexes, one monastic, the
  rest residences of people of high standing, tenth and eleventh centuries.

Oeztuerk, Fatma Guel (2017): "Rock-Cut Architecture", in Philipp Niewoehner
  (ed.), The Archaeology of Byzantine Anatolia, Oxford University Press. - In
  the absence of datable inscriptions, chronology rests on stylistic
  comparison, and none of it gives firm results.

Kalas, Veronica (2006/2007): on Selime-Yaprakhisar - the principal complex read
  as the domestic residence of a local warlord, not a monastery.

Fauvelle-Aymar, F.-X. et al. (2010): "Rock-cut stratigraphy: sequencing the
  Lalibela churches", Antiquity 84/326. - Carving phases can be sequenced; it
  was not possible to give precise dates with the material at hand.
    doi:10.1017/s0003598x00067132

Lamesa, Anais (2020): "Methods for Rock-Hewn Worksite Analysis. The Church of
  Goereme No 4b (Cappadocia): A Case Study", Anatolia Antiqua 28, 23-45. -
  Reading tool marks as stratigraphic units.

Bixio, Roberto (ed.) (2012): Cappadocia: Records of the Underground Sites,
  BAR International Series, Archaeopress. - The Centro Studi Sotterranei survey
  campaigns, 1991-2000; the systematic record of the underground sites.

Kostof, Spiro (1972): Caves of God: The Monastic Environment of Byzantine
  Cappadocia, MIT Press. - Cited in the film as the reading that was later
  revised, not as a current view.

UNESCO World Heritage List no. 357, "Goereme National Park and the Rock Sites
  of Cappadocia", inscribed 1985, with Derinkuyu and Kaymakli among its
  components.


WHAT THE FILM DOES NOT CLAIM
============================

The title says nobody can date it, and the film means that literally and
narrowly: no firm construction date can be established for these complexes with
current methods, for a reason that is methodological rather than mysterious.
It does not claim the site is older than anyone thinks. It does not claim it is
younger. It does not claim a lost civilisation, and it does not entertain one.

Who cut these chambers is not actually in doubt - the people who lived directly
above them, in a landscape whose economy is well documented. What is missing is
a date, and that is worth saying plainly rather than filling with a guess.

"We don't know" is a finding, not a failure.
