THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 11 - Nothing Of It Survived. They Found It Anyway.
Full source list

Four papers with one shape in common: the object under discussion no longer
exists, and it is established anyway. A bed, a house, a piece of cloth, and a
courtly practice. In every case the proof is a by-product - the seeds that
travelled in the stuffing, the plaster that was pressed against the timber, the
crushed stone that fixed a colour, the bones of what a hawk was fed.

Two are in the Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports, one in Antiquity,
one in the International Journal of Osteoarchaeology.

WHAT THIS LIST HAS TO SAY ABOUT ITSELF, AND IT IS NOT THE SAME FOR ALL FOUR.
One paper - the Czarnowko cemetery, published open access in Antiquity - was
read IN FULL. The other three were not reachable: ScienceDirect and Wiley both
refuse automated requests, and the same refusal came back through a browser.
For those three, every figure quoted in the film comes from the authors' own
abstract and nothing else.

That difference is audible in the film, and it should be. Three things in the
first section exist nowhere in that paper's abstract: that not one of the
twenty-three samples contained moss although one deposit had been catalogued
as a moss pillow for years; that the cattail fluff is essentially gone because
each hair is about seventeen microns thick and shatters into single cells; and
that the circular impression in one deposit is thirty-two millimetres across
and matches the foot of a silver cup from the same grave exactly. Nothing of
that kind was available for the other three cases, so those sections stay
closer to the surface. Where a number would have needed a full text - how many
daub fragments were analysed, how much crushed stone was recovered, how many
bird bones the six per cent represents - the film says nothing rather than
estimating.

A NOTE ON DATES. Two of these records carry a real online publication date
(11 September and 26 August 2026). The two Elsevier records carry only
`published-print` - November 2026 - which is an issue label assigned months in
advance, not an event; their Crossref entries were created on 31 August and
10 September 2026, which for this publisher sits at or very near the day the
article went online. Because the four are not comparable in this respect, the
film names no month at all. A previous dispatch read that label as a date and
had to be re-cut.

Every DOI below was checked against the Crossref record - title, journal,
volume, pages and author list, not merely clicked. That check is not decorum:
in an earlier dispatch a DOI that resolved perfectly turned out to belong to a
different paper.

ONE THING THIS FILM DOES NOT SHOW: human remains. Three of the four cases
involve graves or cemeteries, and there is no body, no bone and no burial scene
in any shot. What is shown is the empty cut, the tray, the section, the bench.
The reason is not squeamishness - it is that the findings here are about
materials, and a picture of a burial would carry a claim none of these papers
make.

THE PAPERS

ITEM 1 - THE BEDDING THAT ROTTED, AND THE SEEDS THAT DID NOT
Skora, K., Mueller-Bieniek, A., Schuster, J. & Antosik, L. (2026).
"Pillows, mattresses or log coffin linings: use of cattail (Typha sp.) in
Roman period funerary rituals at Czarnowko in Pomerania."
Antiquity, pages 1-15.
https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10419
Open access, CC BY 4.0. Published online 11 September 2026.
READ IN FULL - the only one of the four.

  Twenty-three samples of compacted organic material from four inhumation
  graves at the Wielbark culture cemetery of Czarnowko in northern Poland;
  thirteen still held identifiable plant remains, and every one of those
  thirteen contained cattail seeds. Not one contained moss, although one of
  the deposits had been catalogued as a "moss pillow". Three samples also
  held woven wool - one running sixteen to eighteen threads per centimetre
  in the warp and twelve in the weft, spun from core-free fleece. One
  fragment carries a circular impression 32 mm across that matches the foot
  of a silver cup from the same grave. The graves date to the first two
  centuries AD; the cemetery holds over 2,000 and was in use from the
  mid-second century BC to the first half of the third century AD. The
  authors attribute preservation to contact with metal: salts migrating out
  of bronze and iron conserved what touched them.

  What the paper does NOT settle, and the film says so: whether these were
  pillows, mattresses or coffin linings. All three are in its title.


ITEM 2 - THE HOUSE THAT ONLY SURVIVES BECAUSE IT BURNT
Mancini, N., Drunagel, E., Ropke, A. K., Vomberg, F. V., Salata, D., Zerl, T.,
Ebel, F. B., Peto, A., Gomori, A., Felegyhazi, F., Fischl, K. P., Kienlin,
T. L. & Anvari, J. (2026).
"Daub as a socio-environmental archive: studying construction materials to
understand their availability and selection at Bronze Age Borsodivanka,
Hungary."
Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports 75, 105992.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2026.105992
Open access, CC BY 4.0. Crossref entry created 31 August 2026; print issue
labelled November 2026.
ABSTRACT ONLY - ScienceDirect refuses automated access.

  Daub - mud mixed with organic and mineral tempers and packed onto a timber
  frame - becomes durable archaeological material when it is exposed to fire
  and then preserved under favourable conditions. It carries impressions of
  the wood and plant stems that were in contact with it. The authors combine
  macroscopic and micromorphological analysis of daub from the Bronze Age
  tell of Borsodivanka with wood charcoal, plant remains and stratigraphic
  samples from beneath the mound, and read out of it a set of decisions
  about which sediments were available and chosen, how they were processed,
  and how daub and daub-like elements - walls, wall decorations, repeatedly
  heated fragments - were used. They note that the material is usually
  neglected because it is morphologically complex and because reading it
  properly needs several specialists.


ITEM 3 - THE WORKSHOP WITH NO CLOTH IN IT
Thacker, P. T., Pereira, C. M. C. & Amoss, J. T. (2026).
"An archaeology of natural dyes and mordants: bridging gaps in Iron Age
textile chaines operatoires."
Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports 75, 106042.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2026.106042
CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. Crossref entry created 10 September 2026; print issue
labelled November 2026.
ABSTRACT ONLY.

  An Iron Age fireplace and an adjacent pit at Castro de Sao Martinho in
  central Portugal produced spindle whorls, seed capsules and flower calyces
  of a wild rock-rose, and crushed magnetite-rich dolerite - but no fibre
  artefacts at all. The authors show in controlled laboratory trials, by
  sequential iron speciation and spectroscopic identification, that iron
  from that dolerite can be converted into iron acetate, a mordant known
  from Bronze and Iron Age Europe. Actualistic experiments using the same
  species and the same plant tissues as the macrobotanical remains from the
  pit yielded a colourfast deep olive green on linen. Their conclusion is
  that the association of hearth, spindle whorls, dye plants and crushed
  dolerite is coherently explained by small-scale or household dyeing.


ITEM 4 - THE PASTIME THAT NOBODY WROTE DOWN
Zinoviev, A. V. (2026).
"Falconry in Medieval Tver (Russia): Osteoarchaeological and Historical
Accounts."
International Journal of Osteoarchaeology, article oa.70163.
https://doi.org/10.1002/oa.70163
Subscription. Published online 26 August 2026.
ABSTRACT ONLY - Wiley refuses automated access.

  Excavations in the kremlin of the medieval Principality of Tver (12th-15th
  centuries) produced gyrfalcons and goshawks, decoy species (buzzards, owls
  and corvids), provisioning pigeons, and game birds (capercaillie, black
  grouse, hazel grouse). Birds of prey make up 6.0 per cent of the kremlin
  bird assemblage - markedly elevated, spatially confined to elite areas and
  almost absent outside the walls. No written source documents falconry at
  Tver; the evidence rests on the zooarchaeological signature alone. The
  paper reports this as the first zooarchaeological evidence of falconry
  from any medieval Russian principality, and sets out diagnostic criteria -
  female-biased raptors, jesses, training corvids, provisioning pigeons -
  for other sites that lack written documentation.

WHAT IS DRAWN AND WHAT IS GENERATED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Twenty of the fifty-four shots are diagrams drawn in code. They carry no AI
label because nothing about them is generated, and that is also why their
labelling is correct - a picture model cannot be trusted with a number.

The diagrams state their own limits on the picture: all four maps say
"schematic - position approximate, no coastline drawn"; the raptor chart says
its bar heights are not to scale and that no figure is given for outside the
walls; the preservation diagram says the extent of the effect is not stated in
the paper.

Every photographic-looking shot is generated and none depicts a real,
identifiable find. No third-party images or footage are used, so no third-party
rights apply.
