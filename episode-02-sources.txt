WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list

was recorded, and the works those checks rest on are named at the end.

THE IMAGES
==========

All 50 shots were made for this film. No photographs of the Antikythera
mechanism, of the wreck, or of any museum object appear anywhere in it, and no
third-party images are used. Photographs of the fragments are under copyright;
rather than borrow what cannot be licensed, this channel draws what the
published record describes.

Two kinds of images appear, and the film keeps them apart on purpose:

  RECONSTRUCTION - AI-generated, marked "AI RECONSTRUCTION" on screen for as
                   long as it is visible, alongside the EU marking for
                   AI-generated content. Photographic-looking shots show place
                   and find; painted shots show people, actions and
                   reconstructions, which are always interpretation.

  DIAGRAM        - the map, the timeline, the machine diagram, the two-readings
                   panel and the survival panel. These were drawn for this
                   film, not generated. Image models place lettering
                   unreliably - an early attempt put Roman numerals on the
                   dial of a Greek machine.

DIAGRAMS - WHAT THEY REST ON
============================

The map at 1:24 uses real coordinates, linearly projected; no coastlines are
drawn - points and the shipping route only, so the map claims only what is
established. The timeline at 3:11 is linear on purpose: the gap is the point.
The mechanism appears on it as a BAND, c. 205-100 BCE, because the date itself
is argued (see below).

Dates: wreck c. 60 BCE · letter forms 150-100 BCE · Saros-dial epoch 205 BCE
(Carman & Evans) · Byzantine geared sundial c. 500 CE · Islamic geared
calendars c. 1000 CE · Wallingford c. 1330 · de' Dondi 1364.

SOURCES AND FURTHER READING
===========================

Price, D. de Solla (1974): "Gears from the Greeks: The Antikythera Mechanism -
  A Calendar Computer from ca. 80 B.C.", Transactions of the American
  Philosophical Society 64/7. - The first full gear-work reconstruction, from
  X-rays; the 127-tooth gear and the Metonic arithmetic.

Freeth, T., Bitsakis, Y., Moussas, X., Seiradakis, J. H., Tselikas, A., et al.
  (2006): "Decoding the ancient Greek astronomical calculator known as the
  Antikythera Mechanism", Nature 444, 587-591. - The CT campaign: gear count,
  eclipse prediction, more than 3,000 characters of engraved text.
    doi:10.1038/nature05357

Freeth, T., Jones, A., Steele, J. M. & Bitsakis, Y. (2008): "Calendars with
  Olympiad display and eclipse prediction on the Antikythera Mechanism",
  Nature 454, 614-617. - The games dial and the Corinthian month names.
    doi:10.1038/nature07130

Freeth, T., Higgon, D., Dacanalis, A., MacDonald, L., Georgakopoulou, M. &
  Wojcik, A. (2021): "A Model of the Cosmos in the ancient Greek Antikythera
  Mechanism", Scientific Reports 11, 5821. - The 82 fragments, 30 surviving
  gears, and the planetary-display reconstruction the film calls a hypothesis.

Carman, C. C. & Evans, J. (2014): "On the epoch of the Antikythera mechanism
  and its eclipse predictor", Archive for History of Exact Sciences 68,
  693-774. - The 205 BCE Saros epoch and the Babylonian arithmetic.

Cicero, De re publica I - Marcellus and the sphere of Archimedes taken from
  Syracuse; De natura deorum II - the sphere of Posidonius. Both in living
  memory of geared planetaria.

The Antikythera wreck excavations: Return to Antikythera project (from 2012),
  including the 2016 human skeleton find.


WHAT THE FILM DOES NOT CLAIM
============================

The title says "shouldn't exist". The film answers that in the open, at 8:30:
the gap in the record is survival bias, not a miracle. Where the machine was
built, and when exactly, is unresolved - the film gives both readings, at
7:19, and does not choose.

"We don't know" is a finding, not a failure.
