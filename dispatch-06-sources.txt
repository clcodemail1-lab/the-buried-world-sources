WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every figure was checked against the sources named at the
end before a word was recorded.

There is a particular reason this time. This film is about labels quietly
doing the work of evidence, so it states its own: which shots are
reconstructions, which are diagrams drawn from published figures, which
numbers were measured by somebody else, and which of the three papers could
actually be read in full.

No photograph in this film is of a real object or a real place. Every image is
either a reconstruction made for the film and marked AI RECONSTRUCTION on
screen, or a diagram drawn for it. Nothing is a press picture, and no
third-party image was used as a reference.

Two deliberate omissions. No human remains appear anywhere in this film: the
Phaleron burials are shown as an excavation surface and an iron shackle, never
as bodies. And nothing here portrays a specific catalogued find - the vessels,
the cuff and the ridges are reconstructions of a type.

SOURCES
=======

ITEM 1 - FIRED CLAY IN THE PRE-POTTERY NEOLITHIC

  Kodas, E., Petrova, N., Daghmehchi, M. and Ozbal, R. (2026). Understanding
  a first attempt at pottery: early fired containers at Cemka Hoyuk.
  Antiquity 100(412). Published online 15 June 2026, in the August print
  issue.
  doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2026.10371

  ACCESS: open access, CC BY 4.0 - the only one of the three papers this film
  could read in full. Forty-six low-fired fragments, nine vessels, a context
  of about 9350 cal BC, firing between 600 and 700 degrees under low-oxygen
  conditions, and three functional groups. The authors write that the pieces
  "may have been deliberately fired"; the film keeps the "may".

  Kenyon, K. M. (1957). Digging Up Jericho. London.
  For the origin of the terms PPNA and PPNB, coined during the excavations at
  Tell es-Sultan between 1952 and 1958.

  NOTE ON THE DATES IN DIAGRAM 13: the ranges drawn there are calibrated.
  Kenyon's own figures were uncalibrated radiocarbon years and sit several
  centuries younger; mixing the two would have placed Cemka Hoyuk outside the
  very period it belongs to. The diagram says so on screen.


ITEM 2 - THE MEN AT PHALERON

  Stamer, J. R., Rothwell, J. E., Simonton, M., Chryssoulaki, S., Knudson,
  K. J. and Buikstra, J. E. (2026). Investigating the residential history of
  the Esplanada mass graves at Phaleron, Greece. Journal of Archaeological
  Science: Reports 74, 105952.
  doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2026.105952

  ACCESS: paywalled. This film is based on the abstract, on the release from
  Arizona State University, and on reporting that quotes the authors directly.
  Sixty-six of the seventy-nine men were sampled, plus ninety-seven other
  adults from the same burial ground; sixty-four of the sixty-six carry a
  signature compatible with Attica.

  NOTE: at least one report places this study in the Journal of Archaeological
  Science. It is in Journal of Archaeological Science: Reports - a different
  journal from the same publisher. The DOI above is the one to follow.

  Chryssoulaki, S. et al. (2019). Bioarchaeological field analysis of human
  remains from the mass graves at Phaleron, Greece. Hesperia 88(4).
  For the excavation of 2016 by the Ephorate of Antiquities of West Attica,
  Piraeus and the Islands, and for the arrangement of the burials.

  Herodotus 5.71 and Thucydides 1.126 are the ancient notices of the coup of
  Cylon. They are named in the film only as the usual candidate, not as an
  identification, because nothing links the texts to this grave.

WHERE THIS FILM DISAGREES WITH THE REPORTING
============================================

1. "The oldest pottery ever found." It is not. Fired vessels from East Asian
   sites are roughly twice this age. The claim in the paper is regional -
   south-west Asia - and the film says so in the segment itself rather than
   leaving it to the comments.

2. "11,500 years old", and elsewhere "12,000". The publication dates the
   context to about 9350 calibrated BC, which is about 11,300 years. The film
   uses the published date.

3. "Deliberately fired." The paper says "may have been deliberately fired".
   The film keeps the hedge, gives the reasons the reading is strong, and
   gives the reason it is still a hedge: an ordinary hearth reaches the same
   temperature.

4. "Archaeologists thought these men were foreign invaders. They were wrong."
   And, from another outlet, "study confirms". Neither is right. Two families
   of explanation have stood side by side since 2016 - an internal Athenian
   killing, with the followers of Cylon as the usual candidates, or captives
   from outside. There was no consensus to overturn or to confirm. The study
   decides between them.

5. How many of the seventy-nine were shackled. Reported as thirty-six, as
   thirty-nine, as "many" and as "all". The accessible text of the study gives
   no figure, so the film gives none either.

6. "The whole system was built in a single dry season." The paper models a
   block of five hundred hectares. Five hundred thousand is the whole complex,
   built and used over more than a thousand years. The film separates the two
   and says the difference out loud.


WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
===================

  That anyone was careless. A period name is a filing decision, not an
  assertion; Kenyon was sorting layers. The chains at Phaleron were read the
  way anybody reads chains. Wittfogel published an argument and lost it.

  That strontium proves where a person came from. It rules places out. Several
  unrelated regions can share a signature, and the method says nothing about
  cause of death, faction or year.

  That the Colombian figures are an excavation result. They are a model built
  from mapped volumes and stated assumptions, including wooden tools that do
  not survive in that ground.

  That the three new readings are safe. "Experimental pottery", "local" and
  "collective labour" are labels too, and one of them will not survive the
  decade. That is the work, not an objection to it.
