THE BURIED WORLD
Episode 18 - The Devil's Arrows
How this film was made, and what the fact check changed


WHY THIS PAPER

Three standing stones in a field at Boroughbridge, Yorkshire, each around
twenty-five tonnes, the tallest stone row in Britain - and, for as long as the
question has been asked, a source thirteen kilometres away that nobody had
tested. In September 2026 a geochemist and an archaeologist published the
test: adhesive tape pressed to the stones, mineral grains from the dust, and
uranium-lead ages for two hundred and thirty-six grains of zircon. The grains
say the stones came from Brimham Rocks, eighteen kilometres to the west, past
the nearer source. The film is about how that is measured, what it answers
(where from) and what it cannot (when, how, by whom).


WHAT WAS READ

The paper (Clarke, Leary and Kirkland 2026, Proceedings of the Royal Society
A) is open access under CC BY 4.0 and was read in full, including the
methods, the two tables of statistics, the discussion of glacial transport
and the authors' own caveats. The press releases of the University of York
and Curtin University were read for their wording, and the film says where
it differs from the paper. Three details come from reference works and are
marked as such below: the legend of the Devil's arrows, the size of the
Thornborough henges, and the road.


WHAT THE FACT CHECK CHANGED

1. THE LASER SPOT LOST ITS SIZE. The draft said the laser burns a pit "a few
   thousandths of a millimetre across". The paper does not give the spot
   size, so the film says "a tiny pit".

2. THE YEAR OF THE SAMPLING WAS REMOVED. The draft said "one day in 2024".
   The paper gives the date of the consent (23 October 2024), not the day
   of the sampling. The film says "one day not long ago".

3. "TWO KILOMETRES" BECAME "A SHORT WALK". The distance from the stones to
   the Battle Cross in Aldborough was our estimate, not a figure in the
   paper.

4. NOTHING IN THE FILM SAYS HOW THE STONES WERE MOVED. No sledges, no
   rivers, no number of people, no duration. The paper gives none, and
   says why: nothing has been found that could show it.

5. "PROVED" IS NOT USED FOR BRIMHAM. The paper writes "most likely source"
   and "closest correspondence"; the press release of the University of
   York writes "proved". The film quotes both and keeps the authors'
   wording.

WHAT THE FILM SHOWS

No photograph of the Devil's Arrows, of Brimham Rocks or of the laboratory is
used. Every photograph-like shot is generated and labelled on screen; it
shows stones, crags, instruments and landscapes of the TYPE described, not
the real monument. The scenes of antiquaries and of prehistoric people are
painted and labelled. The diagrams are drawn in code from the paper's text
and tables; the age fingerprints in them are schematic, not the data.
