WHY THIS LIST EXISTS
====================

Every image in the film carries a number in the bottom right corner. This list
resolves each one. Every claim that could be checked was checked before the
film was recorded, and the studies and reports those checks rest on are named
at the end.

A news round-up is the format where it is easiest to get carried away, because
each item arrives pre-packaged with a headline. Where this film disagrees with
the headline, it says so on screen. Where a source states a limit, the film
states it too.

If something here is wrong, tell me and I will correct it and say so.


THE IMAGES
==========

All 31 shots were made for this film. No press photographs are used - not of
the altar, not of the Suffolk excavation, not of the bronze hoard. Photographs
of public-domain objects are still protected in Germany (Section 72 UrhG), and
press images from museums and archaeological units are not licensed for this
use. Rather than borrow what cannot be cleanly licensed, this channel draws
what the published record describes.

Two kinds of images appear, and the film keeps them apart on purpose:

  RECONSTRUCTION - AI-generated, marked "AI RECONSTRUCTION" on screen for as
                   long as it is visible, alongside the EU marking for
                   AI-generated content. Photographic-looking shots show place
                   and object; painted shots show people and actions, which
                   are always interpretation.

  DIAGRAM        - the trigger panel, the find-map panel (which appears twice,
                   once as a question and once as an answer), the Budapest
                   gauge, the river section, the Sizewell figures, the six
                   thousand year timeline, the two routes to a trench, the
                   cache-or-deposit panel, the Moravia map, the ninety per
                   cent grid and the closing panel. These were drawn for this
                   film, not generated.

One rule this episode applies more strictly than its predecessors: NO PEOPLE
appear in any photographic-looking shot, including modern archaeologists. An
AI-generated person standing in a real excavation would be a claim about a
real working team. Excavation areas are therefore shown empty - trenches,
sections, ranging rods, tarpaulins. People appear only in painted shots, and
only in the past.

The Moravia map uses real coordinates and shows nothing else: no rivers, no
borders, no distance figures. Two distance figures were cut during the fact
check for exactly that reason - see below.

WHERE THE FILM DISAGREES WITH THE HEADLINES
===========================================

1. "Drought reveals Roman treasures." The drought revealed nothing. It moved
   the edge of where anyone is physically able to look, and objects were
   sitting just past that edge - as they presumably are along the rest of the
   river, still under water. The film makes this the point rather than the
   colour.

2. "Roman altar found at Intercisa." True as far as it goes. What the altar
   belonged to is still open; the museum has not tied it to a building or a
   unit. The film says so and stops there. Intercisa was garrisoned for
   generations by a Syrian archer cohort, which is documented and interesting -
   and which this film deliberately does NOT connect to the altar, because
   without a date for the stone that connection would be invented.

3. "Britain's biggest dig." The people running Sizewell C call it the largest
   and most complex dig of a generation. That is their description of their own
   project, and the film attributes it to them rather than asserting it.

4. "Ritual offering." The Kojetin hoard is read as a deposit rather than a
   cache, because you do not hide valuables in standing water. That reading
   rests on a pattern seen across Bronze Age Europe, not on anything written
   down. The film presents it as the best available reading, which is what the
   excavators do.


TWO NUMBERS CUT DURING THE FACT CHECK
=====================================

Both were distances, and both were wrong in the same way - a plausible figure
attached to the wrong thing.

  "Ninety kilometres downstream." That is the distance to the Paks power
  station, not to Dunaujvaros. The published figures for Dunaujvaros differ
  depending on what is being measured (roughly 60 km straight line, 70 km
  quoted as "south of Budapest", 82 km by road), so the number was dropped
  entirely rather than picked.

  "Forty miles down the same coast" for Sutton Hoo. It is roughly twenty.
  Dropped for the same reason.

This channel drops numbers the sources do not carry uniformly. That rule has
now cost something in every production, which is how you know it is working.


THE NUMBERS THAT STAYED
=======================

  Danube at Budapest, 3 August 2026: around 10 cm, against a previous record
    low of 33 cm set in 2018 (25 October). Gauges move daily and differ by
    station, so here is the whole run rather than one figure: the 2018 record
    fell on 28 July 2026 at 31 cm, the lowest single reading of the drought was
    9 cm on 2 August at the Vigado ter gauge, and by 9 August the river had
    come back up to 28-32 cm. "About ten centimetres" in the film describes the
    floor of that run. It is a dated snapshot, not a standing figure - if you
    are watching this later, the river will be somewhere else.
  Sizewell C: c. 140 hectares stripped across 75 separate areas, four years of
    excavation after seven years of evaluation, three more years of analysis to
    come, around 200 archaeologists.
  Neolithic long enclosure: c. 50 x 20 m, roughly 5,800 years old.
  Theberton: two cemeteries, 12 barrows, 50+ flat graves.
  Kojetin: 8 deliberately bent sickles, 1 whole, part of a sword blade, three
    ingots; Urnfield culture, more than 3,000 years old.
  Around 90 per cent of archaeological fieldwork in Britain is
    development-led. Fulford, 2011 - an estimate, for Britain, not the world.

WHAT THIS FILM DOES NOT CLAIM
=============================

It does not claim that the three finds are connected to one another. They are
not. The only thing they share is how they came to be found.

It does not claim that development-led archaeology is a failure. The opposite:
before the rule that made the builder pay, the same ground was destroyed
without record. The film says so in its own closing section, because a film
that left it out would be an outrage piece.

It does not claim to know what the Intercisa altar belonged to, what the two
inscribed fragments say, or why the Kojetin metalwork was broken.

The closing observation - that a find map is strong evidence of where we have
looked and weak evidence of where people were - is a statement about sampling,
not a claim that the archaeological record is worthless.

"We don't know" is a finding, not a failure.
