WHY THIS LIST EXISTS

This channel makes claims about the past using images that were generated by
a machine. That only works if you can check the claims. Everything the film
states is listed below with its source, and everything the film could not
verify was cut - the companion file episode-09-method.txt sets out the
corrections made before recording, including one that would have made the
film wrong.


THE PUBLICATIONS THIS EPISODE RESTS ON

Pilo, L., Reitmaier, T., Fischer, A., Barrett, J.H. & Nesje, A. (2022).
  Oetzi, 30 years on: A reappraisal of the depositional and post-depositional
  history of the find. The Holocene.
  DOI: 10.1177/09596836221126133
  -- Source of the May/June date for the death; of the radiocarbon span for
     material collecting in the gully (c. 7,300 to c. 4,000 cal BP) and of
     the younger material at 2,737-2,429 cal BP; of the permanent sealing at
     c. 3,800 years ago; of the 25-30 m threshold for basal sliding and the
     under-ten-degree slope; of the 1878 survey giving the ice at the find
     spot below 20 m and probably nearer 5; of the phrase "a non-moving,
     stationary field of snow and ice, frozen to the bedrock"; of the
     conclusion that the find circumstances "are quite normal for glacial
     archaeology"; of the quiver at about 7 m and the birch bark vessel at
     about 6 m from where the bow and axe lay; and of the axe handle 50 m
     south of the find spot dating to about 500 years AFTER the Iceman.

Wang, K. et al. (2023). High-coverage genome of the Tyrolean Iceman reveals
  unusually high Anatolian farmer ancestry. Cell Genomics.
  DOI: 10.1016/j.xgen.2023.100377
  -- Source of the 15-fold coverage with contamination measured and removed;
     of the more-than-92-per-cent early Anatolian farmer ancestry and its
     being the highest among contemporaneous Europeans; of the absence of
     detectable steppe ancestry and its attribution to modern contamination;
     and of the phenotype - high skin pigmentation, dark eyes, male-pattern
     baldness.

Keller, A. et al. (2012). New insights into the Tyrolean Iceman's origin and
  phenotype as inferred by whole-genome sequencing. Nature Communications.
  DOI: 10.1038/ncomms1701
  -- The earlier, low-coverage genome. Source of brown eyes, blood group O
     and lactose intolerance - and of the paper's own statement that coverage
     was low and present-day contamination high.

Deter-Wolf, A., Riday, D., Jacobsen, M.S. et al. (2024). European Journal of
  Archaeology.
  -- The tattooing experiment: eight tools across four techniques on a living
     volunteer, compared with the Iceman's marks. Source of the 1-3 mm width,
     the stippling, the rounded ends and the irregular pigment seepage, and
     of the conclusion that the marks were hand-poked rather than incised.

New insights on Oetzi's injuries from a clinical perspective (2025).
  Scientific Reports.
  DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-27969-9
  -- Source of the 11-13 cm wound track; of the argument that external
     bleeding was probably limited and survival therefore measured in hours
     rather than minutes; and of the doubt cast on the reported head trauma
     and the frostbitten toe.

Pilo, L., Barrett, J.H., Eiken, T. et al. (2020). Crossing the ice: an Iron
  Age to medieval mountain pass at Lendbreen, Norway. Antiquity 94(374).
  DOI: 10.15184/aqy.2020.2
  -- Source of the Lendbreen figures, which cover finds up to 2015: roughly
     800 artefacts, some 150 bones and antlers, more than 100 cairns and the
     foundations of a stone-built shelter; of the traffic from c. AD 300 to
     1500 with a peak around AD 1000; and of the named objects.

Artioli, G. et al. (2017). Long-distance connections in the Copper Age: New
  evidence from the Alpine Iceman's copper axe. PLOS ONE.
  DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0179263
  -- Lead isotope analysis matching the axe metal to ore from southern
     Tuscany, at a time when Alpine copper sources were known and worked.

Voordouw, R. et al. (2023). Brief communication: The Glacier Loss Day as an
  indicator of a record-breaking negative glacier mass balance in 2022. The
  Cryosphere 17.
  DOI: 10.5194/tc-17-3661-2023
  -- Hintereisferner, Oetztal Alps: the record mass balance of 2021/22 and
     the Glacier Loss Day of 23 June 2022 against mid-August in the two
     preceding years. The glacier has been measured continuously since
     1952/53.

Additional figures: the Alpine ice-volume loss of about 5.9 per cent in the
summer of 2022, and the roughly 10 per cent lost by Swiss glaciers across
2022 and 2023 - about as much as went between 1960 and 1990. These are two
different areas and two different periods, and the film says so each time.

Discovery details (19 September 1991, Tisenjoch at 3,210 m, the recovery with
ice axes and a pneumatic drill, the survey placing the find 92.56 m inside
Italy) and the 2001 identification of the arrowhead by Paul Gostner follow
the South Tyrol Museum of Archaeology.

WHAT THE IMAGES ARE NOT

The man shown in shots 14, 16 and 23 is a painting made for this film,
following the phenotype in Wang et al. 2023. The figure in shots 7, 8, 17 and
80 is a generated image of a museum reconstruction - it stands for the
familiar reconstruction the film argues with, and it is not a photograph of
the one in Bolzano.

No image in this film shows human remains. Shot 15 shows a conservation cell
through frosted glass; shot 73 shows a radiograph; the wound is drawn as a
schematic in shots 74, 75 and 87. That is a deliberate limit, not an
oversight.

Every image carries a permanent AI label, and the official EU marking is
shown in full between 0:03 and 0:15.


Corrections are welcome and will be made out loud.
