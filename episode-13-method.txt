THE BURIED WORLD
Episode 13 - The Oldest Plague

How this film was made, and what the fact-check changed

Last updated 4 September 2026


WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

Every episode of this channel is checked before it is recorded, and the
corrections are published. Not because they are flattering - this one is not -
but because a channel that uses machine-generated images has no standing to
ask for trust unless it shows its working.

This episode has an unusual entry in that list: a place where I did not merely
get a number wrong, but had a finding exactly backwards, and said so in the
film with an air of scrupulousness. That correction is number 3.


THE SOURCE SITUATION, AND HOW IT CHANGED MID-PRODUCTION

The script was written against the ABSTRACT of the Nature paper. The full text
was not machine-accessible: nature.com redirects to an authentication wall, and
the bioRxiv preprint answered every request with HTTP 429. The first version of
this file said so and listed four things the film therefore left out.

Then the full text arrived, obtained through a university library, and it
changed four things - three of which confirmed the caution, and one of which
exposed a mistake.

The lesson worth publishing is the shape of that: a carefully documented
abstract-only chain is a stopgap, not a substitute. It kept three errors out of
the film. It let a fourth one in, because an abstract is a SELECTION of two
hundred and fifty words from a twenty-page paper, and what it leaves out is not
thereby absent from the work.


1. FORTY-TWO OR FORTY-SIX INDIVIDUALS - SETTLED BY ARITHMETIC

Nature's own press release says 42 individuals were sampled. The press
coverage says 46. Both say 18 tested positive.

The abstract gives neither figure. It gives a detection rate: 39 per cent.

    18 of 46 = 39.1 per cent   - matches
    18 of 42 = 42.9 per cent   - does not

So 46 is right, and the full text confirmed it: "We generated shotgun-sequenced
ancient DNA from 46 Late Neolithic individuals."

The arithmetic beat the more official source. Two sources that contradict each
other are not always a matter of judgement; where a third figure RECONCILES one
of them and not the other, the sum decides.


2. THE END OF THE SECOND OUTBREAK PHASE - CUT, AND STILL CUT

Sources disagreed: 4,425 or 4,235 cal BP. Without the full text the figure was
unresolvable, so it was dropped under the standing rule that a number the
sources do not carry uniformly gets cut rather than picked.

The full text settles it at 5315-4235 cal BP, and adds a qualification no
secondary source carried: that range has a long tail because the second phase
rests on only TWO dates, and the most likely window is about 5050-4850 cal BP.

The figure stays out of the spoken text anyway - not because it is uncertain
now, but because a range with a caveat attached costs more narration time than
it earns. It is in the source list instead.


3. THE MARMOTS - WHERE I HAD IT BACKWARDS

This is the correction that matters.

The press coverage all named marmots as the likely source. The abstract does
not mention them. I concluded that this was a journalistic addition, and wrote
into the film:

    "Press coverage of the Baikal paper reached for marmots ... It is a
    reasonable idea. It is not, however, a finding of the paper, and it is
    not treated as one here."

The full text devotes half a column to marmots, and the discussion says:

    "These outbreaks were probably the result of zoonotic spillover from wild
    marmot populations at Lake Baikal."

The authors set out the evidence: Marmota sibirica is the principal
contemporary reservoir of plague in the region; hunting them for meat and fur
still causes infections, particularly in young men during skinning and
butchery; and prehistoric marmot procurement at these sites is attested by
marmot teeth used as grave goods - though in Early Neolithic Kitoi graves
rather than the later ones.

How it happened: I read an absence in the abstract as an absence in the paper.
Finding nothing is only a finding once the search was complete, and my search
had covered two hundred and fifty words of a twenty-page article.

It was the expensive kind of error, because the film would not merely have
omitted something. It would have ASSERTED THE OPPOSITE - and done it in a
sentence that congratulated itself on being careful. A viewer who read the
paper would have caught the film preening about its own accuracy.

Act 7 was rewritten completely. It now carries both of the authors' answers -
droplet spread within the group, a continent-wide rodent reservoir across the
distance - and the marmots as what they are: the probable origin.


4. THE NAMES OF THE FOUR CEMETERIES - NOT GUESSED, AND JUST AS WELL

The five best-documented mid-Holocene cemeteries of the Cis-Baikal region are
well known from the literature: Shamanka II, Lokomotiv, Ust'-Ida I,
Khuzhir-Nuge XIV and Kurma XI. Which four the study used was not in the
abstract, so the film named none of them.

The full text: Ust'-Ida I, Shumilikha, Bratskii Kamen and Serovo.

Of my four obvious candidates, exactly ONE was right. Three plausible,
unsupported names would have gone into the film. The episode now names Bratskii
Kamen - where the grave of three girls was found - and the river Angara, on
whose banks all four sites lie. Both are in the paper, word for word.


5. "TWICE IN EIGHT YEARS" - COUNTED INSTEAD OF ESTIMATED

The draft said the picture "has changed twice in eight years". That was an
estimate dressed as a fact. There are four papers: Spyrou 2018, Andrades
Valtuena 2022, Macleod 2026 and Sun 2026 - two of them five weeks apart. The
line now names the years.


6. THE SUPERANTIGEN - SHARPENED, NOT TRIMMED

The draft said a superantigen "provokes the immune system into an enormous,
undirected response". True, and useless: nobody knows how enormous that is.

The line now carries the magnitudes. An ordinary infection activates on the
order of one T-cell in a hundred thousand. A superantigen can switch on one in
five. Yersinia pseudotuberculosis, which carries this locus, is the only
gram-negative bacterium known to produce a superantigen at all.

The limit is spoken in the film as well: nobody has shown that this locus is
what killed those children.


7. THE ypm LOCUS - A GRAPHIC THAT CLAIMED MORE THAN THE PAPER

A drawn graphic in act 8 first showed the later plague lineages with the label
"ypm - absent". The paper does not say that. It says that TEN OPEN READING
FRAMES AROUND the locus are present in the ancestral form and absent in later
ones - a different and narrower statement.

The graphic now reads "ypm + 10 flanking ORFs" against "flanking ORFs absent".
A graphic that simplifies still asserts, and this one had asserted something
the source does not.


WHAT WAS CUT AND NOT REPLACED

- A death toll. The paper reports detection rates, not casualties.
- The end date of the second outbreak phase (see 2).
- The names of three cemeteries (see 4).
- The 2022 Kyrgyzstan finding on the origin of the Black Death of 1347.
  Documented and interesting, but a different event on a different timescale;
  in a film about a lineage 5,500 years old it would have blurred two eras.


A NOTE ON THE PICTURES

Thirty-eight of the 86 shots are drawn rather than generated - the highest
share this channel has run, and the reason is that the subject is data.

The pictures also carry a restriction that is worth stating. The victims of
these outbreaks were mostly children between seven and eleven. There is no
depiction of a sick, dying or dead person in this film, and none of a child.
Where the narration speaks about them, the picture is a grave plan or an age
distribution. A drawn grave number is what survives of those three girls at
Bratskii Kamen; a painted face would have been an invention at the one place
where this film can least afford one.
