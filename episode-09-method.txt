THE BURIED WORLD
Episode 9 - Nothing About Him Happened The Way They Said

How this film was made

Last updated 18 August 2026


THE POINT OF THIS FILE

The film argues that a great deal of what was said about the Iceman has been
withdrawn. A film that makes that argument has no business hiding its own
corrections. Below is every claim that was changed, qualified or cut between
the first draft and the recording, and why.

One of them would have made the film wrong.


THE CORRECTION THAT MATTERED

THE AXE HANDLE FIFTY METRES AWAY IS NOT HIS AXE.

The first draft used it in the act about his equipment, as evidence that his
things had been scattered: "The haft of the axe was found fifty metres to the
south-south-east." That is a real object at a real distance, and the sentence
is still false.

The handle found 50 m south of the find spot dates to roughly five hundred
years AFTER the Iceman (Pilo et al. 2022). It is not his. It is evidence of
the opposite of what the draft claimed: not that his kit was thrown about,
but that the ground around the gully went on collecting material for
centuries after he died. In the finished film it appears in the act about the
gully, doing the work it actually does.

His own axe was found with the body and is complete.

The lesson we took from it: a distance quoted in a summary does not carry the
identification with it. "Found 50 m away" says nothing about what the thing
belongs to. Check the date of the object, not only its position.


WHAT WAS CUT FOR STREAKING SOURCES

- HIS AGE AT DEATH. The published estimates range from about 40 to about 53.
  The film does not give a number.

- HIS HEIGHT AND WEIGHT. Same problem. Not used.

- THE NUMBER OF ARROWS. Counts differ between sources. The film says only
  that most of them were unfinished.

- THE SIZE OF THE ARROWHEAD. Press coverage from 2001 gives 13 mm long; the
  2025 clinical paper gives 17 mm wide. Those are not the same measurement
  and the sources do not agree, so the film gives neither. It gives the wound
  track length, and it says which paper that comes from.

- THE DATE OF THE TATTOO STUDY. One outlet reports 13 March 2024, another
  9 April 2024. The film says only the year.


WHAT WAS CUT AS UNSOUND

- "THE FIRST KNOWN CASE OF LYME DISEASE." This appears in museum timelines
  and in a great deal of coverage. It rests on a Borrelia signature recovered
  from the low-coverage 2012 genome - the same dataset whose steppe ancestry
  turned out to be modern contamination. A pathogen signature in a
  contaminated low-coverage read is not a diagnosis. Cut.


WHAT WAS QUALIFIED RATHER THAN CUT

- THE STEPPE ANCESTRY. It would have been neater to say the 2012 paper
  reported it. Keller et al. 2012 report brown eyes, blood group O, lactose
  intolerance and a raised cardiac risk; the steppe signal comes from later
  work on that same thin dataset. The film says "later work on that same thin
  data", with no year attached to a claim we could not pin to a paper.

- HIS SKIN. The publication says high skin pigmentation - darker than in
  present-day Europeans. The film says exactly that. It does not translate it
  into a colour word and it does not attach an ethnicity to it.

- HOW LONG HE LIVED AFTER THE ARROW. Two published readings disagree: the
  long-standing one has the subclavian artery torn and death in minutes; the
  2025 clinical re-examination argues external bleeding was probably limited
  and survival was more likely measured in hours. The film presents both and
  settles neither, because the literature has not.

- THE HEAD TRAUMA AND THE FROSTBITTEN TOE. Both were treated as established
  for years. The 2025 paper doubts both. The film reports them as doubted.

- THE ICE THICKNESS. Pilo gives "below 20 m, probably closer to 5 m" from an
  1878 survey. The film uses that wording rather than the rounder and more
  striking "five metres".

- THE LENDBREEN FIGURES. Roughly 800 artefacts, some 150 bones and antlers,
  more than 100 cairns: these are the numbers in the 2020 Antiquity paper,
  which covers finds up to 2015. They are not the total today. The film says
  so in the same sentence.

- "MORE ICE MUMMIES ARE WAITING." This is the authors' conclusion from their
  own reappraisal, not a measurement. The film attributes it to them.

- THE GLACIER FIGURES. About 5.9 per cent of total ice volume lost in the
  Alps in the summer of 2022; roughly 10 per cent of Swiss glacier volume
  across 2022 and 2023. Two different areas, two different periods. They are
  never combined into one number, and each is named with its area both in the
  narration and on the chart.


WHAT THE PICTURES DO AND DO NOT CLAIM

Every image in this film was generated or drawn for it. None of them is a
photograph of the Iceman, of the Tisenjoch, of the museum in Bolzano or of
any real object.

Two of them needed a rule of their own, because the film is about a
reconstruction being wrong:

- The museum figure (shots 7, 8, 17, 80) is deliberately the familiar one -
  pale, light-eyed, full-haired, bearded. That is the reconstruction the film
  argues with, so it has to look like it.

- The man himself (shots 14, 16, 23) is painted to the genome instead: dark
  skin, dark eyes, male-pattern baldness. These two must never be confused,
  and during production they nearly were - the model has learned the museum
  figure far better than it has learned the paper.

No image shows human remains. The body appears only as a shape behind frosted
glass, as a radiograph, and as an empty hollow. The arrow wound is drawn as a
schematic line with a measured length; the outline around it is not an
anatomical drawing, and the picture says so.

The two maps that draw a line between regions - ancestry and copper - both
carry a caption saying the line shows origin and not a route anybody
travelled.
