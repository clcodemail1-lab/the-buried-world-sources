THE BURIED WORLD
Dispatch 14 - These Four Graves Were Closed. Somebody Came Back Anyway
How this film was made, and what the fact check changed


WHY THESE FOUR PAPERS

They share a shape rather than a subject. In each of the four, a grave was
closed and then opened again - and the gap between those two events grows
across the film, from a few years at Shanidar to more than fifteen hundred in
Dorset. That is the whole claim. It is not a claim about how common the
practice was, and the film says so where it would otherwise be implied.


THE FRAME BROKE ONCE, AND THE FOURTH CASE WAS REPLACED

The episode was first built on a different frame: that the people sharing a
grave had not necessarily arrived together. Three of the four cases fitted it.
The fourth was a study of 142 genomes from three early Christian churchyards in
Sweden, and reading its discussion in full killed the frame: it states that
almost all the multiple burials examined represent primary, SIMULTANEOUS
interments of people who likely died at or around the same time. They did
arrive together; they were simply not related.

Keeping that case would have required the viewer to overhear the difference.
Instead the frame was narrowed to temporal reuse, and the Swedish study was
replaced by the southwestern England genomes, where the reuse is the finding.
The Swedish paper is a good episode on its own and will probably become one.


WHAT THE FULL TEXTS CHANGED

Reading the four papers in full - rather than their abstracts or the press
coverage - changed six things:

1. SHANIDAR IS NOT A MURDER. The scapula perforation is perimortem and shows no
   bone regrowth, so the injury appears to have been fatal. But the paper states
   that whether it resulted from a hunting accident or from interpersonal
   violence cannot be known, and that violence between individuals or groups
   cannot be demonstrated. Headlines announcing the world's first murder are not
   a reading of this paper. The film says so.

2. THE FLOWER BURIAL IS NOT REVIVED. The paper treats that interpretation as
   having failed further scrutiny. It appears in the film for one sentence, in
   order to be dropped.

3. THE THEBAN STACK HAS SETTLED. The paper cautions that the present vertical
   positions of the bodies should not be read as their original depositional
   heights: coffins, wrappings and soft tissue collapse and sediment runs in.
   The stacking is real; the heights are not a measurement of it.

4. "THREE CENTURIES" WAS WRONG. That figure came from a press release. The
   published phase sequence runs from the twenty-fifth dynasty through the
   Persian period into Ptolemaic times, which is roughly four and a half
   centuries. The film says "call it four and a half centuries" and the sources
   file says the span is derived rather than quoted.

5. WESTMINSTER IS STRONGER THAN REPORTED. No press account carried the second
   reuse: when Henry III began rebuilding the abbey in the 1240s, this burial
   was among those exhumed and reinterred, about 1258, in the new sacristy
   courtyard. The coffin was therefore reused once and moved once more.
   The attribution to Abbot Aelfwig is stated by the author as an argument, not
   a finding, and the film keeps that wording.

6. THE DORSET INTERPRETATION IS HEDGED THREE TIMES. The authors write that the
   later burials MAY reflect an attempt to anchor newcomers in an older
   landscape, that this POTENTIALLY invokes imagined ancestral affiliations, and
   that the interpretation SHOULD REMAIN CAUTIOUS. All three hedges are in the
   film.


TWO ERRORS IN OUR OWN WORK, FOUND WHILE WRITING THE SOURCE LIST

The Shanidar article number was written as 28714. It is 30042. The number was
not copied from anywhere - it was simply wrong, and it would have pointed
readers at a different paper.

The source list also stated that all four publications are open access. Three
are, under CC BY 4.0. The Journal of the British Archaeological Association
article is not; it was read through institutional access. Both errors were
caught by resolving the DOIs against the Crossref record rather than by
re-reading the draft.

Resolving the DOIs also turned up something useful: the Theban tomb has a
number, TT 209, which no press account mentioned.


WHAT THE FILM SHOWS, AND WHAT IT REFUSES TO SHOW

There are no human remains anywhere in this film. In all four cases the finding
is at the level of the body, and in all four it is shown as a drawn excavation
plan or section - the form archaeology itself works in - rather than as a
picture of the dead. That decision is why 23 of the 57 shots are diagrams,
a higher share than in any previous episode of this channel.

The decision also shaped the writing of the image prompts. Three generated
shots were rejected and remade, and all three failed the same way: the motif
was described correctly and its ARRANGEMENT was left open, so the model filled
the gap with the likeliest reading. Linen resting on boards became a shrouded
body. An open expanse of chalk rubble became a farm track. An "excavated
section" became a pit instead of a section face. Describing positively is not
enough when the forbidden thing is the obvious completion.


LENGTH

The first voiced version ran 8:06,47 of speech against an estimate of 8:28,1 -
the channel's character-rate formula was 21,6 seconds high, more than four
times its documented spread. Measuring the real rate of all fourteen dispatches
showed why: the constant has been drifting upward for fourteen productions and
the value in the playbook describes the state of late August. It has been
corrected.

The episode was then lengthened, because 8:15,5 would have left only 14,5
seconds above this channel's mid-roll threshold and would have been the
shortest dispatch in more than a year. The three blocks added are the two
sampling cautions from the Dorset paper and the answer to the obvious question
- was this normal? - which the paper answers with five further sites and the
word "unclear".


Corrections are welcome and will be made out loud.
