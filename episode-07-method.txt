THE BURIED WORLD
Episode 7 - They Emptied The Capital Before It Burned

How this film was made
Last updated 14 August 2026


WHY THIS PAGE EXISTS
====================

Every episode of this channel is fact-checked before it is recorded, and the
corrections are published rather than quietly absorbed. A film that asks you to
weigh competing explanations owes you its own workings - including the places
where the first draft was wrong.

Sixteen claims were checked for this episode. Four were changed. Two of those
four would have damaged the film, and both sat in the closing minutes.


THE FOUR CORRECTIONS
====================

1. THE DIRECTION OF THE LAST UGARIT LETTERS WAS WRONG

   The first draft had the king of Ugarit writing to Cyprus for help and
   receiving a question in return. It is the other way round. Alashiya writes
   first, warning that enemy ships have been sighted, and asks where Ugarit's
   own forces are. Ammurapi answers: his troops are in the land of Hatti, his
   ships are in the land of Lukka.

   The correction makes the point sharper rather than weaker, which is exactly
   why it was worth catching. The harbour was not open because Ugarit was
   careless. It was open because the fleet was away doing its duty to an
   overlord who could no longer hold his own capital.

   Tablet numbers are deliberately absent from the film. The literature assigns
   RS 18.147 and RS 20.238 inconsistently, and a number stated with false
   confidence is worse than no number.


2. THE TABLET IN THE KILN IS NOT IN THIS FILM - AND NEITHER IS THE DEBUNKING

   There is a well-travelled story that the last tablet at Ugarit was still
   baking in a kiln when the city fell. The draft used the debunking of it as
   the closing line: a small lesson about stories being tidier than events.

   What is actually attested is that the claim was "once thought". No clean
   published revision could be found. That leaves the correction no better
   supported than the story it corrects - so both were cut, and the film ends
   on the letter instead.

   This is the harder kind of cut. Refuting a myth feels like rigour, and it
   reads well. But an unsourced refutation is still an unsourced claim.


3. SIX PER CENT WAS NOT WHAT THE STUDY SAID

   The tree-ring study (Manning et al., Nature, 2023) places the years 1198,
   1197 and 1196 BC within the driest 6.25 per cent of a seven-century record.
   The draft rounded that to "six per cent", which sounds like nothing and is
   in fact a stronger claim than the source supports. The film says six and a
   quarter.

   The same study describes those years as COINCIDENT with the Hittite
   collapse, not as its cause. The script was held to that wording throughout:
   the film says the dry years "fall in the same window", never that they
   brought anything down.


4. FOUR FIGURES WERE CUT BECAUSE THE SOURCES DISAGREED

   - The tonnage of the Hittite grain demand. Conversions of the recorded
     quantity differ by roughly a factor of two. The film quotes only the line
     that needs no conversion: "it is a matter of life and death."
   - The origin of the tin. Isotope studies point in more than one direction
     and the question is live. The film says only that it came from far
     outside anybody's borders, which is what everyone agrees on and is also
     the part that matters for the argument.
   - The length of the illiterate period in Greece. Sources say 300 years,
     400 years, and things in between. The film says "centuries".
   - Population decline in Greece. The estimates are too far apart to average
     honestly, so the film describes it and gives no percentage.


ON THE DATE IN THE TITLE OF EVERY OTHER FILM ABOUT THIS
=======================================================

1177 BC is year 8 of Ramesses III, converted into an Egyptian chronology that
is itself uncertain by a few years. The author who made the date famous chose
it as a marker and says so. This film treats it as a marker and not as a
finding, and says on screen that the collapse has about half a century rather
than a year.


ON THE IMAGES
=============

Every photographic-looking image is an AI-generated reconstruction and carries
the EU marking and the words AI RECONSTRUCTION on screen throughout. Nothing
reproduces a specific artefact, monument or excavation.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

  - The Egyptian reliefs are inventions in the manner of the genre, not copies
    of the wall at Medinet Habu. That wall is a protected photographic subject
    and is not reproduced here.
  - Anything showing people doing something - a workshop, a family on a road,
    a granary just after it was emptied - is marked as a painting, because
    nobody photographed it and nobody can.

The twelve diagrams are drawn, not generated, because image models place
lettering unreliably and an invented figure on a map is still an invented
figure. Where the diagrams draw arrows between the four explanations, those
arrows are the film's argument and not a finding: you can reject them without
rejecting a single measurement underneath.


ON WHAT THE FILM DOES NOT SHOW
==============================

There is no battle in this film, and no burning city with people in it. That
is a standing rule of the channel, and here it happens to agree with the
argument: the evidence for what ended the Bronze Age is mostly evidence of
absence - emptied storerooms, an abandoned capital, a harbour with nothing in
it, an archive that stops. Those are the images the film uses.
